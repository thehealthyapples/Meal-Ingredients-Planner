# Top-20 food-app overlap review and THA capability gaps

**Checked:** 19 November 2026 — UK-focused, point-in-time review
**Audience:** THA product and development work only; do not publish this strategy section to the public comparison page.

**Risk:** Green — evidence synthesis and internal recommendations only. No application behavior was changed.

## Decision in brief

The closest 20-app set is selected below from the 40-app candidate universe. The set favors breadth across the household food journey over raw download or revenue rank. Popularity was used as a secondary selection signal; chart rankings are store-specific, fluctuate, and do not establish active users. Mealime is excluded because its announced 21 October 2026 shutdown date has passed; it remains historical evidence only.

The most repeated competitor workflow is **recipes or meal choices → a weekly plan → a shopping list**. Dietary filtering and serving adjustment are also frequent. These are areas where THA already has substantial capability. THA's clearer functional gaps are:

1. **Interactive guided cooking** — the audit found recipe instructions but no dedicated mode with step progression and timers.
2. **More reliable social/video recipe capture** — THA accepts links and text and can scan recipe images, but some social posts still require manual pasted text. Several new UK charting competitors explicitly capture TikTok/Instagram or recipe-video posts.
3. **Connect “Add to Freezer” to batch cooking** — THA has a freezer action, but the current audit did not establish an end-to-end flow joining batch portions, planned servings, shopping quantities and thaw reminders.
4. **Clear weekly nutrition from food actually logged** — THA can show food-level nutrition and explore nutrients, but it does not yet substantiate complete week totals for consumed nutrition or clearly distinguish planned from eaten. Specialist diary apps are stronger here, although this capability is less common across general meal-planning apps.
5. **Make pantry matches more useful** — THA can find Cookbook meals that use pantry foods, but does not yet have a full use-it-up search or cooking-linked stock adjustment.

THA's family-first model, individual eater restrictions, reviewed adaptations, positive nutrition additions, paper recipe/plan scanning, household food diary (food, weight, mood and energy), and combined meal-to-shopping journey are meaningful strengths. Do not dilute those strengths to chase raw feature counts.

## How the 20 were selected

The comparison taxonomy already contains 30 tasks. For selection across 40 apps, those tasks were grouped into ten workflow families:

1. Shared household planning and distinct eater needs.
2. Food restrictions and meal adaptation.
3. Creating, saving and importing recipes.
4. Social/video and paper/image capture.
5. Weekly meal planning, reuse and plan capture.
6. Shopping lists, budget visibility and retailer handoff.
7. Recipe nutrition and helpful additions.
8. Food diary, photo logging, weight, mood and weekly intake.
9. Packaged-food scanning and explanation.
10. Pantry/freezer/batch cooking and guided preparation.

Selection used breadth across these workflow families, direct use by the same household preparing meals, and the evidence already available in the 30-task matrix and dated audits. The four personal-nutrition comparators were selected where they connect nutrition goals or a diary to meal planning; a scanner-only app was kept as a challenge benchmark outside the top 20. Popularity is a secondary check, not a substitute for overlap. Where an official source is silent, this review records **not established** rather than claiming the app does not have that feature.

This is a source review of official product/help materials and UK store listings, not hands-on testing of every subscription tier or regional configuration. The resulting shortlist is a practical product benchmark, not a statistically representative “top 20 by users” claim.

## Recommended THA enhancement order

This is a product recommendation, not implementation approval. The first change should be decided and scoped as one Amber decision at a time.

### P1 — Add an optional step-focus view to existing recipes

**Why first:** it is a clear THA gap against several broad recipe/meal-planning apps, while the existing recipe instructions are already the source. A restrained cook mode could reduce missed steps during a family meal without creating another recipe system.
**Possible scope:** an in-place, calm reading view that shows one existing instruction at a time, a simple “step N of M” position, previous/next controls and an easy return to the full recipe. Keep the current step in tab-session state so leaving and returning can resume; clear that view state on explicit exit or session close. Do not add timers or countdowns: the Experience Language says nothing should count down or hurry the person. Do not add tracking, streaks, completion records or automatic diary writes. If a recipe has no stored instructions, keep the current unavailable state and do not invent steps.  
**Risk:** 🟡 Amber — new interactive cooking workflow.
**Architecture/data:** `meals.instructions` remains the one recipe-step owner; the mode is a view over `client/src/pages/meal-detail-page.tsx`. No new entity, API, database record, recipe copy or diary write. The proposed resume marker is local to the browser tab session and contains only meal id and current step; it is removed on explicit exit/session close. Recipe-acquisition lane and attribution rules remain in force.  
**Trust:** display only stored recipe instructions; do not infer missing steps or timing. Preserve the full instruction list and an obvious exit. A user can leave at any time without marking the recipe cooked.  
**Definition of done / manual acceptance:** (1) open a recipe that has instructions and start the focus view; (2) confirm it shows the first step and correct total; (3) move forward and back without losing text or changing the recipe; (4) leave the view and return to the full instructions; (5) navigate away and back within the same tab session and confirm the selected step resumes; (6) explicitly exit and confirm the resume marker clears; (7) confirm a recipe without stored instructions cannot enter the mode; (8) confirm no countdown, recipe mutation, pantry change or diary entry occurs.  
**Data impact:** reads existing meal instructions and id; writes a short-lived browser-tab view marker only; no server/database writes, schema change, backfill or change to existing meaning.  
**Rollback:** remove the view and tab-session marker logic; return to the existing recipe instruction list. No household data cleanup is needed.  
**Approval status:** not yet specifically approved. The owner's broad request to enhance after comparison does not define this behavior's exact scope.

### P2 — Improve social and video recipe import reliability

**Why:** import is a visible, repeated competitor workflow and directly matches THA's current partial capability and the user's recipe-import priorities.
**Possible scope:** assess supported share URLs, fallback to pasted caption/text, parse preview and explicit correction before the existing recipe save path. Preserve recipe-acquisition rules and do not create another assistant/store.
**Risk:** 🟡 Amber; may become 🔴 if it adds AI interpretation, new provider integration, or changes recipe acquisition/licensing.

### P3 — Connect existing “Add to Freezer” with batch cooking

**Why:** Plan to Eat and Real Plans document portions and thaw reminders; THA already has freezer behavior but the end-to-end connection is not substantiated.
**Possible scope:** investigate existing freezer and planner owners, then propose a one-way reviewed transition from planned batch portions to freezer records and reminders. Avoid duplicate inventory or automatically reducing pantry truth.
**Risk:** 🟡 Amber for workflow state; potentially 🔴 if persistence/schema changes are needed.

### P4 — Add transparent weekly nutrition for food actually logged

**Why:** THA's audit identified weekly consumed-nutrient totals as incomplete. Nutrition trackers demonstrate user demand, while THA can distinguish itself by focusing on useful additions rather than restrictive scoring.
**Possible scope:** existing diary entries only; show logged-day coverage, nutrient totals/averages, missing days, and clear separation from planned meals. Keep suggestions optional and reuse existing nutrition rules.
**Risk:** 🔴 Red if AI reasoning, health interpretation, persistence or new targets are introduced; otherwise at least 🟡 Amber for user-facing nutrition logic.

### P5 — Strengthen pantry use-it-up matching and stock truth

**Why:** pantry-led recommendations are a recognized differentiator in Samsung Food, Cooklist and Real Plans. THA can improve match relevance without assuming stock has changed.
**Possible scope:** improve search and review using the existing Larder and Cookbook owners; require confirmation before any stock update.
**Risk:** 🟡 Amber; visual ingredient recognition would be a separate, higher-risk decision.

### P6 — Consider meal-photo-to-diary draft

**Why:** a high-reach diary competitor offers photo logging, but this is not one of the most prevalent features across the whole planning set.
**Possible scope:** correction-first draft to existing diary owner only.
**Risk:** 🔴 Red because it interprets food images and nutrition estimates.
