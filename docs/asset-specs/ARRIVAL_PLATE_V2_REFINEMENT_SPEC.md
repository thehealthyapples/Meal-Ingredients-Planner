## Canonical prompt

Refine this exact photorealistic farmhouse-larder interior, keeping the camera, composition, one-point front-on perspective, eye height, straight verticals, lime-plaster walls, warm-oak materials, stone worktop, flagged stone floor, the right-hand window and its soft daylight arriving from the right (all cast shadows down and to the left), the central open oak shelving and its proportions, the lower oak cupboards and cup-handle drawers, and the empty woven-willow baskets on the right — all unchanged. Apply only these changes:

1. LEFT WALL — replace the tall plain oak cupboard on the left with one **integrated American-style fridge-freezer built into the surrounding cabinetry**: a wide two-door (French-door) refrigerator above and a lower freezer drawer section beneath, full height from floor to worktop line and flush within a hand-built oak surround. Its doors are finished in **warm brushed antique metal or oak furniture panels with slim aged-brass bar handles**, so it reads as premium handcrafted fitted cabinetry — part of the room's joinery, never a commercial stainless-steel kitchen appliance. No control panel, no water dispenser, no chrome, no rubber gasket, no branding. It should sit quietly as furniture.

2. SHELVING — mount a **slim hand-engraved timber name-plate along the front edge of each open oak shelf**, in the manner of an old apothecary or estate pantry: a narrow oak or painted-timber strip fixed to the shelf's leading edge, subtly hand-lettered, softly worn, catching the light. The plates are permanent joinery. (Legible category wording is added later as a crisp runtime layer; here the plates are the handcrafted timber form.)

3. PERMANENT DRESSING — add a few **permanent, non-food handcrafted objects** that belong to the room forever, placed naturally on the stone worktop and floor: a thick **wooden chopping board** leaning at the back of the worktop, a **ceramic mixing bowl**, a set of **vintage kitchen scales**, a small **wooden stool** on the floor, and a **folded linen towel** over the worktop edge. These are architectural dressing only — calm, timeless, handcrafted. **Nothing edible, no produce, no jars, tins or bottles, nothing that could be picked up as a provision.**

Everything else in the photograph stays exactly as it is. Editorial architectural photography, medium-format character, natural light, sharp throughout, realistic — not CGI, illustrated, painterly or stylised. The result must feel like standing inside a real handcrafted pantry.

## Negative constraints

No commercial stainless-steel fridge, no white or glass-fronted appliance, no chrome, no control panel, no water/ice dispenser, no rubber door gasket, no appliance branding or logo. No jars, tins, bottles, packets, food, produce, fruit, vegetables, potted herbs, plants, flowers on the shelves or surfaces. No people, animals. No change of camera, perspective, composition, light direction, window position, shelf proportions, worktop or baskets. No wide-angle distortion, leaning verticals, tilted horizon, second light source, down-right shadows. No text, watermark, logo, UI, signage, price labels. No HDR, sepia, vignette, lens flare, painterly, illustration, clip-art or CGI.

## Candidate strategy

Refine from the v1 base. Permitted variance: the exact finish of the fridge-freezer surround (brushed antique metal vs oak panel), the precise dressing object placement, plaster/oak microtexture, small exposure differences. Fixed and non-negotiable: same camera/composition/light as v1; the LEFT integrated American fridge-freezer reading as handcrafted cabinetry (never commercial steel); slim engraved timber name-plates on each shelf front edge; a few permanent non-food dressing objects; the complete absence of any provision or edible item; warm oak + lime plaster at North Star editorial quality.
