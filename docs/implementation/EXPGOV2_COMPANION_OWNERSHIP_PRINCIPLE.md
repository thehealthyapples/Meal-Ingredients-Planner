## 1. What this is

This promotes one sentence into permanent governing architecture:

> ## The Rooms Observe.
> ## The Companion Understands.
> ## The Household Decides.

It is now **§ 7.4** of the Experience Constitution, stated as three constitutional principles — **GEA21**, **GEA22**, **GEA23**.

It creates **no** route, capability, entity, schema, permission, token, component, string or business logic. **No file under `client/` or `server/` was modified.** No behaviour changed. No UI changed. No canonical ownership moved. Nothing a household can see is different today than it was yesterday.

What changed is that a principle three programmes had already been executing now exists somewhere a fourth programme is obliged to read.

## 2. The finding

The sentence was already governing THA. It was governing nothing that could be cited.

| Programme | What it did on this principle | Where the principle lived |
|---|---|---|
| `UX3` (2026-07-19) | Retired four coaching panels from four rooms | A commit message (`7a5b48cf`) and the Adoption Register |
| `PRESENCE1` (2026-07-20) | Removed **nineteen judgement surfaces** from **eight rooms** | An implementation report |
| `PRESENCE2` (2026-07-20) | Gave the one voice its presence, and removed the last two scoring surfaces — *the Companion's own* | An implementation report, quoting it as *"the governing principle, applied literally"* |

Three programmes, twenty-five surfaces retired, and **not one governing document owned the rule they were retired under.**

This is `GEA20`'s upward-flow defect in its purest available form. The Constitution's own § 19.3 states the obligation directly — *a new principle enters here, once* — and cites the reason THA has written down before (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_COMPLETION_ARCHITECTURE.md` § 9.3): **a finding that stays inside the document that found it will be rediscovered from scratch by whoever asks next.** `GEA8` exists precisely because `HOUSE_ACT2` and `UX3` had reached opposite conclusions with both rationales on record and neither one law. Left where it was, this sentence was on the same path: the next proposal to put an encouraging line in a room would have had to re-argue the boundary from first principles, or — far more likely — would not have noticed there was a boundary.

### 2.1 The half the canon never had

`GEA8` and `GEA9` already covered two of the three parties: *rooms report, the Companion advises*. Two speakers.

The canon said **nothing about the household as an owner of anything.**

That is the gap this amendment closes, and it is not decorative. A change can satisfy every existing rule about voice — no coaching in a room, every adaptive sentence composed, one presence — and still quietly decide something on a family's behalf: by pre-selecting a default, by narrowing the options until one remains, by presenting a recommendation the household has no way to decline. There was no principle to fail that change against. **GEA23** is that principle.

### 2.2 The condition the monopoly was granted under, written next to the monopoly

`PRESENCE1` ended on an admission that this programme takes seriously:

> The house has been made quieter on the promise that the one voice will speak.

`PRESENCE2` then found what the one voice was actually saying: *"Target hit — a 7-day **elite** streak."* **The last two scoring surfaces in The Healthy Apples were the Companion's own.**

That is the structural risk of a sole owner, and it was foreseeable: THA gave one voice a monopoly on interpretation and wrote the monopoly down without writing down the standard it was granted under. **§ 7.4.1 puts the five conditions beside the ownership** — ground every statement in evidence · never invent an observation · prefer silence over weak guidance · explain the reasoning when asked · respect household agency — because *a sole voice that is not held to these is worse than the many voices it replaced, **because it is trusted more***.

None of the five is new law, and none is stated here as new law. Each cites its owner (§ 4 below).

## 3. What the principle says

### Rooms

| **Rooms own** | **Rooms must never own** |
|---|---|
| facts | coaching |
| household state | encouragement |
| workflow | congratulations |
| controls | persuasion |
| truthful reporting | behavioural interpretation |
| | emotional judgement |

### The Companion

| **The Companion owns** | **The Companion must always** |
|---|---|
| interpretation | ground every statement in evidence |
| observations | never invent an observation |
| coaching | prefer silence over weak guidance |
| encouragement | explain its reasoning when asked |
| reassurance | respect household agency |
| explanation | |
| recommendations | |

### The household

| **The household always owns** |
|---|
| decisions |
| priorities |
| values |
| pace of change |

**The Companion never takes a decision away from the household** — not by making it, not by pre-making it and presenting the result as a default, and not by narrowing the options until only one remains.

### 3.1 The one genuinely new piece of reasoning

The principle says *the rooms observe*, and the Companion owns *observations*. Read carelessly, that is a contradiction, and it is exactly the kind of ambiguity a future programme would resolve in whichever direction was convenient.

**§ 7.4.2 resolves it permanently:**

- **A room observes the way a window observes.** It faces the household's life and shows it accurately. It has no view about what it is showing.
- **The Companion observes the way a person observes.** It notices something across time, understands what it means, and says so.

So: **the rooms observe without speaking about what they see, and the Companion is the only party that may say what it noticed.**

> *"Lentils appeared in eleven meals this month"* — a fact of the household's data. **A room.**
> *"Lentils have quietly become a habit here"* — a noticing. **The Companion.**

The room may hold the fact. Only the Companion may make anything of it. This is the distinction `PRESENCE1` § 8 decision 2 asked the owner to rule on and left open; it is now ruled, in the direction `PRESENCE1` recommended (option **a** — the Companion, and only the Companion).

## 4. Why this is a constitutional principle and not an implementation guideline

The distinction is not emphasis. It is enforceability.

A guideline describes how work is usually done, and is negotiable by anyone with a reasonable case. A constitutional principle names **who owns a kind of statement** — so a proposal that puts the wrong owner behind a sentence is wrong *structurally*, before anyone asks whether the sentence is good, well-written, well-placed or well-intentioned.

That distinction is what the evidence demands. Nearly every one of the twenty-five surfaces `UX3`, `PRESENCE1` and `PRESENCE2` retired was **individually defensible**. The encouraging line in the diary was kind. The pantry's "did you know" was accurate. The 7-day streak notice was genuinely trying to be nice to a family. None of them was defensible as a question of *ownership*, and ownership is the only question this principle asks.

**It restates no rule.** Under the Constitution's own yield clause (§ 2.4), the five Companion conditions are collected rather than authored, and each cites its owner:

| Condition | Owner cited |
|---|---|
| Ground every statement in evidence | `ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES.md` Principle 6; composed through `THA_CONTEXT_COMPOSITION_ENGINE_ARCHITECTURE.md`, which owns every byte the model reads |
| Never invent an observation | `ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES.md` Principle 6 (non-fabrication) |
| Prefer silence over weak guidance | **GEA15** — silence is the default, speech the exception |
| Explain the reasoning when asked | `COMP_AUTH1_COMPANION_AUTHORITY_MODEL.md` |
| Respect household agency | `THA_EXPERIENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md` § 11 — *it suggests, the person decides* |

## 5. Architecture Compliance — the four owners

The programme was asked to confirm exactly one owner for each of four concerns. Confirmed, and now checkable:

| Concern | **Single owner** | Fixed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Coaching** | The Companion | **GEA8** (existing), reaffirmed by **GEA21**/**GEA22** |
| **Interpretation** | The Companion | **GEA22** |
| **Observation** | The Companion — for *saying what was noticed*. A room owns the underlying **fact** and may show it, never interpret it. | **GEA21**/**GEA22**, disambiguated by § 7.4.2 |

No concern has two owners. No concern has none. The one boundary that was previously ambiguous — *observation* — is the one § 7.4.2 exists to settle, and it is settled in a form that produces a different answer for two sentences carrying the same fact.

## 7. How future work is tested against this

The principle is not advisory, and it is not evaluated at review. It is two questions in the **Experience Constitution Check** (§ 18.2), answered **before design begins**:

```
✓ OWNERSHIP (§ 7.4, GEA21/GEA22) — THE ROOMS OBSERVE · THE COMPANION
    UNDERSTANDS · THE HOUSEHOLD DECIDES. Every statement this change adds is
    named as one of: a fact a room owns, or an interpretation the Companion
    owns. Nothing in a room congratulates, persuades, or judges the household.
    Anything the Companion says is grounded in evidence, invents no
    observation, and stays silent rather than guess.

✓ AGENCY (§ 7.4, GEA23) — This takes no decision, priority, value, or pace of
    change away from the household. Nothing here decides on their behalf and
    presents the result as settled, and nothing narrows the options until only
    one remains.
```

**If either check fails: STOP, explain why, do not continue.**

The test in one line, for a reviewer with no time: **name the owner of every sentence on the screen.** If a sentence in a room cannot be named as a fact the room owns, it belongs to the Companion. If a sentence from the Companion cannot be traced to evidence, it should not have been said. If the household cannot decline it, it was not a recommendation.
