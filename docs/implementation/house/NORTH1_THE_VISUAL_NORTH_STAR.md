# NORTH1 — The Visual North Star

## Evaluating the kitchen reference as a product designer

**Status:** DESIGN REPORT — an evaluation and a recommendation. **Not** governing architecture, **not** a specification, **not** implementation. It creates no rule and no second owner (Experience Blueprint § 18; Architecture Principle 2). It recommends an adoption; it does not make one.
**Classification:** Experience / UX design (implementation).
**Date:** 2026-07-16 (NORTH1)
**Subject:** `attached_assets/North Star/kitchen concept.png` · `kitchen concept 1.png`
**Evaluated against:** [`THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md`](../../architecture/THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md) · [`THA_ORCHARD_HOUSE_DESIGN_BLUEPRINT.md`](../../architecture/THA_ORCHARD_HOUSE_DESIGN_BLUEPRINT.md) · [`THA_ORCHARD_LIVING_BOOK.md`](../../architecture/THA_ORCHARD_LIVING_BOOK.md) · [`THA_KEPT_ROOM_TRANSLATION.md`](../../architecture/THA_KEPT_ROOM_TRANSLATION.md) · [`DESIGN1_HOME_VISUAL_DESIGN.md`](./DESIGN1_HOME_VISUAL_DESIGN.md) (the previous Home concept) · [`ORCHARD3_VISUAL_CONCEPT_EXPLORATION.md`](./ORCHARD3_VISUAL_CONCEPT_EXPLORATION.md) (the Kept Room lineage)

## 1. THE VERDICT, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The image is the most valuable artefact the visual programme has produced, and it must never be built. It is the first time the canon's governing sentence — *a warm, lived-in home where someone has already thought about dinner* — exists as something a person can stand in rather than read about. It settles ORCHARD3's bet: the Kept Room is real, and it is warm without being twee. But it is a **concept render**, and it earns its warmth by exactly the means the canon forbids — a drawn kitchen, painted props, an aphorism on the wall. Its power and its danger are the same property: it is so persuasive that a team will copy the picture instead of extracting the principle, and copying it produces *the theme park* (Experience Blueprint § 16) — the single most-forbidden thing in the entire document set.

> **The image is a photograph of the destination, not a map of it.** Adopt it as the **feeling to design toward**; never as the picture to draw. That distinction is not a caveat on this report — it *is* this report.

---

## 2. WHY THIS IS STRONGER THAN THE PREVIOUS HOME CONCEPT

DESIGN1 is the previous concept: eleven tonal, deliberately abstract plates of Home drawn as the Kept Room. It is correct and it is unfelt. Its own framing concedes the problem — *"the plates are tonal design-intent drawings; they show composition, proportion, hierarchy and relationship, never the binding appearance"* (DESIGN1 § Fidelity). You cannot tell, from DESIGN1, whether the house is *warm*. You can only tell that it is *supposed* to be.

The image is stronger in five specific ways, each of which is a thing DESIGN1 asserted and this proves:

1. **It proves warmth is achievable without decoration.** THA's *"single greatest visual risk is cold, clinical, lifeless calm"* (ORCHARD3 § 1.2; the entire reason EXPLANG1B exists). Every abstract plate in the programme leaves that risk unresolved, because tonal drawings cannot fail at warmth — they have no temperature. This image takes the risk and beats it. The room is unmistakably calm *and* unmistakably alive.

2. **It proves Home's signature inversion — the orchard largest in area, quietest in attention.** DESIGN1 § 5 claims this inversion as Home's defining move. It is very hard to believe on paper and very easy to get wrong. Here it simply works: the orchard occupies most of the frame and the eye still goes first to the greeting, then to the glance card. That is the hardest claim in the canon, demonstrated.

3. **It proves hierarchy by light rather than by colour.** There is almost no brand colour in the frame. Emphasis is carried entirely by luminance and material. TRANSLATION1 § 5.2 (*light before colour*) stops being an aspiration and becomes an observation.

4. **It proves the three grounds separate cleanly.** World behind (orchard), room in the middle (the counter), foreground (the panels). You can point at each one. Experience Blueprint § 8.1 — *"the middle ground is the whole trick of many places"* — is legible for the first time.

5. **It discovers something no document had: the aperture.** This is the genuine intellectual contribution, and § 4.2 below treats it as the report's central find. The canon says the orchard is *"experienced through windows"* (§ 6.2) and *"never wallpaper"* (§ 6.1), and never explains what makes the difference. The image answers it: **the solid wall between the openings.** The orchard reads as a *view* precisely because plaster interrupts it. That is not in any of the five documents, and it changes what should be built.

**But it is not stronger in every way, and this matters:** DESIGN1 gets two things right that the image gets wrong. DESIGN1 has *one door* — a single primary action re-aimed by relevance (§ 0.1, § 3). The image has no door at all; it has a menu (§ 5.4). And DESIGN1 holds the shell constant; the image reinvents it (§ 5.3). **The image is a better picture of the room and a worse piece of product design.** The right synthesis takes the room from the image and the discipline from DESIGN1 — not the image wholesale.

## 3. WHICH PRINCIPLES IT SUCCESSFULLY EXPRESSES

Scored honestly against the canon's own checks.

| Principle | Owner | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A modern home in an ancient orchard** | EXPBLUE § 1.4 | **Expressed completely.** The contrast is exactly right — the intelligence is clean and current, the orchard mature and permanent. Neither half apologises for the other. |
| **One sun, one direction, one hour** | EXPBLUE § 7 | **Expressed.** Every shadow agrees. It is unarguably morning, and it is unarguably the *same* morning in both plates. |
| **The orchard is the setting, never the decoration** | EXPBLUE § 6, § 16 | **Expressed in the Home plate** — and *violated in the Cookbook plate* (§ 5.5). The Home plate is the strongest statement of this law anywhere in the programme. |
| **Warmth carried by light and material, never ornament** | OHDB § 12 | **Half expressed.** The light and material genuinely carry it. But the props also carry it, and they are not allowed to (§ 5.2). Remove the apples and the mug: does the room stay warm? Mostly yes — which is the proof that the *light* was doing the real work, and the props were riding along. |
| **Air is a material** | EXPBLUE § 8.2 | **Expressed.** The counter is uncluttered; the width became view and air, not widgets (HOUSE1 § 19.3). |
| **The orchard never carries text** | EXPBLUE § 6.1 | **Expressed at Home** — every glyph sits on a solid panel. **Broken at Cookbook**, where the recipe body sits on a rendered page (§ 5.5). |
| **Type on solid ground; legibility never traded for atmosphere** | EXPBLUE § 6.1 | **Broken.** The panels are translucent over photography — legibility is contingent on the backplate, which is precisely the trade the law forbids (§ 5.6). |
| **Composed emptiness, never bare emptiness** | OHDB § 4 | **Cannot be assessed, and that is a finding.** The image shows a good day. The canon's hardest state is the quiet one (DESIGN1 § 12), and a rendered kitchen *always* looks lived-in — even when the household has done nothing. The render cannot fail at the state the design most needs to survive. |
| **Decorated only by the household's real life** | EXPBLUE § 12 | **Broken.** The room is dressed, not lived in (§ 5.2). |
| **One primary action** | EXP ARCH Principle 4 | **Broken.** There is no door (§ 5.4). |
| **The walls never change** | EXPBLUE § 14 | **Broken.** The nav is bespoke to the hero shot (§ 5.3). |
| **Timeless, not fashionable** | OHDB § 14 | **Split, and the split is instructive.** The plaster/oak/morning half would look right in 1974 and in 2044. The frosted-glass-over-photography half is *of this moment* (§ 5.6). Both are in the same frame. |

**The pattern in that table is the whole story.** Everything the image expresses about *place, light, material, and air* is exemplary. Everything it expresses about *interface, navigation, action, and state* is a regression. It is a masterpiece of art direction and a weak product design — which is exactly what a concept render is, and exactly why it must be read as one.

## 4. WHAT SHOULD BECOME PERMANENT THA DESIGN LANGUAGE

Six principles. Each is stated so that it survives the deletion of the image — if a principle only makes sense while looking at the render, it is not timeless and does not belong here.

### 4.1 The compositional grammar: light above, horizon, working plane, hand below
The frame reads top-to-bottom as **sky → light → surface → hand**. DESIGN1 § 4 already names this; the image proves it is not merely a diagram but the thing that makes the room feel like a room. It is device-independent (it is the phone's native vertical rhythm, DESIGN1 § 7), it is drawable in pure abstraction, and it survives having no picture in it at all. **This is the single most transferable thing in the image.**

### 4.2 The aperture, and the wall between apertures
**The find.** The orchard reads as a *view* rather than a *backdrop* because it is admitted through **defined openings separated by solid ground** — a window and an open door, with plaster between them. The wall is not the absence of orchard; the wall is *what makes the orchard legible as orchard*. Uniform presence is what kills it — *"everywhere at once is nowhere in particular"* (§ 16). This explains the previously unexplained line in § 6.2, *"framed by composition, never by a drawn frame."*

> **Permanent law to propose:** *the orchard enters through composed, bounded regions with solid ground between and around them; it is never continuous behind the interface, at any strength.*

This principle has immediate teeth, because the live product does the opposite (§ 8.1).

### 4.3 Warmth is the ground temperature of every plane, not an accent on some
There is no white anywhere in the frame — not on the walls, not on the surfaces, not in the panels. Warmth is not applied to a region; it is the temperature of the whole house (OHDB § 5). **The test this gives us is sharp and checkable:** point at any pixel of chrome and ask whether it is warm. One cold plane breaks the room, and it breaks it more than any other single error, because coldness is the one failure the canon calls unrecoverable (Experience Language § 3A.1).

### 4.4 The interface takes the minimum of the frame its work requires; the room keeps the remainder
The panel occupies roughly a fifth of the width and is pushed to one side. It does not centre itself, does not stretch, does not fill. This is the visual form of *"the extra width of a large screen becomes air and view, never more widgets"* (HOUSE1 § 19.3) — but generalised past width into a stance: **the UI is a guest in the room, sized to its job.** Every dashboard that ever went wrong went wrong by disagreeing with this one sentence.

### 4.5 Hierarchy is made of light and weight; colour is spent almost nowhere
The image demonstrates that a screen can lead the eye with no accent colour at all. Colour is therefore free to mean something when it finally appears. **Permanent:** the primary leads by standing in the light, never by wearing a colour the room lacks (TRANSLATION1 § 5.2).

### 4.6 Depth is shallow, warm, and single-directional
One soft family of shadow, one direction, no drama, no parallax, no stage. The image's depth is entirely the depth of a well-lit room (OHDB § 9) and it is more convincing for it.

**Deliberately not on this list:** the kitchen, the island, the beams, the shelving, the apples, the door, the basket. Those are the *subject* of the render. They are not principles and they are not to be preserved — see next.

## 5. WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED

The load-bearing half of this report. Each of these is a real element of the image and each is already forbidden by a rule that already has an owner.

### 5.1 The rendered room itself — the whole kitchen
Experience Blueprint § 16, first spatial anti-pattern, names it precisely: *"The theme park. Literal rooms: illustrated kitchens, drawn furniture, wood-grain..."* — and continues, *"the 'family table' is a feeling produced by material, light, and composition — the moment it becomes a picture of a table, the place has become a costume."* The image is an illustrated kitchen with drawn furniture and wood-grain. TRANSLATION1 (OAK § 9) is blunter still: *"No painted wood-grain, no faux timber texture."* OHDB § 3.1 requires *"a real working plane, not a picture of one."*

**There is no kitchen in the software.** There never was. The kitchen was always the metaphor *for* the feeling; the render made it literal in order to test the feeling, and the test succeeded. The correct response to a successful test is to keep the result and discard the apparatus. **Keep the light. Delete the room.**

### 5.2 The props — the apples, the mug, the herbs, the basket, the open cookbook
Blueprint § 12.1 rule 2 is written as if it had seen this image: *"Data-borne or dead. Every detail renders something TRUE from a canonical owner... A painted prop (drawn fruit, fake steam, decorative crumbs) is fabricated feeling, forbidden by construction."* There is a bowl of drawn fruit in the frame.

The deeper problem is structural, not literal. The image's warmth is currently *carried* by set-dressing — and OHDB § 4 states the counter-principle exactly: *"The house is warm because it is lived in, not because it was dressed."* This room is dressed. **Where the render puts a bowl of apples, the software puts the household's actual plan.** That substitution — props into patina — is the single most important translation in this document.

### 5.3 The six-tile launcher, and the shell it replaces
Six large tiles with icon + label + subtitle is not the walls; it is a launcher, and it is Home-specific furniture. It fails § 14 (*the walls untouched*) — *"the moment a room modifies the frame to feel more like itself, the house has lost a wall, and every other room pays for it."*

**And it is a regression against shipped work.** The canonical bottom nav (UX1) already exists, has **eight** destinations, and renders icon + single label with **no** subtitles (`client/src/components/nav-bar.tsx:672-716`). The image proposes six with descriptions. It is not just uncanonical — it is worse than what is already live, denser, and it would have to be identical in every room to be legal, which subtitles cannot survive. **Ignore the nav in this image entirely.**

### 5.4 The glance card without a door — the dashboard at the threshold
Three metric rows plus a progress bar plus an amber alert badge is a dashboard greeting the arrival. OHDB § 13.1 *Things to avoid* leads with *"a dashboard of metrics greeting the arrival"*; Experience Language Principle G is *home is not the dashboard*.

Worse, and more important: **there is no one primary action anywhere in the frame.** Home's entire job is *"arrive and be oriented, then step through the one right door for the hour"* (DESIGN1 § 0.1). The image offers four chevron rows and six tiles — that is a menu, not a threshold. This is the most serious *product* failure in the image and it is invisible next to the beautiful light, which is precisely why it needs naming. `2 items need your review`, in amber, with an alert glyph, at the threshold, is also *attention taken rather than borrowed* (Experience Architecture Principle 8).

### 5.5 The Cookbook plate — the image disproving itself
The second image is the cautionary tale, and it is worth more than the first as a teaching artefact. Extrapolate the render honestly into a working room and it collapses:

- The environment is **blurred** behind a giant photoreal book — depth-of-field as drama, and the orchard reduced to uniform mush behind everything: *wallpaper* (§ 16), at last.
- **Text sits on the rendered page.** Curved, italic, low-contrast, in perspective. § 6.1: type gets ground plane *"without negotiation"*, and *"legibility is never traded for atmosphere."* This trades it away completely — the recipe is barely readable.
- It is **a drawn book**, which OHDB § 13.3 forbids by name.
- Cookbook is **E2** — *a framed, partial presence in one committed region*. Here the whole frame is environment: *all view, no room* (§ 16).
- The left rail is **a second navigation**. Home has bottom tiles; Cookbook has back-link + rail + tiles. **The shell forked between two screens of the same concept set.**

That the same designer, in the same sitting, produced a compliant Home and a non-compliant Cookbook is the most useful evidence in this whole exercise: **it shows the render's grammar does not survive contact with functional density.** Which is the exposure scale's entire thesis (§ 6.2), independently rediscovered by failure.

### 5.6 The frosted glass — the one datable thing in the frame
Translucent blurred panels over a photographic hero is *of this moment* (and of iOS 7, and of Big Sur). Apply OHDB § 14.2's test — *would this still look right in ten years, or would it look OF a specific past moment?* — and the plaster and the oak pass, and the glass does not. It also fails structurally: a translucent panel over a photograph has **contingent contrast**, so it cannot be measured against the accessibility floors (UIA § 15) once, and it is exactly why the canon insists type gets *solid* ground. **Warm, solid, opaque panels. Not glass.**

### 5.7 The wall aphorism and the tagline
*"Today's thought — Nourish your family with food that brings you together"*, framed on the wall, is: not data-borne; not the household's life; a **second** ornament in a room permitted exactly one (§ 12.1 rule 1 — Home's one is the greeting); and the product talking about itself on its own wall, which is *technology becoming the subject* (§ 1.5). The footer line *"Real food. Real families. Better together."* is a **marketing line inside the product** — TRANSLATION1 (*Thresholds* § 9): *"No splash screen, loading theatre, or marketing hero standing between the person and the room."*

Both are the same error: the house telling the household what the house is for. A home does not do that. **Delete both.**

### 5.8 The fixed perspective — the quiet practical killer
A photoreal room has a vanishing point. Interfaces reflow; vanishing points do not. Most households arrive on a phone (DESIGN1 § 7; PX1), and there is no honest way to reflow a perspective render from a 1400px hero to a 390px column — the horizon, the island, and the window cannot hold their relationship. Add the unresolved dark-mode open item (§ 18.4), localisation, and long household names, and **the render is not a design; it is one composition at one size for one household.** A design has to survive all the sizes and all the states. This one has been drawn at its best angle, on its best day.

*(A quieter note, worth one line: a Cotswolds farmhouse with beams and a walled orchard is a specific and rather expensive kind of home. "A modern home in an ancient orchard" is a **feeling** every household should be able to occupy. Rendered literally, it becomes an aspirational lifestyle image — someone else's kitchen — and the household stops being the subject. Abstraction is not only the legal route here; it is the more inclusive one.)*

## 6. HOW THIS TRANSLATES INTO A PROFESSIONAL SOFTWARE INTERFACE

The whole translation, in one line:

> **The render shows the feeling of standing in the room. The interface must produce that same feeling out of light, warmth, air, and one framed aperture — with nothing drawn.**

Element by element, this is the substitution table. Left column: what the render uses. Right column: what the software uses to produce *the identical feeling*.

| The render achieves it with… | The interface achieves it with… |
|---|---|
| A photoreal kitchen | **Nothing.** There is no room. The room was always the feeling produced by the four things below. |
| A wooden island | **One warm ground plane** with real elevation and one soft warm shadow — a working surface, not a picture of one *(needs the UIA § 4 amendment)* |
| Plaster walls in morning light | **The warm canvas** — already law, already live (`--background: 42 27% 95%`) |
| A window and an open door | **One composed orchard aperture at the room's governed E-level**, abstract and still, with solid ground between and around it |
| Apples, mug, herbs, open book | **The household's real data** — the Living Details. Props become patina. This is the whole of § 5.2, made constructive. |
| A framed thought on the wall | **Deleted.** Home's one ornament is the greeting, and it is data-borne. |
| Frosted glass panels | **Solid warm panels** — measurable contrast, no trend |
| Six subtitled tiles | **The canonical shell** (UX1), unchanged, in every room |
| Four metric rows + an alert | **Orientation, then one door** re-aimed by relevance |
| Depth of field | **Nothing.** Place survives total stillness (§ 6.1). |

Read down the right column and notice what it is: **it is DESIGN1.** The image has not overturned the previous concept — it has *validated* it, and supplied the one thing DESIGN1 could not: proof that the result will be warm. That is the honest relationship between the two, and it is a much better outcome than a replacement would have been. The canon was right; it just could not show its work. Now it can.

**What genuinely changes as a result of the image**, beyond confidence:

1. **The aperture principle** (§ 4.2) is new, and it is load-bearing. It converts *"never wallpaper"* from a prohibition into a construction: openings with wall between them.
2. **The E3 openness is more generous than the programme assumed.** The image gives the view roughly two-thirds of the frame and stays calm. DESIGN1's plates are more timid. Home can afford more sky than we thought — *at Home only*.
3. **The panel is off-centre, and it is better for it.** Pushing the UI to one side (rather than centring a column) is what lets the room breathe. That is a compositional insight worth carrying into the Home layout.

## 7. THE RECOMMENDED STATUS — HOW TO ADOPT IT WITHOUT IT BEING BUILT

The mission is to establish this as the canonical visual North Star. It should be established, and it must be established **with its refusals attached in the same decision** — because an image of this quality, filed without them, *will* be built. Renders have gravity. The recommended status line, verbatim:

> **The North Star reference — the feeling to design toward. It is a concept render, not a specification: its light, warmth, air, aperture, and composition are canonical direction; its drawn kitchen, props, wall text, tagline, glass panels, and navigation are anti-patterns this house already forbids (Experience Blueprint § 16, § 12). No surface copies it. Every surface is measured against the feeling it produces.**

**Why I recommend rather than make this change:** Blueprint § 18 requires that governing change be *"named, checked for conflict against this document and its governors, and added deliberately."* The image conflicts with § 12 and § 16 as drawn. Adopting it is therefore a decision with a conflict to resolve, and that is the reviewer's to make, not mine to assume. If adopted, the natural home is beside ORCHARD3 in the visual lineage — it is the Kept Room's proof, and ORCHARD3 § 5 is where the Kept Room was chosen.

**One caution on the canon itself.** The Experience Blueprint § 1.3 warns that the governance *"was becoming a library rather than a blueprint — the vision reconstructible only by reading five documents in the right order."* There are now six, plus this. This image is worth more than any of them for onboarding a designer in ten seconds. **Consider it the front page of the canon, not a seventh chapter.**

## 8. THE FIRST UI CHANGES

**No UI is changed by this report.** These are identified, sequenced, and unstarted. The sequencing is governed, not preferential — most of the render's material language is behind a gate that has not landed.

### 8.1 The first change, and it is a deletion — retire the orchard backdrop

**The live product ships the wallpaper anti-pattern right now.** `client/src/components/layout/orchard-backdrop.tsx:9` paints a photographic orchard at `opacity: 0.90` behind **every room**, uniformly, on one plane — with a **parallax** (`--orchard-parallax-strength: 10px`, `index.css:73-75`). Measured against the canon:

- *"The orchard is never wallpaper... a backdrop applied uniformly behind everything is the flattening the Place Principles forbid — everywhere at once is nowhere in particular"* (§ 6.1).
- *"The orchard never animates"* (§ 6.1); *"The rendered world — parallax depths... anything game-like"* (§ 16).
- Every room is currently at the same exposure, so the Exposure Scale (§ 6.2) is not implemented at all.

**This is the highest-value change available and it needs no new visual law**, because it removes a violation rather than adding a vocabulary. It is also the exact point the North Star makes: the image's orchard is powerful *because* it comes through two apertures with wall between them, and the live orchard is inert *because* it is smeared behind everything at 90%. **Adopting the North Star means deleting the backdrop, not enriching it.** Retire it product-wide; then reintroduce the orchard at Home only, as one composed aperture, when the orchard has an owner.

### 8.2 Warm the counter — the one cold plane in a warm house
`--card: 0 0% 100%` (`index.css:11`) is pure white: the working surface, the *counter*, is the only cold value in an otherwise warm palette (`--background: 42 27% 95%`). Today it is masked — cards render at 82% opacity so the cream canvas bleeds through (`components/ui/card.tsx:12`) — which means **the house is warm by accident, through translucency, rather than by construction, through material.** That is fragile and it is backwards. This is a conformance defect against existing law (UIA § 7's warm palette), not a new direction: **a token change, correcting white to a warm surface, and dropping the opacity crutch.** Cheapest meaningful move in the entire report.

### 8.3 Give Home a door
Home currently has **no primary action** — three equal-weight card links, a quiet dashboard link, and not one `<Button>` on the page (`pages/home-experience-page.tsx:179-397`). The image has the same flaw, so it offers no help here; DESIGN1 § 0.1 and Experience Architecture Principle 4 do. **Resolve Home to orientation + one door, re-aimed by relevance across the day.** This is behaviour, so it routes to the Experience Architecture — and it is the most valuable single change to the product in this list, quite apart from how anything looks.

### 8.4 Close the two-shell open item — now precisely located
Blueprint § 18.2 (*Home's header*) is real and now has an address. `home-experience-page.tsx` is the **only one of sixteen** `WorkspaceHeader` pages that does not use `pageContainerClass`: it hand-rolls `max-w-3xl` (`:183`) while passing `wide` to its own banner (`max-w-screen-2xl`). The container owner's docstring says it exists precisely to stop this (`workspace-header.tsx:494-495`). **Bring Home onto the canonical container.** Closes a named open item and unblocks the Blueprint.

### 8.5 Remove the token bypass on Home
Home's icon chips use hardcoded HSL literals (`home-experience-page.tsx:223`, `:280`, `:324`) instead of semantic tokens — a second owner of colour (UIA § 16). Small, mechanical, and it must be done before any palette work lands or the palette will not reach Home.

### Blocked until the governance path lands — do not start
The ground plane, the warm single-direction shadow, the penumbra hierarchy, and the aperture's exposure values **cannot ship** until the **UIA § 4 amendment** admits the depth/light vocabulary and the exposure values enter as **tokens by admission** (§ 2.4). The orchard aperture additionally needs open item § 18.1 (*the orchard's canonical owner*) closed. **The first genuine North Star work is therefore governance, not pixels** — and § 8.1–8.5 are all deliverable before it, because each of them removes a violation or a defect rather than adding a new visual law.

## 9. COMPLIANCE

A design report. It touches no code, no component, no token, and no governing document; it recommends and does not adopt.

- **The Experience Test (EXPBLUE § 15.3):** not applicable — this report designs no screen. Applied *to the image*: *which room?* Home (plate 1) and Cookbook (plate 2). *How should someone feel?* At home — achieved. *The one thing?* **Unanswered by the image** — its central defect (§ 5.4).
- **The Blueprint Checks (§ 15.2), applied to the image:** one home ✗ (two shells across two plates, § 5.5) · a room not a theme ✗ (*the theme park*, § 5.1) · the map respected ✓ Home E3 / ✗ Cookbook E2 · orchard law ✓ Home / ✗ Cookbook (wallpaper, text on view) · one morning ✓ · material honesty ✗ (drawn wood-grain, text on imagery) · Living Detail discipline ✗ (props; a second ornament) · the Companion ✗ (absent from both plates — worth noting: the friend has no chair in this house) · the walls untouched ✗ · the governance path ✓ (nothing shipped).
- **The Design Character Check (OHDB § 16.2):** architectural character ✗ (faux material) · interior philosophy ✗ (dressed, not kept) · timeless not fashionable ~ (split — plaster passes, glass fails) · design disappears ✗ (the render *is* the subject; it is the most memorable thing in the frame, which is the failure the standard names) · the room reading ✓ Home / ✗ Cookbook.
- **One owner per rule (EXPBLUE § 18; Architecture Principle 2):** every rule above is cited to its owner; this report creates none. § 4.2's aperture principle is offered as a **proposed** addition to the Blueprint's § 6 and is deliberately not written as law here — if adopted, it belongs to § 6, not to this document.

**The image fails nine of the Blueprint Checks and it is still the best thing in the programme.** Both of those statements are true, and holding them at once is the entire discipline this report is asking for. A concept render is *supposed* to fail the checks — it is answering a different question. It answers *"is the Kept Room warm?"*, and the answer is yes, decisively. It was never answering *"what should we build?"*, and it must not be promoted to that question.
