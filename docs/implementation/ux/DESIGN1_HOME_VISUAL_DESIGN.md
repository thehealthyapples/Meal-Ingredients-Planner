## 0. FRAME

### 0.1 The one room, named — the Experience Test

Answered before anything else (Experience Blueprint § 15.3):

- **Which room is this?** **Home** — the threshold and the heart; the one room at **E3, the open view**; the Entrance Hall every session begins in and every room is reached through (Experience Blueprint § 4, § 6.2; TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Thresholds*).
- **How should someone feel here?** **At home** — welcomed and expected on arrival; oriented and unhurried while here; calm and kept on return — the seven feelings at their warm temperature (Experience Language § 3, § 3A).
- **What is the ONE thing it helps them do?** **Arrive and be oriented** — *"how are we, and what's next?"* — then step through the one right door for the hour (Experience Architecture Principle 4; Experience Blueprint § 4.1).

### 0.2 The four evaluation questions — the lens on every element

The mission requires every element to be evaluated by four questions. They are applied per element in §§ 3–13 and, in summary, they are the load-bearing test of the whole design:

1. **Does it belong in this house?** — is it the *entrance hall* of a warm, lived-in home, or is it a dashboard, a landing page, a home screen?
2. **Does it preserve The Kept Room?** — is warmth carried by light and honest material, quiet composed rather than bare, patina by data never paint (ORCHARD3 § 5; OHDB § 4)?
3. **Does technology disappear?** — is the tool invisible and the work done, or is the surface showing off its cleverness (Experience Blueprint § 1.5; TRANSLATION1 § 5, *technology should quietly disappear*)?
4. **Does the household remain present?** — is the family the most present thing in the room — their name, their plan, their real life — or has the software become the subject (TRANSLATION1 § 5, *the household should always feel present*; § 4.4 *Household presence*)?

Any element that fails one of the four is not finished, whatever else it does well.

### 0.3 The governance path — what this design may become, and when

DESIGN1 is design intent and jumps none of the governed gates (Experience Blueprint § 2.4; OHDB § 2.4; TRANSLATION1 § 2.4). The plates render the Kept Room's warm ground, lit oak counter and soft single-direction depth as *design intent* — none of it ships as drawn:

1. The Kept Room's **depth / ground-plane / warm-light vocabulary** sits beyond the UI Architecture's current flat-surface law (UIA § 4) and may not ship until the **governed UIA § 4 amendment** admits it.
2. **Home's exposure and light values** (E3; the morning hue and direction; the warm neutral ground) enter only as **semantic tokens by admission** (UIA § 16), never as per-surface choices.
3. The **greeting** and every other Living Detail are admitted **as their own named decisions** against the Experience Review Questions (Experience Blueprint § 12.1, rule 6).
4. **The Kept Room itself** is a *recommended* visual direction (ORCHARD3 § 5.4); DESIGN1 draws Home in it on the mission's instruction, gated on the Kept Room's graduation into the UI Architecture / OHDB by amendment.

Two Blueprint open items (§ 18) bear directly on Home and must close before it ships: the **orchard's canonical owner** and **Home's header**. DESIGN1 depends on both and resolves neither.

## 1. THE VISUAL THESIS — HOME DRAWN AS THE KEPT ROOM

Stated whole, as one interface identity (the synthesis TRANSLATION1 § 6 owns, applied to the one room):

> **Home is warm morning light on honest material, generous with air, oriented to an ancient orchard held at the window, decorated only by a family's real life, framed by walls that never change** — the Entrance Hall of *a modern home in an ancient orchard*.

The plate-set carries this thesis in three grounds and one horizon (Plate 02): the world behind (the orchard, constant), the room in the middle (the warm working counter, the only layer that varies), and what floats in front (the Companion, overlays). Only the middle ground ever changes from room to room; the whole mechanism by which THA is many places and one home (Experience Blueprint § 8.1; TRANSLATION1 § 6).

**The one design decision beneath all others** is HOUSE1 § 0.3, carried here into every plate: *the house keeps one unchanging morning; the household's day moves through it.* Home's "rhythm" is carried entirely by what is true right now — never by the house's light, theme, palette or mood (TRANSLATION1 § 4.3 *Morning Rhythm*). The visual consequence is drawn deliberately in the presentation itself: **the plates keep one warm morning even when the presentation frame is viewed in dark theme** — the frame changes, the room does not.

## 3. DESKTOP — THE ROOM ENTIRE  *(Plate 01)*

The fullest expression of the three grounds and Home's signature inversion: **the orchard is the largest thing and the quietest thing** — area, not attention (HOUSE1 § 4; ORCHARD2 § 3.2). The extra width of a large screen becomes **air and view, never a wider dashboard**; the counter stays compact and centred so the view keeps its share (HOUSE1 § 19.3; TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Space*). Five permanent elements, each callout-numbered on the plate: the walls, the open view (E3), the greeting, the one door, the Companion.

- *Belongs in this house?* Yes — it is entered, oriented, and connected to every room; a hall, not a report (TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Thresholds*).
- *Preserves the Kept Room?* Yes — warm plaster ground, a compact lit oak counter, one soft warm shadow; the soul is the interior, the view is the calm (ORCHARD3 § 5.1).
- *Technology disappears?* Yes — no chrome announces itself; the widest temptation (a dashboard grid) is refused (Experience Blueprint § 16).
- *Household present?* Yes — the greeting in their own hand is the single warmest mark; the room leads with the family (TRANSLATION1 § 4.4).

## 4. ANNOTATED LAYOUT — THE ANATOMY  *(Plate 02)*

The frame reads top-to-bottom as **sky → light → surface → hand** (HOUSE1 § 2.2). The diagram labels the three grounds and the one horizon where the morning meets the counter, and states the law of depth — **depth means distance, not drama**; one soft, warm, single-direction shadow, no parallax (TRANSLATION1 § 5.4; § 6). Only Home earns this much sky, because only Home is E3; every other room lowers the horizon because it has more work (Experience Blueprint § 6.2). This is the anatomy a returning household never has to relearn (HOUSE1 § 3).

## 5. VISUAL HIERARCHY — THE EYE'S PATH  *(Plate 03)*

The order the eye meets Home: **greeting → one door → orientation → view → Companion → walls** (HOUSE1 § 4). The deliberate inversion — the orchard largest in area, quietest in attention — is Home's signature. Hierarchy is carried by **light and weight, not colour**: the primary leads by standing in the morning light, not by wearing an accent the room lacks (TRANSLATION1 § 5.2 *light before colour*; OHDB § 6, § 7). Contrast is measured against the accessibility floors before adoption (UIA § 15). *In a warm minimalist room, hierarchy is made of light and material, so colour and motion never have to shout* (ORCHARD3 § 3).

## 6. TABLET — THE ROOM ON THE TABLE  *(Plate 04)*

The room widens; the counter does not sprawl. As width grows, the counter holds a comfortable measure and lets **air take the extra room** (TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Space*; OHDB § 8). Tablet is where E3 begins to feel most like *standing at the wide window* — the Pavilion's disciplined view, kept warm at Home (ORCHARD3 § 5.2; HOUSE1 § 19.2). The fuller shell navigation returns where width allows; the Companion keeps its corner.

## 7. MOBILE — THE ROOM IN THE HAND  *(Plate 05)*

The **primary** design, because most households arrive on a phone (PX1; Experience Architecture). The vertical rhythm — sky above, counter below, one horizon — is the phone's native gift, so Home's composition is *most itself* here. **E3 is held**, not dropped to a strip: the orchard keeps a generous band because Home's purpose still includes the view (Experience Blueprint § 6.2). The counter fills the width; greeting, state and one door stack in reading order; the one door sits thumb-reachable in the lower third; the **canonical bottom navigation** is the shell (UX1); the Companion keeps its fixed corner. *One house at three sizes — same walls, same morning, same counter, same one door; the device changes the room's proportions, never its identity* (Experience Blueprint § 5.2; HOUSE1 § 19).

## 8. TYPOGRAPHY HIERARCHY — THE THREE VOICES  *(Plate 06)*

Three voices, one hierarchy, shown as **role and relationship** — *every size, weight and face is UIA § 8* (TRANSLATION1 § 6):

- **The Hand** — the signature voice, THA's own hand, rationed to the single moment the house says hello, and forbidden in all functional UI (Experience Blueprint § 10; TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Thresholds*).
- **Display** — identity moments and section titles; quiet weight, never shouting in bold (UIA § 8).
- **Working** — the humane reading voice that carries the whole product, *read never noticed*; warmth lives in the **setting** — measure and leading — never in decorative letterforms (OHDB § 8; TRANSLATION1 § 4.2 *Linen*).

Type is set for **breathing**: the house never shrinks type to fit, it discloses more instead. Text sits on the counter's solid ground, never on the view; contrast is measured against the floors before adoption (Experience Blueprint § 6.1; UIA § 15). A room never coins its own typeface to feel like itself — that is *the costume* (TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Rooms*).

## 9. SPACING STRATEGY — AIR AS A MATERIAL  *(Plate 07)*

Air is a **building material**, not leftover room, and the resting state of every surface (TRANSLATION1 § 4.1 *Space*; § 5.1 *space before decoration*). When there is more to say, the counter **gives more room; it never crowds the work to fit** (HOUSE1 § 2.3). All spacing draws from **one named scale** (UIA § 9), shown on the plate as an even rising rhythm beside a block of pure air marked *the resting state*. The **emphasis budget** is spent on a few elements — the greeting, the one door — and withheld from everything else; *calm is the layout, not a coat applied to it* (UIA § 3; TRANSLATION1 § 4.3 *Calm*). Content that will not fit with its air intact is **split, not compressed** (UIA § 9). A bigger screen becomes **more air and more view, never more widgets** (HOUSE1 § 19.3).

## 10. COMPANION PLACEMENT  *(Plate 08)*

The knowledgeable friend at the counter — **a presence, not a room**; one fixed chair, lower-right foreground, at every hour and in every room, so the household never hunts for it (TRANSLATION1 § 4.4 *Companion presence*; Experience Blueprint § 13; HOUSE1 § 9). Its **entire sign of life is timing**: it arrives a beat after you — no face, no pulse, no typing dots, no simulated mood (UIA § 10). It waits (silence is a valid state), offers once and never decides, is lit as part of the room, and never greets before the house has or follows the household room to room. Its finest hour is the one where, a few seconds in, you have forgotten it is software at all (Orchard Living Book, *Cooking Together*).

## 11. MOTION NOTES — LIGHTING, NOT LIGHT SHOWS  *(Plate 09)*

The one test, quotable in any review: **if the person notices the animation before the content, the animation has failed** (Experience Language Principle H; Experience Blueprint § 9). Exactly **two** motions are sanctioned; everything else is still:

1. **The one light moment** — morning sun settling on the counter, once a day, on arrival; the rest of the day and every return have none (ORCHARD2 § 5.2; HOUSE1 § 7).
2. **The Companion's arrival beat** — manners rendered as timing (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).

**Still by construction:** the shell never moves, the orchard never moves, Living Details never animate; place survives total stillness (Experience Blueprint § 6.1, § 12.5, § 14). **Motion yields to intent:** if the person acts during any settle, it completes immediately and gets out of the way — *attention is borrowed, never taken* (Experience Architecture Principle 8). Reduced-motion is fully honoured: with motion off, Home is *already there*, complete, losing nothing (UIA § 11). Forbidden: an evening theme, a dusk sweep, a pulsing glow, a loading theatre — **the second sun** (TRANSLATION1 § 4.3 *Morning Rhythm*; § 4.1 *Light*).

## 12. EMPTY STATE — THE KEPT QUIET  *(Plate 10)*

A well-run household on a calm day has little to report, so the quiet Home is a **primary state, and the warmest one** — *the deliberate quiet of a room someone keeps, not the emptiness of a room no one uses* (TRANSLATION1 § 4.3 *Quiet corners*; OHDB § 4). This exact distinction is why the Kept Room was chosen over the cold Glass Pavilion (ORCHARD3 § 5). Home is **honest in absence** — no invented content, no filler, no guilt; when there is no useful next step it offers none and stays complete (Experience Blueprint § 12.3). The single guard against calm curdling into **cold** is that a quiet Home is visibly *lived in*: the household's own name in the greeting, the orchard alive beyond the glass, the friend in the chair (Experience Language § 3A.4; HOUSE1 § 15). *A full-strength room with nothing in it must never read as "nobody home."*

## 13. RETURNING HOME  *(Plate 11)*

A household enters Home many times a day, and **every entry after the first is a return, not an arrival** — the design of a return is the design of **constancy** (HOUSE1 § 8; Orchard Living Book, *Returning Home*). The plate sets the first entry beside a later return: the greeting in THA's hand, the one light moment and full orientation give way to a plain quiet state line, no re-performed greeting, no light moment — the room *simply, already, there*. The room is exactly as it was left; nothing is rearranged to seem fresh; the signature hand is not re-spent; the Companion is there again in the same chair, a beat behind, not pouncing with everything that happened while you were out (Experience Blueprint § 12.2; § 13). *The warmth of a return is being kept, not being performed at* (Experience Language Principle E).

## 14. THE PERMANENCE CONTRACT & THE GOLD STANDARD

Two reference plates close the set. **The permanence contract** (drawn as a two-column table): the house keeps one unchanging morning — light, orchard, materials, layout, shell, Companion's chair, the seven feelings' warm temperature, one primary action, the accessibility floors — while only the household's own truth changes — the greeting word, the orientation line, the door's destination, the Living Detail's data, and whether Home is quiet or has content to disclose (HOUSE1 § 20; Experience Blueprint § 15.2; TRANSLATION1 § 4.3 *Permanence*). If a proposed change moves something from the left column, it must **STOP**.

**The gold standard:** Home is entered first and returned to most, so its design *is* the household's sense of the whole house — which is why every future room inherits its **grammar** (arrive before you work; one warm working ground; one primary action; the friend a beat behind; constant walls; one morning, one orchard, one Companion, one shell) but **not its E3 horizon** — the discipline of *sizing the view to the room's purpose* is Home's gift; the *level* is each room's own (HOUSE1 § 22; Experience Blueprint § 5, § 6.2). *Get the entrance hall right and every room off it is already half-designed.*

## 15. COMPLIANCE

A design specification, not governing law and not an implementation; it touches no code, no component, no token, and no governing document. It is checked against the gates it will one day be built under, read against the Kept Room Translation (TRANSLATION1 § 2.5).

- **The Experience Test (Experience Blueprint § 15.3):** *Which room?* Home, the Entrance Hall (§ 0.1). *How should someone feel?* At home — welcomed, oriented, kept, warm. *The one thing?* Arrive and be oriented, then take the one door. **Passes.**
- **The Blueprint Checks (Experience Blueprint § 15.2):** one home (Plates 01, 04, 05); a room not a theme (Plates 06–07, thesis § 1); the map respected — Home E3, full morning, compact counter (Plates 01–02); orchard law — still, ancient, never wallpaper/text/animation (Plates 01, 09); one morning — no dusk at any hour (Plate 09, contract § 14); material honesty — one ground, never nested, air generous (Plates 02, 07); Living Detail discipline — one, the greeting, data-borne, honest in absence (Plates 06, 10); the Companion in its chair (Plate 08); the walls untouched (Plate 02); the governance path declared, not jumped (§ 0.3). **Passes.**
- **The Design Character Check (OHDB § 16.2):** structural honesty, glazed toward the orchard, natural light and material, uncluttered, quietly confident (Plates 01–02, 07); composed emptiness, everything meant, decorated only by real life (Plates 07, 10); timeless not fashionable — one morning, no evening theme, no trend (Plate 09, § 1); design disappears (§ 0.2 Q3); the room reading honoured — matches OHDB § 13.1 Home (Plate 01). **Passes.**
- **The four evaluation questions (§ 0.2):** applied per element in §§ 3–13; every element belongs in this house, preserves the Kept Room, lets technology disappear, and keeps the household present. **Passes.**
- **One owner per rule (Experience Blueprint § 18; Architecture Principle 2):** every rule is cited to its owner and, for interface consequences, to the Kept Room Translation; this document creates none. If any line here is found to duplicate an owned rule, this line is the defect and is corrected to a citation.
