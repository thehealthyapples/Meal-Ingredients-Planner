> The **Brand Constitution** (`BRAND1`, 2026-07-17) is the governing source for **THA's enduring identity** — the single statement of *who The Healthy Apples is*, so that everyone who builds it is building the same thing and a decade of features can be checked against one identity. It is the *why* and the *who* that the Experience Governance documents each express a face of, defining: **why THA exists · Our Promise · Our Character · Our Responsibility · what THA will never become · the Household First Principle · the Trust Test · the One Question before every release**, and closing with **ten Constitutional Principles every future feature must satisfy**. It **restates no rule** — it references and quotes owners throughout — and is the canonical owner only of what none of them owns: **the enduring identity, assembled in one place**, plus the six instruments the concepts add up to (the mission statement, the consolidated *never become*, the **Household First Principle**, the **Trust Test**, the **One Question**, and the **ten Principles**), each built *from* its layer owner and citing it. It sits on **two axes**: **subordinate to `ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES.md`** on the engineering law (bound in particular by **Principle 2** — one owner per fact — and **Principle 6** — non-fabrication, *"Trust is the product … This is non-negotiable"* — which is *why* it references rather than restates), and **upstream of the four Experience Governance documents** on the identity axis — the Experience Architecture (behaviour), Experience Language (feeling), UI Architecture (look), and Experience Blueprint (vision) — which it never overrides and where, on any question of *rule*, the rule's owner prevails and the Constitution is corrected. It is **distinct from `THA_UI_ARCHITECTURE.md` § 10** (Brand Identity Architecture) and § 14 (Visual Trust), which own THA's **visual** identity (marks · logo · the apple · imagery); the Constitution owns **enduring** identity and touches no pixel. Its two enforcement additions — **The One Question before every release** (*"Does this leave the household with less to carry, and could they trust everything it tells them?"*) and the ten Principles — sit **above** the existing gates (the UX/UI checklists, the Experience Review Questions, the Experience Test, the AI Architecture Compliance block) as the identity check they each verify a part of, never in place of them. It creates **no** entity, owner, service, capability, route, token, component, string, or business logic, and no runtime code reads it. Live status: [`THA_BRAND_CONSTITUTION.md`](./THA_BRAND_CONSTITUTION.md).

> The **Trust & Compliance Architecture** (`BUS1`, 2026-07-18) is the governing source for **how THA publishes what it promises, records what a household agreed to, honours their rights over their own data, and lets them reach a person** — and, equally, for how THA can *prove* it did each of those things. It is **required reading before any implementation that touches personal data, a legal document, a consent, or a support channel.** It was written against a finding `LAUNCH1` had already recorded in one line — *"Legal and compliance: 0%"* — in a platform that stores **Article 9 special category health data** (allergies, dietary restrictions, health goals, height and weight, children in the household) **by design**, because a food product that does not know a household's allergies is unsafe. Its central instrument is the observation that **Article 15 (access) and Article 17 (erasure) are the same question asked with different verbs** — *what do you hold about me?* — so they are given **one owner with two verbs** (`server/privacy/personal-data-registry.ts`, Domain 35), because implemented separately they drift, and the drift is always in the same direction and always the dangerous one: the export gains a table and the erasure does not, so THA quietly keeps data it has told a household it destroyed. That registry is **executable rather than declarative**, and the reason is stated as evidence rather than preference: the schema is not regular enough for a generic interpreter — the CORE of a household's data (`meals`, `shopping_list`, `planner_weeks`, `user_preferences`) declares **no foreign key to `users` at all**, ~10 operator-audit tables declare one with no `ON DELETE` and therefore **BLOCK** the delete, `household_eaters` is `ON DELETE SET NULL` so a person's declared **allergies outlive their account as an orphan**, the planner is two levels of parent with no cascade at either, and the `session` table is invisible to Drizzle entirely, so a "deleted" account stays signed in. It establishes four canonical owners (the legal register `shared/legal/`, the consent vocabulary `shared/privacy/consent.ts`, the personal data registry, and the support vocabulary `shared/support/support-request.ts`), three of them **pure zero-I/O modules beside the entity spine** under Principle 5, and **nine rules (TC1–TC9)**. Three of its decisions are recorded as decisions rather than omissions: **the consent ledger is append-only** (a mutable consent record demonstrates nothing, because it cannot tell *"consented then withdrew"* from *"never consented and somebody edited the row"* — **a ledger you can overwrite is not evidence**, Art. 7(1)); **there is deliberately no cookie banner** (THA sets one strictly-necessary `connect.sid` and, with `saveUninitialized: false`, **none at all until you sign in**, so a banner with analytics and marketing categories that do not exist would state in the product's own voice that THA does things it does not do — Core Principle 6 — and train households to dismiss a dialog without reading it); and **placeholder company facts render as a visible, undismissable notice** rather than being published as if verified, which is honest absence applied at the surface where getting it wrong matters most. It creates **no** payment path, subscription, commercial term, analytics, scheduler, or second authorisation authority — `server/lib/access.ts` remains the only one, and the registry *labels* where it never *authorises*. Commercial terms are explicitly deferred to `BUS2` rather than shipped dormant. Live status and the `BUS2` handover: [`THA_TRUST_AND_COMPLIANCE_ARCHITECTURE.md`](./THA_TRUST_AND_COMPLIANCE_ARCHITECTURE.md) § 11.

> The **Commercial Architecture** (`BUS2A`, 2026-07-18) is the governing source for **what THA's plans are, what a household is entitled to, how a subscription would begin, change and end — and, before any of that, what THA may say to a household about money.** It is **required reading before any implementation that touches a plan, a subscription, an entitlement, a feature gate, a plan limit, or a price.** It creates **no payment path**: BUS2A integrates no provider, processes no payment, and activates no live subscription — both its tables are created empty and stay empty. Its architecture is one arrow — **Billing Provider → Billing Events → Subscription Projection → Entitlements → Product Access** — and the *direction* is the whole design: a provider owns **payment facts** (was a card charged, did an invoice settle), THA owns the **entitlement projection** (what a household may actually do), so that a household's access to the product they depend on is never an availability question about a third party's API. It was written against a finding shaped like `BUS1`'s: the gap was not the missing features but that **the platform was already making a commercial claim it could not substantiate.** `TrialBanner.tsx` offered *"25% off your first 6 months"* and *"your 25% discount code is on its way"* — a discount off an unstated price, for a six-month term that did not exist, on a plan that could not be bought, sent by a mechanism that sends nothing — while THA's own published Terms of Service said, in the same repository, *"The Healthy Apples does not currently charge for anything, and does not process payments."* One of those was a lie to households and the Terms were the true one; BUS1 found it, and **BUS2A withdrew it**. Hence its central instrument is mechanical rather than cultural: **Rule C3 — no commercial claim without configured pricing**, gated by `isPublishable()`, which **fails closed** for the entire platform today, and under which **a saving is computed from the configured amounts and never typed** (a hand-written *"save 20%"* beside prices that save 17% is the same defect, only quieter). Three further decisions are recorded as decisions rather than omissions: **safety is never a paid feature** (C4 — no feature or limit key may gate an allergen warning or a restriction, *a household on the free plan is exactly as safe as one paying*, enforced by a test rather than by intention, and the commercial face of the Experience Architecture's § 17 definition of premium as **not exclusivity**); **entitlement is a household fact** (C5 — a subscription is bought by a person and entitles a *home*, resolved as a **maximum** and never a precedence chain, because under precedence one member's lapsed card would silently remove the whole family's access to a plan somebody else is paying for); and **the lifecycle expires on read, not on a schedule** (C6 — **THA has no scheduler**, which BUS1 recorded and carried forward, so a lifecycle depending on a job that does not exist is wrong by default: the stored record says what *happened*, the projection says what is *true now*). Idempotency is **a `UNIQUE` constraint, not an if-statement** (C7 — every provider redelivers webhooks, so a duplicate is the normal case, and a SELECT-then-INSERT check has a race that concurrent deliveries will find). It **converged four owners of the tier rule into one** and moved two **live** plan limits onto the catalogue from an environment variable and an integer literal — the free plan's own ceiling had been settable by deployment config with no review — **with both values unchanged**. It creates **no** new authorisation authority (`server/lib/access.ts` remains the only one; the projection *classifies* and never *authorises*, and `isAdmin` is deliberately not an input to it), no scheduler, no commercial-terms document (there is nothing yet to agree to, and BUS1 refused dormant clauses for things that do not exist), and **no restriction a household does not already live under**. It is a permanently-**INSTANT** domain and **must never consume Household Time** (C10, HT10 — *a trial must not be longer for a family in Auckland*). Live status and the `BUS2B` handover: [`THA_COMMERCIAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`](./THA_COMMERCIAL_ARCHITECTURE.md) § 6.

> **The Canonical Publication Architecture** (CPuBA1, 2026-07-13) is the governing source for **how THA declares ownership, publishes facts, and verifies they are true at runtime.** It establishes the shape of canonical publication that all 22 major domains follow — the lifecycle (Owner → Publication → Projection → Runtime → Verification) and three variants (seed-owned, knowledge, database-owned). Built from CPI1 (Canonical Publication Integrity Audit) and CPV1 (Canonical Publication Verification Platform), it names the architecture THA discovered itself already using correctly in four separate places without noticing it was one shape. It defines eight governance rules binding on every significant implementation and is the enforcement mechanism for ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES.md's Principle 2 (one owner per fact), Principle 7 (no permanent sync bridges), and Principle 8 (retire on introduction). The verification gate (`npm run verify:publication`) runs every domain's publication contract against reality and fails loudly if projection ≠ owner — turning every CPI1 finding into an automated detection.

> The **Household Time Architecture** (`TIME3`, 2026-07-16) is the governing source for **how THA knows what time it is for a household** — the time zone, today, the phase of day, the calendar week, and which planner week a household is living in. It is **required reading before any implementation that reads a clock, a date, a day, a week, or a season**. Promoted from investigations `TIME1` (the Foundation) and `TIME2` (the Consumer Audit), which remain at `docs/investigations/platform/` as point-in-time analysis and are **retired as sources of rule**. It was written against a finding that inverts the question it was asked: **THA does not lack time — it already tells households the time in six places, with six private definitions, owned by nobody, and at least three of them are wrong.** So Household Time is a **convergence, not an addition**, and its first act is retirement (§ 14 — five rival "current week" implementations, nineteen week-shape declarations across sixteen files, four `getGreeting()` copies, three season implementations, two fabricated-date builders; **five live frames** — UTC · server-process-local · browser-local · noon-anchored-local · week-index — reduced to one). Its canonical owner is **`shared/time/household-time.ts`**, a pure, zero-I/O reference vocabulary **beside the entity spine that owns the rules of household time and none of its data** — the exact class Appendix A of the Source of Truth Register already records for ATTN1 and DEC1, under Principle 5. It needs **two facts, both on owners that already exist**: `households.timeZone` (Domain 16 — *a clock is a property of the home, never of the device or the session*; per-member zones are refused as a split-brain over one shared plan) and `planner_weeks.weekStartDate` (Domain 14 — written **only at week creation and never back-filled**, because that is the only moment THA can honestly know what a week means, and a back-filled anchor is the fabrication the architecture exists to retire). **No new domain, no new store, no new write funnel, no new dependency.** Its central instrument is the **CIVIL/INSTANT model** (§ 7) — *if this household moved to Tokyo tomorrow, would this value have to change?* — which produced the audit's headline: **every INSTANT consumer in THA is correct and every CIVIL consumer is broken, and the line between them is exactly this architecture's boundary.** A duration is not a date: the trial countdown, session lifetime, the 90-day evidence window and every cache TTL are correct *because* they need no calendar, so **five domains are named as MUST NOT CONSUME — a permanent verdict, not a migration backlog** (§ 8.1) — and the Observation Engine is forbidden outright (§ 7 there). Eighteen rules (**HT1–HT18**) bind every consumer, and the boundary rules cite their owners rather than restating them: time may aim **words and doors, never light** (`THA_KEPT_ROOM_TRANSLATION.md` *Morning Rhythm* § 9 — the one-morning law and NORTH2 § 3.5's rejection of visual time both stand untouched); time **triggers**, INT20 **decides**; time reaches the model **only** through a Context View composed by INT17, never templated into a prompt; the attention stack stays clock-free; and **season is not Household Time** — the architecture supplies its input, never its answer. > The Platform Knowledge Completion Architecture is the governing source for **how every knowledge domain THA ships must grow**: one graduation pipeline (candidate → gate → confirm → publish, with rejection as a first-class terminal state), one layered evidence chain, one owner and one "mouth" per knowledge type, and one stated completion criterion per domain. Promoted under `EWO-PKCA1` (2026-07-03), it named a shape THA had already built correctly four separate times without noticing it was one shape. **Extended under `PKR3` (2026-07-11) to admit a fifth domain: Product Knowledge — the canonical knowledge describing The Healthy Apples itself — alongside Food, Nutrition, Recipes and Household Knowledge**, with the Product Knowledge Registry (below) as its canonical owner. `PKR3` creates **no new governing architecture**: Product Knowledge fills a row in every table the document already had (§1.1 pipeline, §2.1 one-mouth, §3.1 MVF, §6.1 completion, §7 roadmap) and invents no lifecycle of its own — which is the clearest evidence available that the generalisation was real. What it adds is the domain's own character (§4.3, §9): Product Knowledge is the platform's **first self-describing knowledge domain**, where the subject and the source are the same system — so the evidence chain inverts. A food fact is wrong because it was *never* true; a product fact is wrong because it *stopped* being true. Fabrication is nearly impossible; **staleness is the whole risk**, and a stale entry is indistinguishable from a fresh one by reading it. Hence **Rule KC14** (for a self-describing domain, *currency* is the evidence standard — `last_verified` and a named owner accountable for it being true *now*), **Rule KC13** (`visibility` is part of the MVF bar and fails closed — the first domain where a missing field is a *security* defect, not an invisible fact), **Rule KC12** (a declined discovery is *recorded*, or every future audit rediscovers it and re-asks the same question forever), and **Rule KC15** (maintenance is not a follow-up; it *is* the work — Product Knowledge decays by default and no other knowledge domain does, which is why updating the registry is a Definition of Done item in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`). It names the domain's declared-vs-enforced gap (Rule KC8) **on the day the domain is created** — currency, visibility, and prose↔inventory bijection are today entirely unenforced — rather than months after it started costing something, as §4.1's Layer 2 gap was. And it fixes the load-bearing distinction: **investigations discover; the registry owns; implementations maintain.** Discovery without transfer of ownership is the failure THA has repeated for three years — something is found, written down beautifully, read once, and never maintained, so the next person to ask finds it out again (Risk R7). Phase 7 of the roadmap exists so the forthcoming **Platform Discovery & Experience Audit** has a canonical destination for everything it discovers, and a gate that fails it if its findings stay inside itself.

> The Product Knowledge Registry Architecture (`PKR1`, 2026-07-11) is the governing source for **knowledge about The Healthy Apples itself** — the question none of the other architectures answers. Platform governs how THA is built; the Source of Truth Register governs which store owns which fact; Intelligence governs how THA reasons; Experience governs how a person encounters THA; UI governs how THA looks. None of them holds the list of what THA *is*: its domains, pages, routes, journeys, capabilities, hidden and admin surfaces, dialogs, integrations, settings, claims, benefits, glossary, and screenshots. That knowledge is currently reconstructed from code and 380+ investigations on every ask, and is never retained. The registry becomes its single owner, under one governing distinction: **investigations discover; the registry owns.** An investigation is history the moment it is written and is never edited; a registry entry is present-tense, carries a named human owner, and is *corrected* rather than superseded — staleness in it is a defect, not drift. It cites Data, Intelligence, Experience, and UI ownership and restates none of it, and it is a description, never a dependency: no runtime code may read it. It defines 28 canonical sections and the folder structure they will occupy beneath `docs/product/` — **which it deliberately does not create or populate.** Enhanced by `PKR2` (2026-07-11) with the three principles that make the registry a platform asset rather than a filing system: **Permission-Aware Product Knowledge** (§11) — one truth, four audiences (`public` · `household` · `admin` · `developer`), monotonic and cumulative, keyed on *role* and never on subscription tier, because knowing what premium does is not access to it; the registry *classifies* what each audience may be told and **never authorises** — `server/lib/access.ts` remains the sole authority on who anyone is, so that a Markdown edit can never become a privilege escalation. **The Registry as Companion Knowledge** (§12) — the Companion *queries* the registry and may never duplicate product knowledge into a prompt, template, or fallback string; it enters the platform as a registered Knowledge Capability composed through INT17, which keeps its ownership of every byte the model reads; filtering happens before composition, never by asking the model to withhold what it has been shown; and the Companion never explains an absence, because acknowledging withheld content discloses the fact the tier protected. **Engineering Governance** (§16) — `PKR2` amends the `PKR1` boundary that forbade any runtime read (now *read, never obeyed*: Intelligence may read it, no code may branch on it), and wires the Definition of Done obligation into `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md` (a mandatory **Product Registry Impact** section, a **Product Registry Compliance** block, and a Completion Gate step) and the implementation template — so every user-facing implementation must update, add, or retire the affected entries **in the same change**, by default rather than by memory. > The **Governing Experience Architecture** (`EXPGOV1`, 2026-07-20) is the **Experience Constitution** — and is **mandatory reading before any UX, UI, frontend or visual implementation**, ahead of every other document in this section.
