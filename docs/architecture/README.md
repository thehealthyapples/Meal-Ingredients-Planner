# THA Governing Architecture

**This directory is the single canonical home for The Healthy Apples' governing architecture.**
Established 2026-06-30 (GOV-AI1). Architecture documents no longer live in `docs/investigations/` — investigation files there are point-in-time analysis and history only.

Anything in this directory is **governing**: required reading before significant implementation, and enforced by the Architecture Compliance Checklist in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`.

> **Architecture Bootstrap (mandatory entry point).** This README is the first thing to read before any significant investigation, recommendation or implementation. The documents listed below are the governing architecture; every proposal and implementation must comply with them. If a proposed change conflicts with the governing architecture: **STOP, explain why, and do not continue until approved.** This bootstrap is enforced as STEP 2 of `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md` (added GOV-AI2, 2026-06-30).

> **The Canonical Publication Architecture** (CPuBA1, 2026-07-13) is the governing source for **how THA declares ownership, publishes facts, and verifies they are true at runtime.** It establishes the shape of canonical publication that all 22 major domains follow — the lifecycle (Owner → Publication → Projection → Runtime → Verification) and three variants (seed-owned, knowledge, database-owned). Built from CPI1 (Canonical Publication Integrity Audit) and CPV1 (Canonical Publication Verification Platform), it names the architecture THA discovered itself already using correctly in four separate places without noticing it was one shape. It defines eight governance rules binding on every significant implementation and is the enforcement mechanism for ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES.md's Principle 2 (one owner per fact), Principle 7 (no permanent sync bridges), and Principle 8 (retire on introduction). The verification gate (`npm run verify:publication`) runs every domain's publication contract against reality and fails loudly if projection ≠ owner — turning every CPI1 finding into an automated detection.

> The **Household Time Architecture** (`TIME3`, 2026-07-16) is the governing source for **how THA knows what time it is for a household** — the time zone, today, the phase of day, the calendar week, and which planner week a household is living in. It is **required reading before any implementation that reads a clock, a date, a day, a week, or a season**. Promoted from investigations `TIME1` (the Foundation) and `TIME2` (the Consumer Audit), which remain at `docs/investigations/platform/` as point-in-time analysis and are **retired as sources of rule**. It was written against a finding that inverts the question it was asked: **THA does not lack time — it already tells households the time in six places, with six private definitions, owned by nobody, and at least three of them are wrong.** So Household Time is a **convergence, not an addition**, and its first act is retirement (§ 14 — five rival "current week" implementations, nineteen week-shape declarations across sixteen files, four `getGreeting()` copies, three season implementations, two fabricated-date builders; **five live frames** — UTC · server-process-local · browser-local · noon-anchored-local · week-index — reduced to one). Its canonical owner is **`shared/time/household-time.ts`**, a pure, zero-I/O reference vocabulary **beside the entity spine that owns the rules of household time and none of its data** — the exact class Appendix A of the Source of Truth Register already records for ATTN1 and DEC1, under Principle 5. It needs **two facts, both on owners that already exist**: `households.timeZone` (Domain 16 — *a clock is a property of the home, never of the device or the session*; per-member zones are refused as a split-brain over one shared plan) and `planner_weeks.weekStartDate` (Domain 14 — written **only at week creation and never back-filled**, because that is the only moment THA can honestly know what a week means, and a back-filled anchor is the fabrication the architecture exists to retire). **No new domain, no new store, no new write funnel, no new dependency.** Its central instrument is the **CIVIL/INSTANT model** (§ 7) — *if this household moved to Tokyo tomorrow, would this value have to change?* — which produced the audit's headline: **every INSTANT consumer in THA is correct and every CIVIL consumer is broken, and the line between them is exactly this architecture's boundary.** A duration is not a date: the trial countdown, session lifetime, the 90-day evidence window and every cache TTL are correct *because* they need no calendar, so **five domains are named as MUST NOT CONSUME — a permanent verdict, not a migration backlog** (§ 8.1) — and the Observation Engine is forbidden outright (§ 7 there). Eighteen rules (**HT1–HT18**) bind every consumer, and the boundary rules cite their owners rather than restating them: time may aim **words and doors, never light** (`THA_KEPT_ROOM_TRANSLATION.md` *Morning Rhythm* § 9 — the one-morning law and NORTH2 § 3.5's rejection of visual time both stand untouched); time **triggers**, INT20 **decides**; time reaches the model **only** through a Context View composed by INT17, never templated into a prompt; the attention stack stays clock-free; and **season is not Household Time** — the architecture supplies its input, never its answer. It was **DECLARED, NOT BUILT** for exactly one day — the order `CANONICAL_PUBLICATION_ARCHITECTURE.md` § Transition Rules requires (*"every new domain must be declared before implementation"*) — and **`CONV1 P5` built it on 2026-07-17**: the module exists (`OWN-4`, pure and clock-free, with its HT18 gate in the same change), the three season implementations are one (`OWN-3` — `shared/seasonal/season-rule.ts`, Domain 11), and `households.timeZone` exists (`SCH-1` — nullable, no back-fill, no silent default). *(This sentence previously read "**It is DECLARED, NOT BUILT (§ 17): the module and both columns do not exist**" — corrected 2026-07-17 in the same change that made it false, rather than repeating the `DOC-4` failure recorded two paragraphs below, where a stale claim sat in this mandatory Bootstrap for five days.)* **`CONV1 P6` converged Phase 3's T2/T3 consumers on 2026-07-17**: the Companion's temporal anchor (`READ-4` — it had been UTC's today, reaching the model as `TODAY:` and deciding the diary day it read and wrote), the freezer's write and comparison (`BEH-6`), the diary's day and both `T12:00:00` guards (`SCH-4`), and the four `getGreeting()` copies (§ 14 target 3, 4 → 1 — the *clock*; the *words* stay INT21's, scheduled as CP3). **What is NOT built:** `planner_weeks.weekStartDate` (Phase 4 — so `resolvePlannerWeek` answers `anchored: false` for every household, the honest floor), and therefore the **T5 consumers** — the five rival "current weeks", streaks and savings — are still live and cannot be converged until the anchor exists. *(This sentence previously read "**no consumer has converged yet** (Phase 3)" — corrected 2026-07-17 in the same change that made it false, which is the `DOC-4`/`KC14` failure recorded two paragraphs below.)* **The law was in force before the code, and that was the point** — it stopped the duplication growing while the module was written; **live status: [`THA_HOUSEHOLD_TIME_ARCHITECTURE.md`](./THA_HOUSEHOLD_TIME_ARCHITECTURE.md) § 17.** The Experience canon is **byte-untouched** — `TRANSLATION1` *Morning Rhythm* § 6 and § 8 **already required** THA to know the hour, so this architecture adds no rule to the document that required it; **the law was right and the platform was behind it.**

> The Platform Knowledge Completion Architecture is the governing source for **how every knowledge domain THA ships must grow**: one graduation pipeline (candidate → gate → confirm → publish, with rejection as a first-class terminal state), one layered evidence chain, one owner and one "mouth" per knowledge type, and one stated completion criterion per domain. Promoted under `EWO-PKCA1` (2026-07-03), it named a shape THA had already built correctly four separate times without noticing it was one shape. **Extended under `PKR3` (2026-07-11) to admit a fifth domain: Product Knowledge — the canonical knowledge describing The Healthy Apples itself — alongside Food, Nutrition, Recipes and Household Knowledge**, with the Product Knowledge Registry (below) as its canonical owner. `PKR3` creates **no new governing architecture**: Product Knowledge fills a row in every table the document already had (§1.1 pipeline, §2.1 one-mouth, §3.1 MVF, §6.1 completion, §7 roadmap) and invents no lifecycle of its own — which is the clearest evidence available that the generalisation was real. What it adds is the domain's own character (§4.3, §9): Product Knowledge is the platform's **first self-describing knowledge domain**, where the subject and the source are the same system — so the evidence chain inverts. A food fact is wrong because it was *never* true; a product fact is wrong because it *stopped* being true. Fabrication is nearly impossible; **staleness is the whole risk**, and a stale entry is indistinguishable from a fresh one by reading it. Hence **Rule KC14** (for a self-describing domain, *currency* is the evidence standard — `last_verified` and a named owner accountable for it being true *now*), **Rule KC13** (`visibility` is part of the MVF bar and fails closed — the first domain where a missing field is a *security* defect, not an invisible fact), **Rule KC12** (a declined discovery is *recorded*, or every future audit rediscovers it and re-asks the same question forever), and **Rule KC15** (maintenance is not a follow-up; it *is* the work — Product Knowledge decays by default and no other knowledge domain does, which is why updating the registry is a Definition of Done item in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`). It names the domain's declared-vs-enforced gap (Rule KC8) **on the day the domain is created** — currency, visibility, and prose↔inventory bijection are today entirely unenforced — rather than months after it started costing something, as §4.1's Layer 2 gap was. And it fixes the load-bearing distinction: **investigations discover; the registry owns; implementations maintain.** Discovery without transfer of ownership is the failure THA has repeated for three years — something is found, written down beautifully, read once, and never maintained, so the next person to ask finds it out again (Risk R7). Phase 7 of the roadmap exists so the forthcoming **Platform Discovery & Experience Audit** has a canonical destination for everything it discovers, and a gate that fails it if its findings stay inside itself.

> The Product Knowledge Registry Architecture (`PKR1`, 2026-07-11) is the governing source for **knowledge about The Healthy Apples itself** — the question none of the other architectures answers. Platform governs how THA is built; the Source of Truth Register governs which store owns which fact; Intelligence governs how THA reasons; Experience governs how a person encounters THA; UI governs how THA looks. None of them holds the list of what THA *is*: its domains, pages, routes, journeys, capabilities, hidden and admin surfaces, dialogs, integrations, settings, claims, benefits, glossary, and screenshots. That knowledge is currently reconstructed from code and 380+ investigations on every ask, and is never retained. The registry becomes its single owner, under one governing distinction: **investigations discover; the registry owns.** An investigation is history the moment it is written and is never edited; a registry entry is present-tense, carries a named human owner, and is *corrected* rather than superseded — staleness in it is a defect, not drift. It cites Data, Intelligence, Experience, and UI ownership and restates none of it, and it is a description, never a dependency: no runtime code may read it. It defines 28 canonical sections and the folder structure they will occupy beneath `docs/product/` — **which it deliberately does not create or populate.** Enhanced by `PKR2` (2026-07-11) with the three principles that make the registry a platform asset rather than a filing system: **Permission-Aware Product Knowledge** (§11) — one truth, four audiences (`public` · `household` · `admin` · `developer`), monotonic and cumulative, keyed on *role* and never on subscription tier, because knowing what premium does is not access to it; the registry *classifies* what each audience may be told and **never authorises** — `server/lib/access.ts` remains the sole authority on who anyone is, so that a Markdown edit can never become a privilege escalation. **The Registry as Companion Knowledge** (§12) — the Companion *queries* the registry and may never duplicate product knowledge into a prompt, template, or fallback string; it enters the platform as a registered Knowledge Capability composed through INT17, which keeps its ownership of every byte the model reads; filtering happens before composition, never by asking the model to withhold what it has been shown; and the Companion never explains an absence, because acknowledging withheld content discloses the fact the tier protected. **Engineering Governance** (§16) — `PKR2` amends the `PKR1` boundary that forbade any runtime read (now *read, never obeyed*: Intelligence may read it, no code may branch on it), and wires the Definition of Done obligation into `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md` (a mandatory **Product Registry Impact** section, a **Product Registry Compliance** block, and a Completion Gate step) and the implementation template — so every user-facing implementation must update, add, or retire the affected entries **in the same change**, by default rather than by memory. The cost of that read path is stated plainly rather than buried: under `PKR1` a stale entry misled a reader who went looking; under `PKR2` it is read aloud to a household in the Companion's voice, and the Companion will always be more convincing than the registry is accurate. **`PKR3` (2026-07-11) places this document under the platform-wide law:** the registry is named the **canonical owner of the Product Knowledge domain** in the Platform Knowledge Completion Architecture (§9), on exactly the footing NK1/NK2 hold for Nutrition and FS1/FS2 hold for Recipes — the domain's own architecture, subordinate to the general one, corrected if the two ever conflict. `PKR3` adds no rule to this document and restates not one line of it; it *cites* it, because restating it would create the second owner Rule PKR13 forbids, in the document that admits the rule. **Defined by `PKR1`/`PKR2`/`PKR3`; built by others, later** *(corrected 2026-07-16, `DOC-4` — this sentence previously read "The registry is still **defined here and populated nowhere**: `docs/product/` does not exist, and `PKR3` deliberately does not create it", which stayed in the mandatory Architecture Bootstrap for five days after it stopped being true)*. All three PKR documents deliberately created nothing, and **that part remains exactly true**. What has changed is the world around them: **`PDA1` (2026-07-11) created and populated `docs/product/`** — 151 files, **154 entries** across 22 of the 28 canonical sections, each carrying a named owner, a visibility tier and `last_verified` — and **`PHASE5A` (2026-07-12) registered the `product-knowledge` capability and built the first runtime read path**, which reads `inventory/product.json` only, never the prose (Rule PKR21). **Live status: [`THA_PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_REGISTRY_ARCHITECTURE.md`](./THA_PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_REGISTRY_ARCHITECTURE.md) § 23**, and Domain 29 of the Source of Truth Register.

> The Experience Architecture (EXP1, 2026-07-10) is the governing source for how people experience THA — the timeless, technology-independent principles (Home as the emotional centre, progressive disclosure, calm before capability, one primary action, and the rest) plus the UX Governance Checklist every user-facing implementation must pass. It is the general law of which the Intelligence Discovery & Presentation Principle and the Companion Card Experience Principle (indexed under Intelligence Governance above) are the Intelligence-specific faces; it does not alter them or any ownership boundary. The UI Architecture (UIA2, 2026-07-10, from the approved UIA1 discovery) is its visual constitution — the governing source for how THA looks: the Calm Orchard Visual Language, visual hierarchy, colour/typography/spacing/motion law, Brand Identity Architecture, Visual Trust, one-owner-per-visual-concern with retire-on-introduction, and the UI Governance Checklist. It is subordinate to the Experience Architecture: Experience governs behaviour, UI governs presentation, and Experience prevails in any conflict.
>
> The Experience Architecture was **enhanced by `EXP2` (2026-07-11)** with the **Premium Experience Principles** (§ 17) — a lens over Experience Principles 1–8 governing the *standard of craft* to which they must be done, never a rival set of principles — and the Premium Standard block of its UX Governance Checklist (§ 18). Premium in THA is defined as *the perceptible result of care taken on the household's behalf*: not ornament, density, motion, or expense, and explicitly **not exclusivity** — it is the quality every household receives, including the ones who pay nothing. Its test: *if the household would not feel the care, it is decoration; if they would feel its absence, it is craft.*
>
> **Both documents became enforceable on 2026-07-11 (`ARCH-VERIFY1`).** Until then, each declared that its checklist *"stands beside the Architecture Compliance Checklist in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`"* — while `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md` had never referenced either document, leaving both checklists unreachable by anyone actually following the workflow. The **Experience & UI Governance Compliance** block now makes them mandatory for every user-facing implementation, and the Architecture Bootstrap (STEP 2) now names them as required reading. Where the Experience Architecture is the **law** of the user-facing layer and the UI Architecture is its **visual constitution**, the Product Knowledge Registry is its **census** — the enumeration of surfaces without which Experience Principle 6 (*one canonical place for everything*) was never checkable.

> The Experience Language (`EXPLANG1`, 2026-07-15) is the governing source for **how THA must *feel***. If the Experience Architecture governs what the experience must *do* and the UI Architecture governs how it must *look*, the Experience Language governs the **feeling those two exist to produce** — THA's emotional architecture, stated as emotion so a person can check for it. It is the **emotional constitution**: subordinate to the Experience Architecture (behaviour prevails over a description of feeling, because a feeling is produced by behaviour and presentation, never decreed) and a sibling to the UI Architecture (which owns the look, including the motion vocabulary, and which the Experience Language never overrides and is never overridden by). Its purpose is to catch the gap no checklist can: a change can pass every box on both governance checklists and still feel cold, theatrical, cluttered, or slippery — because a checklist verifies that rules were followed, not that a feeling was produced. It defines the seven feelings THA must produce (calm · welcoming · effortless · intelligent · reassuring · premium · quietly memorable) *without becoming theatrical, gimmicky or distracting*; thirteen **Principles of Feeling** (each with why it exists, how users should feel, practical implications, and things to encourage/avoid); the canonical six-beat **Experience Rhythm** (Arrival → Orientation → Confidence → Action → Understanding → Completion) applied to every realm; the **Experience Review Questions** (§ 6) every implementation is evaluated against before it is complete; and the **Experience Anti-Patterns** (§ 7) THA must never adopt. It restates neither the eight Experience Principles nor the eight UI Principles — restating a rule creates a second owner of it — and it creates **no** colour, component, token, route, or runtime dependency. Its Review Questions stand beside the two checklists in the Experience & UI Governance Compliance gate; where any conflict of *rule* remains, it resolves upward to the Experience Architecture. It was **enhanced by `EXPLANG1A` (2026-07-15)** with **§ 4A — The Place Principles**: eight arrival-derived principles of feeling (The THA Promise · THA is a place · Arrival before work · Walking, not scrolling · The application shell is constant · Light has meaning · Home is not the dashboard · Premium through restraint), discovered during the ARRIVAL1 experience prototype and admitted by governance. They frame THA as a *place a household arrives in and moves through* rather than a set of pages, deepening the thirteen Principles of Feeling without renumbering or restating any of them; the enhancement extended § 6 and § 7 only where a new principle genuinely required an additional check. It was **further enhanced by `EXPLANG1B` (2026-07-15)** with **§ 3A — The Emotional Palette of THA**, discovered during the Arrival prototype reviews: the eight temperatures THA must never feel (cold · clinical · empty · silent · sterile · luxury-for-luxury's-sake · funeral-parlour calm · emotionally distant), the seven-note palette it must always feel (calm · warm · energised · thoughtful · comforting · decisive · curious), the orchard fixed as *life* (bright, growing, optimistic — never gloomy, misty, or melancholy), the one-sentence feel (*"a warm, lived-in home where someone has already thought about dinner"*), and the governing principle **"Calm must never become lifeless"** — closing the quiet counterfeit of calm that § 3 had left unnamed, with § 6 and § 7 again extended only where the palette genuinely required it.

> The **Experience Blueprint** (`EXPBLUE1`, 2026-07-15) is the **unifying blueprint of Experience Governance** — the one place where the whole of THA's experience is legible at once. The three documents above divide one experience cleanly (*do* · *look* · *feel*), and each is deliberately silent on the others' questions — which meant no single document held the *vision they all serve*. The Blueprint holds it: the Vision (*"a warm, lived-in home where someone has already thought about dinner"*), the map connecting the three documents, and the **Design North Star**. It **restates no rule from any of them** — it cites owners throughout — and is the canonical owner only of what none of them owns: the **One Home, Many Places** spatial concept (the shell as the walls, each domain a room differentiated by purpose, light, material, and one sign of life — never by its own architecture, palette, or theme); the **Orchard Exposure Scale** (E0–E3, a governed per-domain window size obeying "exposure is inversely proportional to functional density"); the house's **light** (one sun, one morning, rooms differing by exposure never by hour) and **material** direction (three grounds; the middle ground — the ground plane — as the only layer that varies by domain); the **Living Details** constitution and library (at most one per domain, data-borne or dead, honest in absence, still); the Companion's **place in the house** (the friend at the counter — a presence, not a room); the **Blueprint Checks** (§ 15.2) that stand beside the three existing gates; and the **spatial anti-patterns** (the theme park, the costume, wallpaper, the second sun). It graduated these concepts from the arrival/place discoveries (`ARRIVAL1`, `EXP2`–`EXP5`) — which remain point-in-time history — while changing **no visual law**: the depth/light vocabulary still enters the UI Architecture only by the governed amendment EXP4/EXP5 specified, exposure values only as tokens by admission, and each Living Detail one at a time. It is subordinate to the Experience Architecture (which prevails in any conflict) and a sibling to the UI Architecture and Experience Language, owning its own question — *what is the vision, and what is the place?* — and never theirs. It was **extended by `EXPBLUE2` (2026-07-15)** with the refined governing vision **"A modern home in an ancient orchard"** (§ 1.4) — the intentional contrast of a *contemporary* home (clean architecture, natural light and materials, calm uncluttered surfaces, quiet confidence) within a *timeless* orchard (mature, in season, always present, seen through windows never walked into), which reads as THA itself: *modern intelligence in the service of timeless household values*; the **Technology Principle** (§ 1.5) — *"technology should quietly disappear; the household should always feel present"* — technology supports the experience and never becomes it; the **digital-home reading of the rooms** (§ 4.1: THA is the household's digital home, the Planner its planning table, the Cookbook its living cookbook, the Pantry its pantry, Shopping preparing to leave the house — each domain a different room within one home); the **ancient orchard** (§ 6.0: timeless, mature, always present); a **mandatory Experience Test** (§ 15.3, wired into the Experience & UI Governance Compliance gate) — every screen must answer *which room is this · how should someone feel here · what is the one thing this room helps them do*; and the **refined Design North Star headline** (§ 17: *"The Healthy Apples is a modern home in an ancient orchard, where technology quietly supports timeless family life."*). The extension **restates no rule** — each addition cites its owner (the light of § 7, the materials of § 8, the map of § 5.1, the orchard laws of § 6.1–§ 6.2, honest presence in Experience Architecture § 3) and only names the vision they already serve; it adds no visual value, no new document, and leaves the three sibling documents byte-untouched.

> The **Orchard House Design Blueprint** (`OHDB1`, 2026-07-15) is the **design-language face of the Experience Blueprint** — the coherent visual design identity of the house the Blueprint draws. The Blueprint says what THA *is* (*"a modern home in an ancient orchard"*) and owns the place — the rooms, the orchard, the light, the materials, the signs of life; it does not say, in one voice, **what that house looks and feels like as a piece of design**: its architectural character, its interior philosophy, why its restraint is a design decision, and how a designer a decade from now keeps building the *same house*. This document holds that. It **restates no rule** — every already-owned concern is cited (the light of Blueprint § 7 and UIA § 7; the materials of Blueprint § 8; the rooms of Blueprint § 5.1; colour and type of UIA § 7/§ 8; the seasonal one-morning law of Blueprint § 6.0/§ 6.1; the feeling of Experience Language § 3/§ 3A/§ 5) — and is the canonical owner only of what none of them owns: the house's **architectural character** as a design stance (structural honesty · glazed toward the orchard · natural light and material · uncluttered planes · quiet confidence); the **interior design philosophy** (composed emptiness, everything present is meant, the household's own life as the only ornament); the **per-room design reading** (§ 13 — Home · Planner · Cookbook · Pantry · Shopping · Diary · Profile · Companion · Admin, each bound into one coherent room from attributes cited to their owners); the **"Timeless, not fashionable"** doctrine (§ 14 — why THA refuses temporary UI trends and builds an enduring identity, with the test to apply at the moment of a decision); the **Design Manifesto** (§ 15 — ten lines to steer THA's design for the next decade); and the **Design Character Check** (§ 16.2), its one addition to the Experience & UI Governance Compliance gate, which duplicates neither the Experience Test nor the Blueprint Checks. It changes **no visual law** and jumps **no governance path** — its vivid descriptions of light, depth, and material ship only through the path the Blueprint already fixed (the UIA § 4 amendment, tokens by admission, one Living Detail at a time). It is subordinate to the Experience Blueprint (and, through it, the Experience Architecture, which prevails in any conflict) and a non-overriding sibling of the UI Architecture (the binding look) and the Experience Language (the feeling), owning its own question — *what is the house's design character, and how does it stay one house for a decade?* — and never theirs.

> The **Orchard Living Book** (`OLB1`, 2026-07-15) is the **lived, felt account of the house** — the one document written to be *read*, not checked. The five governing documents above are all written to be *enforced*: the Experience Architecture, UI Architecture and Experience Language state the *do* · *look* · *feel* as rules; the Experience Blueprint draws the house and the Orchard House Design Blueprint gives it a design character. None of them tells a newcomer, in plain language they can simply recognise, **what it is like to live inside The Healthy Apples** — so that a designer or developer knows the feeling they are protecting before they read a single rule. The Living Book holds that. It is **not architecture, not UI, not a design system**: it adds **no rule, no gate, and no check**, and is the canonical owner only of what none of them owns — the *narrative* of dwelling in the house across the moments of an ordinary life. It is organised as sixteen chapters (First Light · The View Into The Orchard · Tea Before The Day Begins · The Family Planning Table · The Living Cookbook · The Pantry Shelves · Preparing To Leave For The Shop · Returning Home · Cooking Together · Quiet Moments · Rain Against The Glass · Spring · Summer · Autumn · Winter · Evening In The Orchard), each written as design prose moving through where you are, what is happening, the atmosphere, the light, the orchard, the materials, and how the Companion keeps company — closing on what must **never** happen and the feeling the household should be left holding — and it ends with **The Orchard Promise**, a single statement of what every future THA experience must protect. It **restates no rule** — where a chapter touches a governed concern it is describing what that rule already produces and cites the owner once — and it holds hard to the house's one-morning, one-season law (Experience Blueprint § 6, § 7; Orchard House Design Blueprint § 11): the chapters on rain, the four seasons, and evening are about the *household's* weather, seasons and evening — the life they bring through the door — never the house's light or the orchard's calendar, both of which stay constant so the family's changing life has something unchanging to return to. The feeling it describes is protected by the existing governance gates, the Blueprint Checks, the Experience Test and the Design Character Check — it adds none of its own. It is subordinate to the Experience Architecture (which prevails in any conflict) and a non-overriding sibling of the UI Architecture, the Experience Language, and the two blueprints it extends, owning its own question — *what is it like to live here?* — and never theirs.

> The **Kept Room Translation** (`TRANSLATION1`, 2026-07-15) is the **governing bridge between the Orchard House architecture and the software interface** — the one place that translates each architectural characteristic of the house into the interface it becomes, and the interface it must never become. The five documents above describe the house completely, but in the language of *architecture and feeling* — light, oak, thresholds, morning, patina; software is built in the language of *behaviour, layout, spacing, type, motion, interaction*, and between those two languages there had never been a single deliberate translation. That gap is exactly where a soul is lost: a team can read every rule, pass every gate, and still build a "warm oak counter" as a brown rectangle or a "threshold" as a splash screen, because no document sat between the metaphor and the mechanism and said, plainly, *this becomes this, and never that.* This document is that bridge, and is the canonical owner only of what none of the others owns: the **translation schema** (§ 3, the nine facets every characteristic is translated across — architectural meaning · emotional purpose · behaviour · layout · spacing · typography · motion · interaction · what must never happen); the **twenty characteristic translations** (§ 4 — Light · Space · Thresholds · Rooms · Windows · Orchard views · the Kitchen island · Oak · Stone · Linen · Handmade ceramics · Shelving · Quiet corners · Morning rhythm · Patina · Permanence · Calm · Warmth · Household presence · Companion presence, each translated as a *feeling produced by light and material* and never as a literal picture); the **governing UI translation principles** (§ 5 — space before decoration · light before colour · materials before effects · depth before shadows · calm before information · household before technology · people before data · truth before charm · restraint before expression, closing on *technology should quietly disappear* and *the household should always feel present*), the ordering laws that settle any translation the table does not; and the **complete visual language stated whole** (§ 6), the house as one interface identity described without reference to any individual screen. It takes **The Kept Room** (ORCHARD3 § 5 — warm minimalism, *modern bones, warm skin*) as the sensibility being translated, on the mission's instruction. It **restates no rule** (every facet cites its owner), **sets no value** (no colour, token, size, duration, or pixel), **adds no gate** (the existing gates — the Experience Test, the Blueprint Checks, the Design Character Check, and the UI/UX checklists — verify every translation, read against this dictionary), and **jumps no governance path**: its material, light, and depth translations ship only through the path the Experience Blueprint fixed (§ 2.4 there — the Kept Room's graduation by amendment, the UIA § 4 amendment, tokens by admission, one Living Detail at a time), and it inherits the Blueprint's four open items without closing any. It is subordinate to the Experience Blueprint (and, through it, the Experience Architecture, which prevails in any conflict) and a non-overriding sibling of the UI Architecture (the binding look), the Experience Language (the feeling), and the two blueprints and the living book it translates, owning its own question — *how does each architectural characteristic become interface, and what must it never become?* — and never theirs.

## Compliance

Every significant implementation must pass the **Architecture Compliance Checklist** in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`.

Every **AI-related** implementation must additionally pass the **AI ARCHITECTURE COMPLIANCE** block in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`, which confirms it uses the canonical Intelligence Platform, the Capability Registry, and the Intent Engine; reuses existing business services; creates no second assistant; duplicates no conversation state; uses registered capabilities only with permission-aware access; and produces honest gaps rather than fabricated knowledge.

Every **user-facing** implementation must additionally pass two blocks in `ENGINEERING_WORKFLOW.md`:

- **EXPERIENCE & UI GOVERNANCE COMPLIANCE** — which requires the **UX Governance Checklist** (`THA_EXPERIENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md` § 18, including its Premium Standard block) and the **UI Governance Checklist** (`THA_UI_ARCHITECTURE.md` § 18) to be completed in full, with any conflict between them resolved in the Experience Architecture's favour.
- **PRODUCT REGISTRY COMPLIANCE** — which requires every affected Product Knowledge Registry entry to be created, updated, or retired **in the same change** (`PLATFORM_KNOWLEDGE_COMPLETION_ARCHITECTURE.md` Rule KC15).

Every implementation that adds, changes, or retires a **client-side building block** (a component, hook, token, utility class, or shared pattern) must additionally pass:

- **ADOPTION REGISTER COMPLIANCE** — which requires every owner created, adopted, or retired to be recorded in the **Platform Experience Adoption Register** (`docs/implementation/ux/ADOPTION_REGISTER.md`) **in the same change**, and `npm run adoption:check` to pass (`THA_UI_ARCHITECTURE.md` § 17). *Added under `PX1-W5` (2026-07-13).* UIA § 17 has always mandated this register — *"authored-but-unadopted must be impossible to hide"* — and for as long as it did not exist, that sentence was the only rule in the governing architecture with **no owner and no way to check it**. `PX1` found the consequence: `hover-elevate` consumed by every control and defined nowhere; `prefersReducedMotion()` with zero consumers; `PageHeader.tsx`, a complete unadopted successor to `WorkspaceHeader`, sitting live-looking in the tree. The register is operational, not architectural: it adds no law, and makes the existing law checkable.

**If any check fails: STOP, explain why, do not continue.**
