## 0. Mandate

`LIVINGHOME2` established **what** Environmental Dressing may be — a governed, claim-free, non-personalised hospitality layer that marks the quiet passage of the year (its rules ED1–ED12). It deliberately did **not** fix **how a dressing object must look**. Its own User Acceptance Evidence recorded the gap in one line:

> *"the rendering medium of dressing items … inherits `EXP3` Verdict 3's open illustration question unchanged and adds no position to it."* (`LIVINGHOME2` § 11, User Acceptance Evidence.)

This document closes that gap **as a standard, not as an approval.** It defines the one coherent visual and material language against which every future dressing object — the first bowl of apples, and everything admitted after it — must be judged before it is admitted. A dressing object that is lawful under ED1–ED12 but does not meet this Constitution is **refused**; a dressing object cannot be admitted on the strength of its behaviour alone.

> **The Living Home Design Constitution is the design-language face of `LIVINGHOME2`** — exactly as the Orchard House Design Blueprint (`OHDB1`) is the design-language face of the Experience Blueprint. `LIVINGHOME2` says what the layer *is and may do*; this document says what an object in it must *look and feel like* to belong in this house.

It exists so that a designer admitting the tenth dressing object a decade from now builds the **same house** as the one who admits the first — and so the pressure that always returns ("make it warmer", "make it richer", "make it pop") meets a written standard instead of taste.

## 2. The single standard, in one paragraph

Environmental Dressing must feel **warm, calm, natural, crafted, lived-with, restrained, and consistent with the existing house** — the seven-note feeling the house already owns (`EXPLANG1B`'s palette, cited). It must **never** feel **glossy, synthetic, generic, promotional, themed, gamified, ornamental for its own sake, like stock imagery, like a dashboard decoration, or like fabricated household activity.** These two lists are the whole Constitution compressed; every section below is one of them made checkable for one facet of an object. When a facet's rule and these two lists ever seem to disagree, **these two lists win**, because they are what the house is for.

---

## 3. Hospitality purpose

Every dressing object exists for **hospitality and nothing else** — welcome, comfort, care, the quiet passage of the year (ED8, cited). This Constitution adds the *visual* form of that rule: an object earns admission only if a person arriving tired at the end of a day would feel its warmth **without being able to name why**. Hospitality here is *felt, not performed*: the object does its work by being quietly, believably present, the way a real home is warm before its family walks in. *"It looks nice"* is refused at ED8; *"it makes the room feel richer/busier/more designed"* is refused here. The test is **subtraction**: if removing the object cools the room slightly, it was hospitality; if removing it changes nothing a person would feel, it was decoration and never belonged.

---

## 4. Visual restraint

Restraint is the whole craft (ED7, cited). The visual standard:

- **A home with three well-chosen touches is warm; a home with thirty is a shop window** (`LIVINGHOME2` ED7, cited). The register's size is itself a governed fact; **removing** a dressing object is as legitimate an act of curation as adding one (`LIVINGHOME2` § 9.9).
- Every object sits **below the emphasis budget** (Blueprint § 12.1.4, cited) and **within the declared token ceiling, never tuned per surface** (UIA § 15, cited). A dressing object is never the loudest thing in a room, never competes with the room's data for the eye, and never draws attention to itself.
- The governing order is the Kept Room Translation's ordering law (`TRANSLATION1` § 5, cited): **space before decoration · light before colour · materials before effects · restraint before expression.** A dressing object is admitted only if the room would still be composed and calm *with it present* — it must read as **more room, not more stuff.**
- The house's interior philosophy is **composed emptiness — everything present is meant, and the household's own life is the only ornament** (`OHDB1` § interior philosophy, cited). A dressing object is the rare exception the household-life-as-ornament rule tolerates precisely because it claims nothing; it must therefore be quieter than any Living Detail, never richer.

## 5. Material palette

A dressing object is built from the **materials the house is already made of** — it introduces no new material vocabulary. The house's materials are owned by the Blueprint (§ 8) and translated by the Kept Room Translation (`TRANSLATION1` § 4): **oak (warm timber), stone, linen, handmade ceramics, woven basketry, paper** — natural, honest, matte, aged by use. The standard:

- **Only the house's own materials.** Wood is warm timber with visible grain; textiles are linen and wool with a real weave; vessels are hand-thrown ceramic, not machined. Nothing enters that the house does not already contain.
- **Natural over manufactured, always.** Glass, polished metal, plastic, chrome, acrylic, high-gloss lacquer and any material that reads as *factory-made* are refused — they carry the synthetic and glossy temperatures the house must never feel (`EXPLANG1B`, cited).
- **Material honesty** (`OHDB1` structural-honesty stance, cited): a material is what it appears to be. A dressing object never fakes a material (printed "wood grain", plastic pretending to be ceramic); the Kept Room Translation's law **materials before effects** (`TRANSLATION1` § 5) governs — warmth comes from the material itself, never from a filter over it.
- **Materials are feelings, never pictures** (`TRANSLATION1`, cited): *"'Oak' does not become a wood-grain texture; it becomes a warm, honest working surface. The moment a translation becomes a literal image of the architectural thing, it has become the theme park."* A dressing object built from the house's materials must read as *made of* them, never as a *picture of* them — imperfection is carried by the honest material, *never by faux distress* (`TRANSLATION1`, cited).
- **Air is a material** (Blueprint § 8, cited): generous space around a dressing object is part of it. An object is never crowded onto a surface to fill it; the breathing space is the setting.
- The apple — the home's own fruit and standing signature (`LIVINGHOME2` § 5) — is a **material of this house** and the canonical reference object; new materials are admitted only where they sit believably beside oak, linen and ceramic.

*(Exact colour and token values for any material stay UIA's; see § 8. This section governs which materials may appear, not their hex.)*

---

## 6. Texture and surface language

- **Matte, never glossy.** Every surface is low-sheen. Specular highlights, wet looks, mirror finishes and lacquered shine are refused — gloss is the first tell of the synthetic, promotional, stock-image register the house must never enter (§ 2).
- **Tactile and real.** Surfaces carry the fine grain of the real material — the tooth of linen, the throw-marks of ceramic, the grain of oak, the give of wool. Texture is *felt at a glance*, never rendered as a decorative pattern applied on top (that is **effects before materials**, refused at `TRANSLATION1` § 5).
- **Depth from light and material, never from shadow tricks** (`TRANSLATION1` § 5 *depth before shadows*, cited): an object reads as solid because it is lit like the room, not because a drop-shadow was drawn under it.
- Surface language is **quiet**: no high-contrast surface detail that pulls the eye, no ornamental surface treatment. A surface earns its place by being believable, not by being interesting.

---

## 7. Craftsmanship

The house is drawn **by its own hand** — *modern bones, warm skin* (`TRANSLATION1`, cited). Dressing objects inherit that standard:

- **Hand-made, not manufactured.** Every object should read as something a person made or chose with care — thrown, woven, turned, gathered — never as a catalogue product or a rendered 3-D asset. This is the premium standard the house already holds: *the perceptible result of care taken on the household's behalf* (`EXP2` Premium Principles, cited) — **if the household would feel its absence, it is craft; if they would not feel the care, it is decoration** (cited).
- **Consistency over novelty** (`HOMEOWNER1` principle, cited): every admitted object is drawn in the same hand as those before it, so the register reads as one home furnished over time, not a collection of unrelated assets.
- Craftsmanship is **evidence of care, never a display of skill.** An object that shows off its own rendering — virtuoso detail, technical flourish — has become ornamental for its own sake (§ 2) and is refused.

---

## 8. Realism and illustration treatment

`EXP3` Verdict 3 left the **rendering medium** of Living Home assets a *genuinely open* owner decision — *"may that truth be rendered as commissioned still-life illustration, or only in the product's existing media (content photography, type, material tokens)?"* — and recorded that the **default, lawful today with no approval needed, is existing media**, while the **illustrated track requires the owner's explicit approval recorded at the first admission that uses it** (`EXP3` Verdict 3, cited). This Constitution **does not resolve that verdict** — doing so would be legislating the medium from below (`EXP3`, cited). It states the **quality standard that binds whichever medium the Home Owner chooses**, and routes the medium choice to § 20.

**The medium, unresolved and correctly so:**

- **Default (no approval needed): the house's existing media** — the material tokens, honest content photography (*"real food in real light … no stock-photo tone"* — UIA § 10, cited), and type the house already uses. An object buildable in existing media needs no medium approval, only admission against this Constitution.
- **Illustrated track (approval required): a single named illustration style** — *"One hand: a single, named illustration style in Calm Orchard's palette — warm, simple, unhurried. Illustration decorates meaning; it never substitutes for content or fakes it"* (UIA § 10, cited). This track is lawful **only** with the Home Owner's approval recorded at the first admission that uses it (`EXP3` Verdict 3; § 20).

**The quality standard, binding on either medium:**

- **Never photographic-as-stock.** Anything that reads as stock imagery — glossy, staged, promotional, generic — carries temperatures the house must never feel (§ 2) and is refused. Where content photography is used, it is *honest, natural, appetising, real food in real light* (UIA § 10, cited), never stock tone.
- **Never cartoon / never gamified.** Flat, high-saturation, character-styled or "app-illustration" treatments read as gamified and synthetic (§ 2) and belong to a louder product. Refused.
- **Never a claim.** No object may render at a fidelity or specificity that a household could read as *their* real, particular thing (their fruit, their bowl) — that edges out of dressing and into a claim (ED3, cited).
- **Coherence is the binding rule, whatever the medium.** Every dressing object, the orchard backdrop, and the Living Details must read as **drawn by the same hand in the same house** (the *"drawn by the house's own hand"* discipline, EXP3 § 4.1, cited). A single object in a divergent medium or style is a defect no matter how good it is alone; the register is **one hand** (UIA § 10, cited).

The verdict on medium is the Home Owner's, made against this standard and recorded at first admission (§ 20); this document supplies the criteria, not the verdict.

## 9. Object scale and proportion

- **Domestic, believable scale.** A dressing object is sized as the real object would sit in a real room — a bowl on a counter, a blanket over a chair-arm. It never dominates a region and never shrinks to a decorative motif.
- **Subordinate to the room's subject, always** (ED4, § 5.1, cited): where a dressing object and the room's data share the eye, the object is the smaller, quieter presence. It occupies the room's **margins and surfaces**, never its centre of information.
- **Proportion is honest.** Objects relate to each other and to the room at true relative scale; nothing is enlarged for emphasis or miniaturised for cuteness (cuteness is a gamified temperature, refused at § 2).
- Scale is **fixed within a registered state** and never responsive to engagement, density, or attention (ED6/ED7, cited) — an object is never made bigger to be noticed.

---

## 10. Colour relationship to the Living Home

- **The dressing object borrows the house's palette; it never brings its own.** Every colour value stays UIA's (§ 1). A dressing object is admitted only in tones that already live in the house — the warm neutrals of oak and linen, the muted greens of the orchard, the soft daylight of the one morning.
- **Warm, natural, low-saturation.** The house must feel *warm* and never *cold, clinical, or sterile* (`EXPLANG1B` temperatures, cited). Dressing colour is muted and earthy; high-saturation, neon, candy, or "brand-pop" colour is refused as promotional and gamified (§ 2).
- **The orchard is life** (`EXPLANG1B`, cited): where a dressing object carries the orchard's own colours (the apple's red-green, a flower's bloom), those are the orchard's living tones — bright but never garish, optimistic but never loud.
- **Colour never encodes information.** A dressing object's colour means nothing — no status, no season *signal*, no warmth-of-relationship (ED4, cited). Seasonal colour shifts belong to the *object* (autumn's warmer objects), never to the *house's light or palette*, which hold their one morning absolutely (ED5; the year enters through the door as objects, never through the window as weather).
- **Calm must never become lifeless** (`EXPLANG1B` governing principle, cited): restraint of colour is not drabness. A dressing object may be quietly, warmly coloured — it must simply never be *loud*.

---

## 11. Ageing, imperfection and patina

- **Lived-with, not new.** The house is warm because it is *lived in*; patina is one of its owned characteristics (`TRANSLATION1` § 4 Patina, cited). A dressing object should read as an object a household has had and used — softened edges, a gentle wear, the honest imperfection of the hand-made — never showroom-fresh, never sealed-in-plastic new.
- **Imperfection is warmth, never damage.** Patina means the quiet evidence of care and use, not dirt, breakage, decay, or neglect. An object is *cared-for and worn*, never *spoiled*.
- **Restraint applies to ageing too.** Patina is a whisper, not a costume — heavy distressing, "rustic" affectation, or performed shabbiness is a theme (§ 2) and is refused. The object is lived-with, not styled to look lived-with.
- Patina is **fixed within a registered state**; an object does not visibly age over time or with use (that would be motion/engagement machinery, refused at ED6/ED7).

---

## 12. Lighting treatment

- **One morning, borrowed — never its own.** The house has **one sun, one direction, one hour — the morning sun sits upper-left, forever, in every room** (Blueprint § 7, cited); *"every room in this house is a morning room"* and *"rooms differ by exposure, never by hour"* (Blueprint § 7; GEA10 / HT13, cited). A dressing object is lit **by that one existing light**: its shadow agrees with every other shadow in the house, it carries no light source of its own, casts no independent light, and never encodes time of day or season in its lighting (ED5/ED6, cited).
- **Light carries five meanings and no others — welcome, warmth, calm, clarity, optimism** (Blueprint § 7, cited); a dressing object's lighting may serve those and never emphasis, drama, or signal.
- **Soft, warm, natural daylight.** Lighting is the gentle morning light the house already owns — no hard studio key light, no rim/spotlight, no glow, no bloom, no lens effects. Dramatic or theatrical lighting is refused as promotional/themed (§ 2).
- **Depth before shadows** (`TRANSLATION1` § 5, cited): an object sits in the room through consistent, gentle light and contact with its surface, not through a heavy or stylised drop-shadow. Shadows are soft, short, and consistent with the one morning; no object floats and none is dramatically grounded.
- **Light never becomes a channel of meaning** (GEA10, cited): no object is lit to draw the eye, signal importance, or mark an occasion. If light is doing work other than making the object believable, it is refused.

## 13. Asset composition and transparency

- **Decorative-declared, in the `orchard-backdrop.tsx` manner** (`LIVINGHOME2` ED7, EXP3 § 11, cited): every dressing asset is composed **`aria-hidden`, with empty `alt`, and `pointer-events-none`** — it is environment, not content, and is announced to no assistive technology as information.
- **Still, always.** The asset is a single still image — no motion, no video, no animated format, no sprite sequence, no interactive state (ED6/ED7, cited).
- **Transparent, region-fitting composition.** A dressing asset composes **inside a region the house already commits** (EXP3 § 5 band regions; `LIVINGHOME2` § 7.1, cited) — it adds no region and no layout. It is delivered as a clean-edged, transparent-background asset that sits *within* the room's existing surface, never as a rectangular card, framed panel, or bounded tile (a frame or card is a dashboard-decoration tell, refused at § 2).
- **Zero-byte where there is no view** (EXP3 § 10; `LIVINGHOME2` § 10.3, cited): rooms without a committed region load and render **nothing** — the E1/E0 zero-byte invariant holds for dressing exactly as for the house.
- **Composition never covers absence** (`LIVINGHOME2` § 6, cited): a dressing asset is never positioned to fill or animate an empty-data surface so the product looks alive. It composes *around* the household's honest state, never *instead of* it.

---

## 14. Seasonal continuity

- **One home across the year.** The seasonal turns (ED6, cited) vary the *objects on the surfaces*, never the house, its light, or its palette. Across every season the home must remain recognisably **the same home** — same materials, same hand, same light, same restraint. A season is a change of *what is set out*, not a change of *what the house is*.
- **A shared visual family.** Spring flowers, an autumn blanket, harvest pumpkins and the year-round bowl of apples must read as belonging to **one collection furnished by one household over years** — not as separate seasonal "packs" or themed sets (a pack is refused at ED10; a theme at § 2).
- **Continuity of restraint.** Every season holds the same emphasis budget and the same quiet. No season is louder, richer, or more decorated than another; the year turns *slowly and evenly*, never building to a crescendo (no seasonal escalation — ED6/ED9, cited).

---

## 15. Transition between seasonal states

- **The year turns between reviewed, registered states — never as an animation of the turn** (`LIVINGHOME2` § 7.1, ED6, cited). A dressing object is present in a season or it is not; there is no dissolve, morph, fade-through, or motion between states, and no "watch the seasons change" moment.
- **A handful of states, each admitted deliberately** (ED6, cited): the year is a small number of registered still states (`spring · summer · autumn · winter · year-round`), each resolved on read from Domain 11, each byte-constant within itself. The change a household perceives is simply that on a later visit, different objects are quietly set out.
- **No transition may imply the house changed.** Because states are still and discrete, a season change never reads as the *house* transforming — only as the home having been re-set, the way a real home changes with the year while its walls stay the same. The transition is felt *between visits*, never performed *within one*.

---

## 16. Accessibility and legibility

- **Invisible to assistive technology, by construction** (§ 13): dressing carries zero information, so it is `aria-hidden` with empty `alt` and is never announced. Removing every dressing object loses **no** information, capability, or state for any user (ED4, cited) — so nothing a dressing object shows is ever the *only* way to perceive something.
- **Never degrades the legibility of the room's data.** A dressing object may never reduce the contrast, spacing, or clarity of any text, control, or datum in the room. Where an object would sit behind or beside content, the content's legibility standard (UIA, cited) is untouched and wins; the object yields (ED4/§ 5.1).
- **Contrast and motion safety inherited, not re-owned:** dressing introduces no motion (§ 13), so no `prefers-reduced-motion` concern arises; all colour-contrast law for surrounding content remains UIA's. Dressing adds no new interactive target, no focus stop, and no keyboard path — there is nothing to tab to.
- **Legibility of the object itself** matters only insofar as it must read *unmistakably as the home's warmth and never as the room's data* (ED3, § 17) — an ambiguous object that a household could misread as their information fails on legibility grounds even if it is beautiful.

---

## 17. Room-placement compatibility

The placement law is `LIVINGHOME2` § 5.1's and is **cited, not re-owned**: a dressing object may not be placed where its context would make it read as the room's data — **no produce dressing in the Pantry/Larder, no book dressing in the Cookbook room, no meal-shaped dressing in the Planner.** This Constitution adds the *visual* compatibility criteria layered on top of that law:

- **An object must be visually legible as the home's warmth in the specific room it is admitted to** — the same object may be admissible in one room and refused in another purely on how it *reads there* (ED3, cited). Admission is per object **and** per placement.
- **The object must sit believably in that room's material and light.** A dressing object is admitted to a room only where it belongs in that room's ground and exposure (Blueprint Exposure Scale, cited) — it never looks imported from a different house.
- **The object must never contest the room's centre.** It occupies surfaces and margins the room's data does not need (§ 9); it is refused any placement where it would compete for the eye with the Companion, Planner, Pantry/Larder, Cookbook, Shopping, Nutrition, Diary, Stories or the Notice Engine (ED4, cited).
- **Placement causes no layout shift** (EXP3 § 9, cited): the committed region exists whether or not the object renders, so admitting or retiring an object never moves the room's content.

## 18. With-and-without review criteria

Every object is reviewed **with-and-without** (ED10, cited) — the room composed with the object present and with it absent, side by side — and admitted only if **all** of the following hold. This is the Constitution's central gate:

1. **Warmth on subtraction.** *Without* is perceptibly, quietly cooler than *with* — the object adds felt hospitality (§ 3). If *without* is indistinguishable, the object is decoration and is refused.
2. **No loss of information or clarity.** *Without* loses no information, capability, legibility, or calm (§ 16). If *without* is clearer or calmer, the object was clutter and is refused.
3. **Still one house.** *With* still reads as the same house — same materials, hand, light, restraint, palette (§ 5–§ 14). If *with* reads as busier, richer, more "designed", themed, or promotional, the object is refused.
4. **Never a claim.** *With* could not be read by a reasonable household as *THA knows this about us* (ED3, cited). If the object could read as the household's own data in that placement, it is refused (§ 17).
5. **Coherent with the register.** *With* sits in the same visual family as every already-admitted object (§ 8, § 14). A beautiful but stylistically divergent object is refused for coherence.
6. **Quiet under repetition.** The object remains unobtrusive on the hundredth viewing — nothing about it demands attention, tires, or begins to feel like a gimmick (§ 4).

The review is recorded as part of the object's admission evidence (§ 20).

---

## 19. Rejection criteria

An object is **refused** — regardless of how well-made it is — if it meets **any** of the following. These are the two lists of § 2 made operative, with the refusing standard named:

- **Feels glossy, wet, lacquered, or high-sheen** — § 6 (matte only).
- **Reads as synthetic, manufactured, plastic, or machined** — § 5 (natural materials only).
- **Reads as stock imagery, staged, or promotional photography** — § 8 (honest media only, never stock tone).
- **Reads as cartoon, flat-app-illustration, or gamified** — § 8.
- **Uses colour the house does not own, or high-saturation / brand-pop colour** — § 10.
- **Carries its own light, a studio/spotlight, glow, bloom, or dramatic shadow** — § 12.
- **Is showroom-new, or conversely damaged/decayed/performed-shabby** — § 11.
- **Is out of scale, dominates a region, or competes for the room's centre** — § 9, § 17.
- **Reads as a framed card, panel, tile, or dashboard decoration** — § 13.
- **Animates, transitions, or performs a seasonal change** — § 13, § 15.
- **Reads as themed, as a seasonal "pack", or as promotional / a channel** — § 14, ED11 (cited).
- **Could be read as a claim about the household** (their fruit, their books, their occasion) — ED3 (cited); the layer's cardinal sin.
- **Adds no felt warmth on with-and-without subtraction** — § 18.1 (ornamental for its own sake).
- **Breaks visual coherence with the register** — § 8, § 18.5.
- **Is fabricated household activity** — an object staged to imply the household did or has something (a half-drunk mug "someone left", a plate "just used"): fabricated feeling, refused at the prop ban's purpose (Blueprint § 12.1.2, cited) and ED3.

A refusal **needs no further rule** beyond these: *it does not belong in this house* is a complete verdict (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited).

## 20. Home Owner approval authority

There is **one** approval authority for every dressing object, and it is the one the canon already names: the **Home Owner** (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited). This Constitution creates **no** new authority and duplicates none.

- **The Home Owner governs feeling, not functionality** (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited). Whether a dressing object *belongs in this house* is exactly the judgement `HOMEOWNER1` was written to seat — the approvals the canon's aesthetic gates already required of "the owner" (`EXP3` Verdict 3 *awaits the owner*; every Living Detail admission terminates in an owner decision).
- **Authority is exercised through the governing documents, never around them** (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited): the Home Owner admits an object *by this Constitution's standard*, not by taste against it. A decision to admit an object that fails a criterion here is not an exception — it is an **amendment proposal to this document**, made and reviewed as an amendment, never a silent pass. **Approval can never pass what a gate fails.**
- **This Constitution supplies the standard; the Home Owner supplies the judgement.** The open `EXP3` Verdict 3 **medium** question — existing media (default, no approval) versus the illustrated track (approval required) — is decided by the Home Owner *against § 8's quality standard*, and the illustrated track's approval is **recorded at the first admission that uses it** (`EXP3` Verdict 3, cited). This document does not pre-empt that verdict; it makes it a judgement against a written standard instead of against nothing. Environmental-dressing approval is explicitly the Home Owner's seat: *"the approval those admissions require — each item admitted, refused, or retired by the Home Owner under ED8's named-purpose brief"* (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited).
- **An unrecorded approval is not an approval** (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited): every admission is recorded in the object's admission document (§ 21), with the with-and-without evidence and the criteria it met.
- **Refusal needs no rule** (`HOMEOWNER1`, cited): the Home Owner may refuse an object that meets every criterion here and still does not belong. The Constitution is a floor for admission, never a compulsion to admit.

## 21. Admission evidence required for each future object

No dressing object is admitted without an **admission document** (ED10 — *no anonymous charm*, cited) that carries **all** of the following as evidence. This is the checklist every future workstream (ED4 onward) satisfies before an object may enter the register:

1. **Identity** — the object's id and register row, with its Domain-11 season key and (if celebration-gated) its `LIVINGHOME2` § 7.2 permission binding.
2. **Named hospitality purpose** (ED8, cited) — the specific welcome/comfort/care/year's-passage the object extends, in the words of § 3; *"it looks nice"* is not evidence.
3. **Material and craft statement** — which of the house's materials the object is built from (§ 5), and how it satisfies the hand-made / matte / lived-with standard (§ 6, § 7, § 11).
4. **Medium conformance** — the medium the object uses (existing media by default; the illustrated track only with the Home Owner's approval recorded here, per `EXP3` Verdict 3), and evidence it meets § 8's quality standard — never stock-photographic, never cartoon, never a claim, and coherent with the register's one hand.
5. **Scale, colour and lighting conformance** — evidence the object is domestically scaled (§ 9), uses only the house's owned palette (§ 10), and is lit by the one morning with no light of its own (§ 12).
6. **Composition conformance** — evidence the asset is still, `aria-hidden`, empty-`alt`, `pointer-events-none`, transparent, region-fitting, and adds no layout (§ 13, § 16).
7. **Placement statement** — the committed region(s) the object is admitted to, the rooms it is refused in (§ 17, `LIVINGHOME2` § 5.1), and why it reads as the home's warmth and never the room's data in each admitted placement.
8. **With-and-without review** (§ 18) — the recorded side-by-side judgement against all six criteria, including the subtraction-warmth and no-loss-of-clarity findings.
9. **Rejection-criteria clearance** — an explicit statement that the object trips none of § 19.
10. **Home Owner approval, recorded** (§ 20) — the dated, recorded admission decision; without it, the object is not admitted.
11. **Checksum and register discipline** — the object's checksum, recomputed in the same commit that admits it (`LIVINGHOME2` § 10.3, EXP3 § 4.4, cited), so admission is always a deliberate, reviewable act.

An object missing **any** item is not admitted. The evidence lives with the object's admission document and is what the verifier's admission-doc-existence check (ED2 `dressingChecks()` D5, cited) resolves against.
