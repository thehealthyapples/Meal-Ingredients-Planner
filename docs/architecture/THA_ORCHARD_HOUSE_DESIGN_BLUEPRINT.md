It says what the house is. It does not say, in one coherent voice, **what that house looks and feels like as a piece of design** — its architectural character, the philosophy of its interior, why its restraint is a design decision and not an absence, and how a designer a decade from now keeps building the *same house* rather than a fashionable copy of it. ## 1. THE DESIGN VISION

### 1.1 The house, said as design

The governing vision is owned by the Experience Blueprint and quoted here once because the whole of this document serves it:

> **The Healthy Apples is a modern home in an ancient orchard.** *(Experience Blueprint § 1.4.)*

This document's single job is to make that sentence **legible as design** — to answer the question a designer actually asks at the surface: *what does a modern home in an ancient orchard look like, and how do I build one more room of it without breaking the house?*

The answer, in one line, is the design vision this document owns:

> **The Orchard House is designed the way a fine modern home is built beside something old and loved: with structural honesty, natural light and materials, uncluttered surfaces, and the quiet confidence to leave the orchard alone.**

Every design decision in THA is a decision about that house. The intelligence is the modern architecture — clean, current, quietly capable. The family eating well together is the ancient orchard — the timeless thing the architecture is *for* (Experience Blueprint § 1.4). The design language exists to keep the first in service of the second, forever.

### 1.2 The design principle beneath every decision

The Blueprint's Technology Principle — *"technology should quietly disappear; the household should always feel present"* (§ 1.5) — has a direct design consequence, which this document owns as its first law of craft:

> **Design should quietly disappear too. The best surface in THA is one the household feels and cannot name.**

A surface that shows off its own design — its density, its cleverness, its of-the-moment styling — has made the *design* the subject and pushed the family into the background, exactly as a screen that shows off its technology does. In the Orchard House, the design is felt as calm, warmth, and ease; it is never noticed as design. This is the same truth the Design North Star closes with — *noticed the product not at all* (Experience Blueprint § 17) — read here as a standard for the visual craft itself.

## 3. THE ARCHITECTURAL STYLE OF THE HOME

*(Owned here — the design character of the house the Experience Blueprint draws. The **placement** of windows, the **exposure** of the orchard, and the **rule** that the shell is constant are the Blueprint's, cited throughout; the **architectural character** is this document's.)*

The Orchard House is built in one style, and every room is unmistakably of it. Named as a design stance, the style has five characteristics:

1. **Structural honesty.** The house shows how it is made and hides nothing structural behind decoration. A surface is what it appears to be — a real working plane, not a picture of one (Experience Blueprint § 16, *the theme park*; Visual Trust, UIA § 14). Nothing is faux, skeuomorphic, or dressed up to seem more than it is. The confidence of the house is that it does not need to pretend.

2. **Glazed toward the orchard.** The defining architectural gesture is the window. The house is oriented outward — every room *faces* the orchard rather than containing it — and how much glazing a room carries is set by its purpose, not its taste (the Orchard Exposure Scale, Experience Blueprint § 6.2; the map, § 5.1). This is the single most recognisable line of the architecture: **a THA room is a working surface with a view held at exactly the right size, never a working surface with a landscape pasted behind it.**

3. **Natural light and natural material.** The house is lit by one morning sun and built from a small palette of honest, warm materials (Experience Blueprint § 7, § 8; the Calm Orchard visual language, UIA § 4). There is no artificial light in this house, no synthetic gloss, no chrome — the modernity is the *cleanness of the natural*, not the coldness of the technological.

4. **Uncluttered planes.** Surfaces are calm and generous; air is a building material, not leftover space (Experience Blueprint § 8.2, *air is a material*; UI spacing, UIA § 9). The modern home is uncluttered on principle: what is present is meant, and nothing else is there.

5. **Quiet confidence.** The architecture never raises its voice. It does not announce its cleverness, its brand, or its newness. It is the confidence of a well-made modern home standing beside an old orchard — sure enough of itself to be plain (Experience Language Principle H, *premium through restraint*; § 3A, the never-cold, never-clinical palette).

**The house is contemporary in form and timeless in feeling** (Experience Blueprint § 1.4). Its *form* may be as modern as the work of a room requires — a dense working bench is a modern working bench. Its *feeling* is the timeless one the orchard stands for. Where the two seem to pull apart, the feeling wins, because the feeling is what the household came home for.

## 4. INTERIOR DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

*(Owned here — how the rooms are furnished, in principle. The **material rule** is the Experience Blueprint's (§ 8) and the UI Architecture's; the **furnishing philosophy** is this document's.)*

If § 3 is the architecture, this is the interior: how a room of the Orchard House is composed once its walls, windows, and light are given.

- **Composed emptiness, never bare emptiness.** The resting state of every room is generous, calm space — but it is *composed*, the deliberate quiet of a room someone keeps, not the emptiness of a room no one uses. The distinction is the whole of the interior philosophy: emptiness that feels *tended* is calm; emptiness that feels *abandoned* is cold, and cold is forbidden (Experience Language § 3A.4, *calm must never become lifeless*; § 3A.1, the never-feelings).
- **Everything present is meant.** A room holds what its purpose needs and nothing it does not (Experience Blueprint § 4.1, the verb of each room; Experience Architecture Principle 4, one primary action). Furnishing by subtraction is the default: the question is never *what could go here* but *what does this room's one job require*, and the answer furnishes it.
- **The household's own life is the only ornament.** The Orchard House is decorated by exactly one thing — evidence that this family lives here — and never by applied charm. That evidence is the Living Detail: at most one per room, always rendering something *true* from a canonical owner, absent when the data is silent (Experience Blueprint § 12). A painted prop — drawn fruit, fake steam, decorative crumbs, worn edges — is forbidden by construction (§ 12.1, *data-borne or dead*). **The house is warm because it is lived in, not because it was dressed.**
- **The furniture is shared; the room is particular.** Every room furnishes itself from the *same* canonical component owners, token system, type scale, and motion vocabulary (Experience Blueprint § 5.2). A room is a *use* of the shared furniture — arranged for its purpose, under its light, on its ground plane — never a fork of it into a bespoke style (§ 16, *the costume*). This is what lets THA be many particular rooms and still one coherent home.
- **The interior is designed for the person standing in the doorway, not the one who built it.** Progressive disclosure is an interior principle as much as a behavioural one: a room reveals its depth as the household needs it, and never greets an arrival with everything it contains (Experience Architecture Principle 2; Experience Language Principle 1, arrival before information). Calm at the threshold; capability on request.

## 5. MATERIAL PALETTE

*(Cited synthesis. The material **direction** — the three grounds, the ground plane as the room's identity, air as a material — is the Experience Blueprint's (§ 8). Every material **value** — radius law, shadow definition, surface tokens, spacing scale — is the UI Architecture's (§ 4, § 9, § 16). This section restates neither; it names the design sensibility that makes the palette read as one house.)*

The Orchard House is built from a **small, honest, warm** material vocabulary, spoken identically in every room (Experience Blueprint § 8). The design sensibility governing that vocabulary:

- **Few materials, used everywhere.** A restricted palette is a design decision, not a limitation. The house gains its coherence from using the *same* small set of grounds, surfaces, and warmth in every room (Experience Blueprint § 8.1, the three grounds). A room that reaches for a new material to feel like itself has misunderstood the house (§ 16, *the costume*).
- **The middle ground is where a room becomes itself.** Only one layer varies by room — the ground plane and the surfaces on it (Experience Blueprint § 8.1). A counter, a table, a shelf, a note, a bench, a record: same material system, different proportion and posture (§ 5.1, § 8.2). The world behind (the orchard and its light) and the foreground (overlays, the Companion) are constant product-wide. This is the whole mechanism of *many places without forking the product*.
- **Solidity follows importance.** The primary surface is solid and lit; support is lower and quieter; quiet content lies directly on the ground like a note on a counter — and dense data is *never* naked ink on the environment (Experience Blueprint § 8.2). The material palette carries hierarchy so that colour and motion do not have to.
- **Warmth is the constant temperature of every material.** Every ground in the house is warm (Experience Language § 3A, the always-warm palette; Calm Orchard, UIA § 4). There is no cold surface, no clinical white, no synthetic gloss anywhere in the palette — warmth is the material signature of the whole house.

The binding law of what may actually ship remains UIA § 4 until the governed amendment lands (§ 2.4). This section changes nothing about that; it states the sensibility, not the values.

## 6. LIGHT PHILOSOPHY

*(Cited. The house's light — one sun, one direction, one morning; rooms differing by exposure, never by hour; penumbra as hierarchy — is the Experience Blueprint's (§ 7). The **meaning** of light is the Experience Language's (Principles 6 and F). Every light **value** — hue, gradient, shadow colour, contrast — is the UI Architecture's (§ 7). The design reading only.)*

Light is the fastest carrier of the house's temperature, and the design language treats it as the primary instrument of calm:

- **One morning, forever.** Every room in the house is a morning room, lit by the same sun from the same upper-left direction at the same hour (Experience Blueprint § 7). The design consequence is total consistency of shadow: a designer never chooses a light direction, because the house has exactly one. This is a discipline that reads instantly as coherence and is impossible to fake room by room.
- **Rooms differ by how much light, never by what kind.** A room's light character is how much of the one morning its window admits and what the light falls on (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7) — never a different colour of light, never a different time of day. Quieter never means darker (§ 16, *the second sun*).
- **Light is the quietest instrument of hierarchy.** The primary stands in the light; support waits in the penumbra; quiet content sits in the shade — depth meaning distance, never drama (Experience Blueprint § 7). The design language prefers light to line: where a heavier border and a shift in illumination both express hierarchy, the house uses illumination.
- **Light carries five meanings and no others — welcome, warmth, calm, clarity, optimism — and never performs** (Experience Language Principles 6 and F, quoted, not re-legislated). The house has lighting, not light shows; the one sanctioned light *moment* is the arrival's, and it reads as morning sun on a surface — once, softly (Experience Blueprint § 7).

## 7. COLOUR PHILOSOPHY

*(Cited. The colour system — every hue, both palettes, the full-mode law — is the UI Architecture's (§ 7). The **feeling** of colour is the Experience Language's (§ 3, § 3A). The design reading only; this section names no colour.)*

The design sensibility governing colour in the Orchard House:

- **Colour is warm daylight, not brand paint.** The palette is the Calm Orchard's — the warm, natural, optimistic colour of the orchard in morning light (UIA § 7; Experience Language § 3A.3, the orchard as life). Colour in this house is *environmental* — the temperature of the place — before it is ever decorative or brand-assertive.
- **Restraint is the colour discipline.** Most of the house is warm neutral ground; saturated colour is spent sparingly and meaningfully, never spread for energy or brand presence (Experience Language Principle H; § 7 anti-patterns). A room that reaches for its own accent colour to feel distinct has become a costume (Experience Blueprint § 16). Rooms differ by light and material, not by palette (§ 5.2).
- **Colour never carries meaning alone, and never alarms outside genuine safety.** Status and emphasis are legible without relying on hue (accessibility floors, UIA § 15); alarm colour is reserved for real safety, never for attention (Experience Language Principle 7; § 3A.1, never-clinical). The house is optimistic by default — colour that reads as warning, clinic, or luxury-for-its-own-sake is outside the palette (§ 3A.1).
- **Both light and dark are the same house.** Any colour mode is a *complete* rendering of the one house's warmth, never a half-built alternate (UIA § 7's complete-mode law). Dark mode remains unexplored design work and an open item (Experience Blueprint § 18.4).

## 8. TYPOGRAPHY FEELING

*(Cited. Typography is fully owned by the UI Architecture (§ 8): three voices — the working UI typeface, the display typeface, and the signature typeface (THA's own hand). The Experience Blueprint adds the house reading of the signature voice (§ 10). This section adds only the design *feeling*, and no value.)*

The typographic feeling of the Orchard House is **quiet, legible, and human**:

- **The working voice is invisible.** The primary UI typeface carries the whole working product and is meant to be *read, never noticed* (UIA § 8). Legibility and calm are its entire brief; personality in the working voice would be the design showing off (§ 1.2).
- **The display voice gives weight to identity moments** without shouting — used where the house introduces itself, not where it works (UIA § 8).
- **The signature voice is how the house says hello, and only that.** THA's own hand appears in exactly one mapped surface — the greeting at Home, at arrival — and every further use is a separate governed admission in the adoption register (Experience Blueprint § 10; UIA § 8, § 17). **Scarcity is the signature's entire value:** the day it becomes an ordinary font, the warmth it carries is gone. The design language treats the signature voice as the single most protected material in the house.
- **Type is set for breathing, not for density.** Generous measure, generous leading, generous air (Experience Blueprint § 8.2, air as a material; UIA § 9). The house never crowds its type to fit more in; when there is more to say, the room discloses it progressively (§ 4), it does not shrink.

## 9. TEXTURE AND DEPTH

*(Cited. The depth vocabulary — ground plane, warm shadow, penumbra, the three grounds, the hand answering physically — is the Experience Blueprint's material and light direction (§ 7, § 8), and remains unshippable until the governed UIA § 4 amendment lands (§ 2.4). This section names the design sensibility only, and jumps no path.)*

Texture and depth in the Orchard House are **soft, warm, and shallow** — the depth of a well-lit room, never the depth of a dramatic stage:

- **Depth means distance, not drama.** The house has three grounds — the world behind, the room in the middle, what floats in front (Experience Blueprint § 8.1). Elevation is reserved for what genuinely floats (overlays, dialogs, the Companion); the working surfaces sit calmly in the room's light. There are no deep drop-shadows for effect, no layered parallax, no game-like depth (§ 16, *the rendered world*).
- **Shadow is warm and soft.** The single morning sun casts one family of soft, warm shadows in one direction (Experience Blueprint § 7). Hard, cool, or multi-directional shadow would break the one-morning law and read as a second sun (§ 16).
- **Texture is the honesty of a surface, not an applied grain.** A surface reads as a real, warm working plane because of light and material honesty (§ 3, § 5), never because a wood-grain, paper-fibre, or fabric texture was painted onto it — that is the theme park (Experience Blueprint § 16).
- **The hand answers physically, identically everywhere.** Hover lifts into the light; press seats; focus is the canonical ring — one interaction feel, product-wide (Experience Blueprint § 8.2). Depth is something the household *feels through their pointer*, subtly and consistently, far more than something they see.

## 10. THE HOUSE AND THE ORCHARD

*(Cited. The orchard — one canonical environment, one season, always present, experienced through windows and never walked into — and the Orchard Exposure Scale are the Experience Blueprint's (§ 6). Its meaning as *life* is the Experience Language's (§ 3A.3). The design reading of the relationship only.)*

The relationship between the modern home and the ancient orchard is the defining design idea of THA, and its governing rule is fixed:

> **The orchard is the setting, never the decoration.** *(Experience Blueprint § 6, § 16; § 1.4.)*

The design language expresses that relationship as follows:

- **The house is oriented toward the orchard; it never contains it.** No room *has* the orchard as a backdrop; every room *faces* it through a window sized to the room's purpose (Experience Blueprint § 6.1, *the orchard is never wallpaper*; § 6.2). The uniform backdrop is the single most tempting and most forbidden move in the whole design language — *everywhere at once is nowhere in particular* (§ 16, *wallpaper*).
- **Exposure is inversely proportional to work.** The more a surface asks the eye to do, the further the orchard recedes — from the open view (E3, Home only), to a framed window (E2, browsing rooms), to light alone (E1, working rooms), to lit-from-the-hall (E0, admin and dialogs) (Experience Blueprint § 6.2). The orchard is always *present*; how much of it a room *shows* is a design constant, set once, never adjusted for taste mid-feature.
- **The orchard is life, and it is timeless.** Bright, growing, in season, tended, in one perpetual morning — never gloomy, misty, melancholy, or turning with a calendar (Experience Language § 3A.3; Experience Blueprint § 6.0, § 6.1). Its permanence is the point: it was there before this visit and will be there after, which is exactly why the household never has to tend it (§ 6.0).
- **The orchard never carries text, never animates, and never invites exploration of itself** (Experience Blueprint § 6.1). Legibility is never traded for atmosphere; place survives total stillness; the household is at home, not in a scene (§ 16, *the rendered world*).

The whole relationship, said as one design instruction: **build the working surface first and honestly; then let exactly the right amount of orchard in — and not one pixel more.**

## 11. SEASONAL BEHAVIOUR

*(Cited. The one-season, one-morning law is the Experience Blueprint's (§ 6.0, § 6.1, § 7). This section states, as a design doctrine, *why the house has no seasons* — the reasoning is this document's; the rule is the Blueprint's.)*

**The Orchard House has one season and one hour, and this is a deliberate design decision, not an unfinished one.**

- **One perpetual bright morning, in leaf, in season, forever** (Experience Blueprint § 6.1, § 7). No room is ever at dusk, in autumn, in winter bareness, or under a cycling calendar; those would break the one-morning and one-orchard laws (§ 6.0).
- **"Ancient" is maturity, never a turning year.** The orchard's age shows as settled, established fullness — never as decay, weathering, bare branches, or a season in transition (Experience Blueprint § 6.0). Timelessness *is* its constancy.
- **Why the house refuses seasonal dressing.** A seasonal theme — autumn leaves in October, snow in December, spring blossom in April — is precisely the kind of temporary, fashionable charm this document's § 14 forbids. It is applied decoration, not lived-in life; it dates the product to a moment; it is a second orchard by the back door; and it trades the household's calm constancy for novelty. Seasonal dressing was **considered and declined** and is recorded as such (`EXP5_ONE_HOME_MANY_PLACES.md` § 5.3; Experience Blueprint § 12.2). The house does not change with the calendar for the same reason it does not change with fashion (§ 14): its value to the household is that it is *always the same warm morning*, no matter what season the household's life is in.

The only thing in the house that changes with time is the household's own data — the clock in the greeting, the sun on today's plan, the freshness of the pantry (Experience Blueprint § 12.2, the Living Details). **Time shows through the household's life, never through the house's weather.**

## 12. EMOTIONAL ATMOSPHERE

*(Cited. The complete emotional target — the seven feelings, the Emotional Palette of never-/always-feelings, "calm must never become lifeless" — is the Experience Language's (§ 3, § 3A). This section states only the design reading: how the design language *produces* that atmosphere.)*

The atmosphere the whole design language exists to produce is the Experience Language's, quoted once:

> **A warm, lived-in home where someone has already thought about dinner** — calm · warm · energised · thoughtful · comforting · decisive · curious; never cold, clinical, empty, silent, sterile, luxury-for-luxury's-sake, funeral-parlour calm, or emotionally distant. *(Experience Language § 3A; Experience Blueprint § 1.1.)*

The design language's contribution to that atmosphere — the levers this document's owned character actually pulls:

- **Warmth is carried by light and material, never by ornament** (§ 5, § 6). The house feels warm because its ground is warm and its light is morning, not because anything was decorated to seem friendly.
- **Calm is carried by air, restraint, and one primary action** (§ 4; Experience Architecture Principle 4). The house feels calm because each room asks for one thing at a time and leaves the rest as composed space.
- **Life is carried by the household's own data** (§ 4; Experience Blueprint § 12). The house feels lived-in because the household's real life shows through it — the single guard against calm curdling into the lifeless, sterile, funeral-parlour quiet the palette forbids (Experience Language § 3A.4).
- **Confidence and welcome are carried by consistency** (§ 8, § 14). The house feels trustworthy because its walls, light, and materials never change from room to room; the household relaxes into a place they already know (Experience Language Principles 9, 11, E).

**The design language's one atmospheric test:** *does this surface feel like a home someone keeps, or like a screen someone shipped?* If the honest answer is the second, the design is not finished (§ 16.2).

### 13.1 Home — the threshold and the heart

- **Purpose.** To welcome the household and orient them: *how are we doing, and what's next?* — before anything is asked of anyone (Experience Blueprint § 4; Experience Architecture § 4).
- **Emotional tone.** Welcomed, expected, calm — arrival before information (Experience Language § 5.1, Principle 1, § 4A Principle A).
- **Light.** Full morning; the brightest room in the house (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** A compact counter that keeps its share of the frame so the view keeps its own (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E3 — the open view.** Home only; the view *is* part of the room's purpose (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** *The greeting in THA's hand* — the household's name in the signature voice, at most once a day (Experience Blueprint § 12.2, § 10).
- **Things to avoid.** A dashboard of metrics greeting the arrival; the signature voice used more than once; the open view turned into wallpaper behind dense content; all view and no room (Experience Blueprint § 16; Experience Language Principle G, *home is not the dashboard*).

### 13.2 Planner — the family table

- **Purpose.** To lay out and decide the week together (Experience Blueprint § 4.1; Experience Language § 5.2).
- **Emotional tone.** In control, unhurried, decisive — a table the family gathers around, not a form they fill in (Experience Language § 5.2).
- **Light.** Even working light; the sun on today (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** One solid table holding the whole week — one ground, never nested (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8.2).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E1 — light only.** A working room; the orchard is warmth and daylight, not image (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** *The sun on today* — a half-step of warmth on the current day, never a colour or a border (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).
- **Things to avoid.** A view upgrade because the week looked plain; the orchard behind the grid; metaphor taxing the work — if the concept ever fights the Planner, the concept loses (Experience Blueprint § 16, *metaphor taxing function*).

### 13.3 Cookbook — the recipe book by the window

- **Purpose.** To hold and open *this family's* used cookbook — what they cook, not a catalogue of what anyone could (Experience Blueprint § 4.1).
- **Emotional tone.** Warm, familiar, unhurried browsing — a well-loved book, not a store (Experience Language § 5.3).
- **Light.** Warm side-light across the page (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** A shelf; recipe cards as objects you pick up (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E2 — the window.** A framed, partial presence in one committed region the content does not cover (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** *The well-thumbed page* — a half-step of surface warmth from real cook/plan counts; never a badge, rank, or label (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).
- **Things to avoid.** A drawn book with page-turn animation (the theme park); a rank or streak badge (charm competing with content); the window covered by cards (Experience Blueprint § 16).

### 13.4 Pantry — the pantry, orchard beyond

- **Purpose.** To tell the household, honestly, what is in the house right now (Experience Blueprint § 4.1).
- **Emotional tone.** Practical, honest, reassuring — helpful, never guilt-inducing (Experience Language § 5.6).
- **Light.** Practical, morning-warm (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** Shelf strata — the pantry's tiers (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E2 — the smallest window** in the house (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** *Freshness, honestly told* — the canonical status vocabulary from real freshness data; helpful words, never guilt (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).
- **Things to avoid.** Painted jars, drawn produce, or fake fullness (the theme park; fabricated feeling); status that shames rather than helps (Experience Blueprint § 16; Experience Language § 7).

### 13.5 Shopping — the list by the door

- **Purpose.** To gather the list by the door, ready for the trip out (Experience Blueprint § 4.1).
- **Emotional tone.** Brisk, satisfying, momentary — a room passed through, not lingered in (Experience Language § 5.4).
- **Light.** Plain bright; passed through (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** One note sized to its list (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E1** (E2 when the list is clear — an honest empty room may open the window one level; Experience Blueprint § 6.2, rule 2).
- **Signature moment.** *The crossing-off* — items struck and settled into shade from real list state; no celebration per item (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).
- **Things to avoid.** Confetti or reward animation on completion (decorative delight; earned delight only); a full landscape behind a short list (all view, no room) (Experience Blueprint § 16; Experience Language Principle 10).

### 13.6 Diary — the window seat

- **Purpose.** To hold a quiet, personal record the household returns to (Experience Language § 5; Experience Blueprint § 5.1).
- **Emotional tone.** Quiet, reflective, gently remembered — never dim, never funeral-parlour still (Experience Language § 3A.4; Experience Blueprint § 5.1).
- **Light.** A gathered pool of the same morning — quiet, never dim (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** A lap desk; the most air in the house (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8.2).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E2, softly** (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** *Yesterday's trace* — one line from the person's own last entry, only when true, never analysed back unasked (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).
- **Things to avoid.** Quiet curdling into cold or lifeless (the counterfeit calm); dimming the room to signal "reflective" — quieter is never darker (Experience Language § 3A.4; Experience Blueprint § 16, *the second sun*).

### 13.7 Profile / Household — the family record

- **Purpose.** To hold the record of the people this house is for (Experience Blueprint § 4.1, § 5.1).
- **Emotional tone.** Personal, first-class, respected — a record of people, not a settings screen (Experience Language § 5.8).
- **Light.** Even, honest, unshadowed (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** The record; anchored strata (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E1** (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** *The family, first-class* — the canonical avatar/name treatment from real household membership; no album theming (Experience Blueprint § 12.2).
- **Things to avoid.** Treating people as form fields; decorative "profile" theming or cover imagery (the costume) (Experience Blueprint § 16).

### 13.8 Companion — the friend at the counter (a presence, not a room)

- **Purpose.** To be the knowledgeable friend at the counter: present, prepared, suggesting — never deciding, never a room of its own (Experience Blueprint § 13; Experience Architecture § 11).
- **Emotional tone.** Present, never pushy; quiet competence on the household's side (Experience Language Principle 7, § 5.7).
- **Light.** The light of whatever room it opens in — never differently lit, or it becomes a stage (Experience Blueprint § 13).
- **Material emphasis.** Foreground — it floats (Experience Blueprint § 8.1, § 13); it carries the house's warmth, not a view of its own.
- **Orchard exposure.** None — it is foreground. It speaks *about* the orchard's world; it never displays it (Experience Blueprint § 13).
- **Signature moment.** *Arrives a beat after you* — manners rendered as the one governed motion exception; its entire sign of life (Experience Blueprint § 12.2, § 13).
- **Things to avoid.** A face, a pulse, typing theatrics, a simulated mood; following the household from room to room; a differently-lit panel that becomes a stage (Experience Blueprint § 13; UIA § 10).

### 13.9 Admin — the study off the hall

- **Purpose.** The operational study: the work of running the house, kept off the main hall (Experience Language § 5.9; Experience Blueprint § 5.1).
- **Emotional tone.** Focused, plain, trustworthy — task light, still unmistakably in the house (Experience Language § 5.9).
- **Light.** Task light; the warmth floor still applies (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 7).
- **Material emphasis.** Solid working ground (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 8).
- **Orchard exposure.** **E0 — lit from the hall.** No orchard image; the same warm canvas, tokens, and light temperature (Experience Blueprint § 6.2).
- **Signature moment.** **None — deliberately.** Admin is the one room with no Living Detail; its honesty is its warmth (Experience Blueprint § 5.1, § 12.1).
- **Things to avoid.** A cold, clinical "admin console" temperature that leaves the house (Experience Language § 3A.1); adding a Living Detail because every other room has one (charm by the batch) (Experience Blueprint § 16).

## 14. TIMELESS, NOT FASHIONABLE

*(Owned here — the doctrine has no owner in any other governing document. It is the design-craft reading of "the orchard is timeless" (Experience Blueprint § 1.4, § 6.0) and "premium through restraint" (Experience Language Principle H), fused into a rule a designer can apply at the moment of a decision.)*

The single most important design decision THA makes is a decision *not* to make: **THA does not follow UI fashion.** The Orchard House is built to look right in ten years, not fresh this quarter.

### 14.1 Why timeless, and not fashionable

- **The orchard is timeless, so the house must be too.** The vision is a modern home *in an ancient orchard* (Experience Blueprint § 1.4). An ancient orchard does not follow fashion — *"it does not change with fashion because it never followed one"* (§ 1.4). A house that chased trends beside a timeless orchard would be the one thing in the whole scene that looked dated, and the contrast would collapse.
- **The household's trust compounds with constancy.** THA's deepest value is that it is *always the same warm morning* — the household relaxes because the house does not change under them (Experience Language Principles 9, 11, E; § 12 here). Every trend adopted is a small betrayal of that constancy: it dates the product, resets the household's familiarity, and spends trust to look current. Fashion and trust pull in opposite directions, and in this house trust always wins.
- **Trends are the technology becoming the subject.** A trend is, by definition, design calling attention to itself as *of the moment*. That is precisely what the Technology Principle forbids — the tool made the subject, the family pushed to the background (Experience Blueprint § 1.5; § 1.2 here). A trend-following surface has *noticed itself*, and the North Star's test is that the household *noticed the product not at all* (Experience Blueprint § 17).
- **Fashion is cheap; restraint is expensive.** It is easy to look current — copy the season's motif. It is hard to look right for a decade — choose only what serves the household and leave the rest out. Restraint is the more expensive, more premium decision, and THA's premium is *care the household feels, not ornament they see* (Experience Language Principle H, § 17). Following a trend is decoration; resisting one is craft.

### 14.2 The test at the moment of decision

When a designer is tempted by a technique, one question settles it:

> **Am I adding this because it serves the household, or because it is current?** If the honest answer is "because it is current," it does not enter the house.

A sharpening of the same test, quotable in review:

- **Would this decision still look right in ten years?** If it would look *dated* — not merely old, but *of a specific past moment* — it is fashion, and it is refused.
- **Does removing it cost the household anything?** If the household would not feel its absence, it was decoration, not craft (Experience Language Principle H's test, applied). Fashion is almost always removable without loss; that is how you recognise it.
- **Does it change the house, or just re-skin it?** A genuine improvement deepens the same house — clearer light, honester material, more air. A trend *re-skins* the same house to look different for its own sake, which is the costume (Experience Blueprint § 16).

### 14.3 What "timeless" is *not*

Timeless does not mean frozen, and this doctrine is not a licence to stop improving:

- **Timeless is not stagnant.** The house is refined continually — but by *deepening its own character* (better light, honester material, more restraint), never by *adopting an external trend*. Refinement over redesign is the premium standard (Experience Architecture § 17). The house gets better at being itself; it does not get replaced with a fashionable copy.
- **Timeless is not anti-modern.** The house is emphatically modern in form (§ 3; Experience Blueprint § 1.4). "Modern" and "fashionable" are different words: modern is *considered and current in service of the work*; fashionable is *current for its own sake*. THA is the first and never the second.
- **Timeless is not featureless.** New capability enters constantly. What is timeless is the *design language it enters through* — the same walls, light, materials, and restraint. A new room is furnished from the same vocabulary as every old one (§ 4; Experience Blueprint § 5.2).

## 15. THE DESIGN MANIFESTO

*(Owned here. Not a rule and adding none — it is the direction every rule in this document and the Experience Blueprint points. It is the Design North Star (Experience Blueprint § 17), read as a manifesto meant to steer THA's design for the next decade.)*

**The Healthy Apples is a modern home in an ancient orchard, where technology quietly supports timeless family life** *(Experience Blueprint § 17)*. If a designer keeps only these ten lines, they can keep building the same house:

1. **Build a home, not an interface.** Every surface is a room in one house the household lives in — never a page, a screen, or a dashboard. If it does not feel like somewhere, it is not finished.

2. **Let the household be the subject, always.** The design, like the technology, quietly disappears. The best surface is one the household feels and cannot name. When the design and the household compete for attention, the design loses.

3. **Face the orchard; never paste it on.** The orchard is the setting, not the decoration. Build the working surface first and honestly, then let in exactly the right amount of view — and not one pixel more.

4. **Keep one morning.** One sun, one direction, one warm hour, in every room, forever. Quieter is never darker. The house is always the same bright morning, whatever season the household's life is in.

5. **Furnish by subtraction.** Everything present is meant, and nothing else is there. Air is a material. Restraint is the more expensive, more premium decision — and it is the one THA makes.

6. **Decorate only with the household's real life.** The one ornament is evidence that this family lives here — always true, always data-borne, honest in its absence. Never a painted prop, never applied charm.

7. **Carry warmth in light and material, never in ornament.** The house is warm because its ground is warm and its light is morning — not because anything was dressed up to seem friendly. Calm must never become cold, and warmth must never become decoration.

8. **Make every room particular, and every room the same house.** Rooms differ only by purpose, light, material, and one sign of life. They never fork the shared walls, palette, type, or motion. Difference is a use of the house, never a departure from it.

9. **Refuse fashion; deepen the house instead.** Build to look right in ten years, not fresh this quarter. When tempted by a technique, ask whether it serves the household or is merely current — and let only the first in. Refine the house into more of itself; never re-skin it to look new.

10. **When two designs both pass every gate, choose the one the household would feel and could not name; when restraint and expression tie, choose restraint; when the product and the household tie, there is no tie** *(Experience Blueprint § 17)*.

> **The design is done when a household ends every visit having thought about food a little less, trusted it a little more, and noticed the product not at all** *(Experience Blueprint § 17)*. Build the Orchard House so that what lingers is the feeling of the place — calm, warm, alive, already thinking about dinner — and never a screen, an effect, or a trend.

### 16.1 This document is governing architecture

Every user-facing *visual design* decision is made under it, beside the three gates and the Blueprint Checks it does not duplicate. It is indexed in the Architecture Bootstrap (`docs/architecture/README.md`) as required reading beside the Experience Blueprint it extends.

### 16.2 The Design Character Check

This document adds exactly one check to the Experience & UI Governance Compliance gate, covering only the concerns it alone owns. It sits **after** the Experience Test (Experience Blueprint § 15.3) and the Blueprint Checks (§ 15.2), and duplicates neither — **if any item fails: STOP, explain why, do not continue until approved.**

```
□ Architectural character
  Does this surface read as the one house — structural honesty, glazed toward
  the orchard, natural light and material, uncluttered, quietly confident?
  Nothing faux, no applied texture, no synthetic gloss? (§ 3, § 9)

□ Interior philosophy
  Composed emptiness, not bare emptiness? Everything present meant, nothing
  else there? Decorated only by the household's real life, never by applied
  charm? Furnished from the shared vocabulary, not a bespoke style? (§ 4)

□ Timeless, not fashionable
  Is this here because it serves the household, or because it is current?
  Would it still look right in ten years — or would it look OF a past moment?
  Does removing it cost the household anything? If it re-skins the house
  rather than deepening it, it does not enter. (§ 14)

□ Design disappears
  Would the household FEEL this surface and be unable to NAME it? If the
  design calls attention to itself as design, it has failed. (§ 1.2, § 12)

□ The room reading honoured
  Does the surface match its room's design characterisation (§ 13) — the tone,
  light, material emphasis, exposure, and signature moment its owners fix? If
  a NEW room: has its reading been added by governance, not improvised? (§ 13)
```

### 16.3 One rule, one owner, forever

If a concern owned here later earns a fuller governing home — for example, the architectural character graduating into the UI Architecture's Calm Orchard visual language by amendment — this document yields ownership in the same change and cites the new owner.
