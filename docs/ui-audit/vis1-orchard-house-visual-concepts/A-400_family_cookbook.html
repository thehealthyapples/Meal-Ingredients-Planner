The headline finding this room carries “The Cookbook is the only room in the house named for a family that does not have one.” meals.userId is notNull with no householdId — “the family's cookbook is N private books” (COOK1). The constitutional clause is a possessive, not an aesthetic: “a catalogue with warm lighting is still a catalogue.” No amount of design on this sheet fixes that, and this sheet does not pretend to.

1 · Emotional intention

Appetite and possibility. Browsing what we could cook — leafing, lingering, being tempted. The warmest browsing register in the product; the food itself is the colour.

EXP5 · OHDB § 13.3 — “warm, familiar, unhurried browsing — a well-loved book, not a store”

The register is recognition, not choosing: "A catalogue asks you to decide; a family's book asks you to recall." The pleasure is oh, this one — and the small warmth of a page that has clearly been opened before. This is the one room in the house made for lingering.

The guard on lingering “Lingering is a permitted feeling, never a measured goal.” The measure remains the same as every other room's: the household ate better with less effort — never time in the product. This room's completion may honestly be "returns to browsing"; that is a rest state, not an engagement metric.

2 · Light, and the relationship to the orchard

The Cookbook earns more window than any room but Home — "recipes and growing food belong within sight of each other" (EXP5). It can afford it because its density is browsing density, not working density. This is the inverse law paying out in the room's favour for once.

The empty-state rider is the only sanctioned response to a thin book: E2 → E3, only while genuinely empty. “The answer to an empty book is more light, not more prompting.”

Live — E2 rendered as wallpaper “The Cookbook is E2 and is currently rendered as wallpaper” — 18% of the orchard behind every recipe card (see A-120). NORTH1 § 5.5 predicted this room by name, having watched the render's own Cookbook plate fail: "the whole frame is environment: all view, no room… it shows the render's grammar does not survive contact with functional density." The render disproved itself in this exact room.

3 · Materials, and the ground's posture

Recipe cards are the most object-like surfaces in the product — handmade ceramics in the material vocabulary: the individual objects a household picks up and sets down, each honest and slightly its own (TRANSLATION1, HANDMADE CERAMICS). Their individuality is the household's real content, never applied variation. No bespoke per-surface card styling; no drawn ceramic; no skeuomorphic pot.

This room's ground is the Planner's opposite, deliberately — and both currently obey. Where PLAN1 required one solid table and forbade nested cards, the Cookbook is the one room where discrete card objects are the canonical posture. That is not an inconsistency in the house; it is the middle ground doing exactly the job Blueprint § 8.1 gives it.

The food is the room's real decoration — "honest, appetising, in real light" (EXP5). This is the one warmth carrier no other room has, and it is contingent on real photography of real food. Absent it, the room falls back to a tinted panel and a crossed-cutlery watermark — which is the whole of its warmth budget, gone.

4 · Depth

Objects on a shelf: shallow depth, but plural. Each card lifts a half-step into the light on hover and seats on press — the one product-wide interaction feel. A card is a thing you pick up. It never animates for attention.

The house's shadow law still binds: one family of soft, warm, single-direction shadow. Six cards do not mean six light sources.

Favourites are noticed, never collected “There is no star. There is no favourite button, and there must not be one.” A declared favourite is a rating, and a rating is a rank. “A favourite in THA is a thing the household did, never a thing they filed.” The absence of the button is compliance, not a gap. Warmth here is earned, not declared.

The Companion is "the friend leaning on the counter while you leaf through — happy to say 'you loved this in the winter' or 'this one's quick tonight' if you want it, happy to stay quiet while you decide. It never turns the browsing into a task or the book into a store."

7 · The one sign of life

The ones you cook most often carry a faint, earned warmth, the way a loved page falls open on its own — never a badge, never a rank, never a score. This book keeps no leaderboard. It simply shows, quietly, that it is used.

Orchard Living Book, The Living Cookbook

The formulation to keep “The delight is that the page falls open. It is not that the page tells you it fell open.” (COOK1) — the clearest available statement of the whole Living Detail constitution, in any room.

8 · What this room must never become

Refused

  • A drawn book with page-turn theatrics — the theme park. Entry route: the word “cookbook” itself, the most literal noun in the map
  • Seasonal dressing — “the room's most attractive violation”: blossom, pastel, autumn leaves. It is a second orchard by the back door. The season appears in the food, never in the room
  • A store. A four-tab control and a “From the Web” section = the family's book arranged like a shop
  • A catalogue of what they haven't cooked
  • The favourite star, a streak, a rank, “you've cooked 50 meals!”, “Your autumn wrapped!” — all refused, and recorded as refused

Entry routes to watch

  • Discovery — the boundary is already drawn in the intent router: “An ownership-qualified meal query belongs to meals. An unqualified one belongs to meal-discovery. The qualifier is the boundary.”
  • A “cook this tonight” auto-pick — the Companion deciding what the family cooks
  • Losing the food photography and reaching for decoration to replace its warmth

The test

  • Does this ask them to decide, or let them recall?
  • Is the warmth earned or declared?
  • Is this their book, or everyone's?
The coldness risk is highest here, and the fallback is thinnest Clinical white is more dangerous in this room, because its warmth is supposed to come from the food itself. “The Cookbook must get clearer without getting colder, and it has the least fallback if it does.”

Its warmth comes from the food; its light comes across the page from a window it earns more of than any room but Home; its pleasure is recognition, not choosing; and its one sign of life is that a loved page falls open on its own — a half-step of warmth, never a badge, never a rank, never a score.
