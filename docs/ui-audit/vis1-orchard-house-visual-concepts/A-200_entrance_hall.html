1 · Emotional intention

Welcomed, expected, calm — arrival before information.

Experience Language § 5.1 · Principle 1 · § 4A Principle A

Home is the only room whose first job is not work. It exists to say you are expected here before it says anything else, and everything it can do afterwards is downstream of getting that beat right. The house does not greet you with signage, and it does not greet you with a report.

The emotional failure this room is most likely to commit is the most reasonable-sounding one: be useful immediately. That is how an entrance hall becomes a dashboard.

Home is not the dashboard "A dashboard of metrics greeting the arrival; the open view turned into wallpaper behind dense content; all view and no room." (OHDB § 13.1; Experience Language Principle G) The render this programme is measured against failed exactly here: NORTH1 § 5.4 named its "glance card with no door" — a beautiful summary of the household's week that offered them nowhere to go — and NORTH2 § 6 records the live product has the same hole: Home has no primary action.

2 · Light, and the relationship to the orchard

orchard, admitted as image bounding architectural ground the counter — solid, lit
Why E3 is an exception and not a licence Home is the only room in the house at E3. NORTH2 § 3.4 refused to generalise it, and the reasoning is the sharpest sentence in the exposure canon: "In the Planner, the Analyser, and every dense surface, the work is the primary experience and the room is deliberately almost gone. Home is E3 — the exception the scale exists to bound — not the rule the house follows." A future room that wants a view must earn it by having less to do, never by wanting to look better.

3 · Materials, and the ground's posture

The counter is the middle ground of Home: one warm working plane, solid and lit, holding everything the product could hold on the household's behalf. It is "centred-low where a hand rests, meeting the view at one horizon" (TRANSLATION1, THE KITCHEN ISLAND § 4) — and at E3 it is compact on purpose, because the view is part of the work here and a counter that spread would be taking the household's own window.

Never nested. One ground per workspace. Never a grid of metric tiles standing to attention.

4 · Depth

Home carries the house's standard three grounds (see A-110) at their widest separation: the orchard genuinely behind, the counter genuinely in the room, the Companion genuinely in front. It is the room where depth is most legible and therefore most tempting to overspend.

Depth stays shallow, warm and single-directional. The counter does not float — it sits. Elevation is reserved for what genuinely floats, which at Home is the Companion and nothing else.

The one datable thing in the render NORTH1 § 5.6 singled out the render's frosted glass panel — the translucent slab the interface sat on — as "the one datable thing in the frame." It is fashion, it will look of-its-moment in five years, and it is the exact move a designer reaches for when a surface must sit over a view. The answer at E3 is not to frost the view; it is to stand beside it on solid ground.

6 · Interaction principles

The welcome is a gift, never a gate. Nothing modals, interrupts, or demands input before the arrival has landed. No splash screen, loading theatre, or marketing hero standing between the person and the room. You are in the house the moment you are in it (TRANSLATION1, THRESHOLD § 8–9; Experience Architecture Principle 8).

The product orients; the person chooses. It decides the most useful next step and offers it — and never takes the step for the household.

The one sanctioned light moment in the whole house is Home's arrival, and it is spent once: morning sun on a surface, softly, carrying warmth rather than attention. Every later return that day is quieter and does not re-greet. The room is simply, already, there.

7 · The one sign of life

The greeting in THA's hand — the most protected material in the house The household's name, in THA's own signature voice, at most once a day. It appears in exactly one mapped surface in the entire product, and every further use is a separate governed admission in the adoption register. Scarcity is the signature's entire value: the day it becomes an ordinary font, the warmth it carries is gone (Blueprint § 10; UIA § 8; OHDB § 8). Of the five rooms in this set, Home is the only one permitted to use it at all.

8 · What this room must never become

Refused

  • A dashboard of metrics greeting the arrival
  • The signature voice used more than once — the one irreversible spend in the house
  • The open view turned into wallpaper behind dense content
  • All view and no room — a welcome floating on a landscape, reading as nobody home
  • A glance card with no door — orientation with nowhere to go

Entry routes to watch

  • “Be useful immediately” — the most reasonable-sounding way to build a dashboard
  • “The greeting worked so well, let's use the hand elsewhere”
  • “The view is our best asset, let's show more of it” — a form never earns a view upgrade because it looked plain; nor does a home
  • Frosting the view to seat the interface on it

The test

  • Does a household arriving here feel expected, or assessed?
  • Is there a door?
  • Would they feel this surface and be unable to name it?

The design is done when a household ends every visit having thought about food a little less, trusted it a little more, and noticed the product not at all.
