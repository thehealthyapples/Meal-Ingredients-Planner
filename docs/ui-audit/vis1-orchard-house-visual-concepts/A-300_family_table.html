The sentence this room is governed by “If the concept ever fights the Planner, the concept loses.” (Blueprint § 16) — and its corollary, which binds this board: “The Planner may refuse the concept; the concept may never refuse the Planner.” (PLAN1) Every drawing on this sheet yields to the work.

1 · Emotional intention

In control, unhurried, decisive — a table the family gathers around, not a form they fill in.

OHDB § 13.2 · Experience Language § 5.2

The emotional purpose has a name in the canon: the week is held — "the feeling of a plan coming together, never of a spreadsheet demanding cells" (EXP5 § 4.2). Here there is work, and it is the good kind.

The distinction that carries the whole room is load-bearing and worth stating exactly:

A form is owed completion; a table is available. A form with three of seven cells filled is 43% done and failing. A table with three meals on it is a table with three meals on it.

PLAN1

The entry route is progress Every mechanism that turns this table back into a form arrives wearing the same costume: a completion ring · “3 of 7 days planned” · an empty-slot count. They are all the same move — measuring a table against a completion it was never owed.

2 · Light, and the relationship to the orchard

E1 is not a demotion “E1 is not the Planner being denied a view — E1 is the view standing back for the work.” (PLAN1) The orchard has stepped back to being warmth and daylight through the room rather than a view, because the work is the point and the work deserves the room (Orchard Living Book, The Family Planning Table). NORTH2 § 3.1 narrowed the aperture rule to be silent at E1 precisely so this room could never be argued into a window.

3 · Materials, and the ground's posture

Depth is believable exactly when it describes one room. One ground, one light, one direction. "The historical failure — cards with drop shadows everywhere — was many imaginary rooms on one page" (EXP4 § 5). This is why the Planner must be a single table and not a grid of floating objects — and why the Cookbook's ground is its deliberate opposite. Both are correct; both are the same house.

Forbidden materials, named: a drawn table edge, wood texture, “place settings” iconography (EXP5). The family table is a feeling produced by material, light, and composition — the moment it becomes a picture of a table, the place has become a costume.

4 · Depth

The shallowest depth in the set. At E1 the background is light without image, the middle ground is a single unbroken table, and the foreground holds only the Companion. There is one plane where the work happens, and it does not float.

Generosity is the depth rule here: "when there is more to consider, the table gives you more room, it never crowds the week to fit" (Orchard Living Book).

The temperature trap — two symmetric failures This room fails in both directions and the second is the one a correct diagnosis causes. Muddying the table with an orchard wash is one failure. Reading “E1 means no image” as “remove the wash” and landing on clinical white is the other — and it is worse, because --card: 0 0% 100% is what the table would fall back to (see A-130). The Planner must get clearer without getting colder.

6 · Interaction principles

BeatThe Planner's own words
ARRIVALthe week opens as a calm, ordered surface, not a spreadsheet demanding entries
ORIENTATION“here is the week, here is what's planned, here are the gaps”
CONFIDENCEthe plan reads as trustworthy; suggestions show they are grounded, not invented
ACTIONone obvious move at a time, each with a sensible default
UNDERSTANDINGthe plan visibly updates; the person sees exactly what changed and why
COMPLETIONthe week resolves to “the plan is made” — a restful, finished state, not a prompt to do more

Function is sacred. The grid remains a grid — "this is the domain where any metaphor that costs one click dies" (EXP5).

The Companion sits at the table and never reaches across it. "It might suggest, it might notice you're short a night or repeating a meal, but it never reaches over and decides for you. The choice stays yours, every time" (Orchard Living Book). Stated as a rule: “It is not forbidden to suggest a week. It is forbidden to have decided one.”

The room's success condition is the household leaving it. The measure is that they ate better with less effort — never time in the product.

7 · The one sign of life

The sun on today — a half-step of warmth on the current day, never a colour or a border.

Blueprint § 12.2 · OHDB § 13.2

It is legal — despite the one-morning law — because it is the calendar's own truth rendered as light, not the house's light changing with the hour. Time shows through the household's life, never through the house's weather (OHDB § 11). The exact formulation is the finest sentence in the Living Details library:

The sun does not move; today is simply where it already falls.

PLAN1

⚑ Dead — the detail has no data “The room built to hold the household's week does not know which day is today.” PLAN1 found zero Date constructions across 4,252 lines and no date column. The Planner's one permitted moment of life is waiting on a column. Under Blueprint § 12.1 r2 — data-borne or dead — it is drawn here and it does not render. r3 makes that survivable: when the data is silent, the detail is absent, and the room is still complete.

8 · What this room must never become

Refused

  • A drawn table. Wood-grain, a table edge, place settings — the theme park
  • A form. The table measured against a completion it was never owed
  • A view upgrade because the week looked plain
  • Clinical white. Calm curdled into lifeless — the unrecoverable failure
  • A scoreboard. The live “🍎 This Week · 🌱 0/30” is this already: “a household that has planned nothing is greeted by a scoreboard reading zero” — a number nobody measured
  • A claimed outcome. “A planned meal is not an eaten meal.” The Planner may celebrate a plan. It may never celebrate a meal.

Entry routes to watch

  • Progress — rings, counts, “3 of 7 planned”
  • Auto-fill — the Companion deciding
  • Signage — “a home does not greet you with signage”
  • A second ambient-notice channel
  • Removing the orchard wash without warming the value

The test

  • Does this cost one click? If yes, the concept loses.
  • Is the week the first thing they see?
  • Does the room feel available, or owed?

The Planner is the family table in the house that is the kitchen. It is the densest working surface in the product, and therefore the room where the concept has the least to say and the most to prove. The orchard is here as light and never as a view. The Companion sits at it and never reaches across it. The week is held, not filled in; the plan is made, and then the household leaves.
