A-010 The form decision — why this board is not a render Scale — NTS

The single most important decision in this programme was not what to draw. It was what kind of drawing is safe to make — because the visual programme has already produced one artefact so good that its own author condemned it.

The image is the most valuable artefact the visual programme has produced, and it must never be built… It is a concept render, and it earns its warmth by exactly the means the canon forbids — a drawn kitchen, painted props, an aphorism on the wall. Its power and its danger are the same property: it is so persuasive that a team will copy the picture instead of extracting the principle, and copying it produces the theme park — the single most-forbidden thing in the entire document set.

NORTH1_THE_VISUAL_NORTH_STAR.md:16 — about its own render

This hazard is not hypothetical. It has a date. HOUSE5 § 1 records that PLAN1 § 0 found the render's noun arriving in a mission brief one day after the render was filed, attached to a room — "the first live instance of the risk NORTH1 predicted." Renders have gravity.

An architect who has shown a client the plans does not next show them a photograph. They bring out the material and light studies — real specimens, under the real light of the site, laid on geometry that is deliberately not the building. That is the third form, and it is the one this mission needs: it shows warmth without drawing a kitchen.

Two rules make it safe, and this board is built to obey them rather than to promise them:

Rule 1 — nothing on this board is a screen Every figure is an instrument: a specimen, a section, a ladder, an aperture diagram, a massing study. Ground posture is drawn as massing — solidity, nesting, proportion — never as layout. A plan of the Planner's cells would be the Planner's grid. There is no composition on this board that could be pasted into a component, because there is no composition of a room on it.
Rule 2 — the board is graphite; the only warmth in it is real material The board decorates itself with nothing. Its sole colour is a specimen chip carrying a real, cited value from the one token source, and a redline where a value contradicts the canon. It practises the law it describes: warmth is carried by light and material, never by ornament (OHDB § 12; TRANSLATION1 § 5.3). And it is not dressed in THA's palette, for the reason HOUSE5 § 2.3 already fixed: "a board that looked like the product would be a render wearing a plan's clothes."
What is adopted from the render, and what is deleted NORTH1 § 4 extracted six principles written to survive the deletion of the image — the sky → light → surface → hand grammar; the aperture and the bounding wall; warmth as the ground temperature of every plane; the interface taking the minimum of the frame its work requires; hierarchy from light and weight, colour spent almost nowhere; depth shallow, warm, single-directional. Those six are the spine of this set. Deleted, per NORTH1 § 5: the kitchen, the island, the beams, the apples, the door, the basket, the six-tile launcher, the frosted glass, the wall aphorism, the tagline. "Keep the light. Delete the room."

A-100 Light study — the one morning Scale — NTS

One sun, one direction, one hour. The morning sun sits upper-left, forever, in every room; every shadow on every surface in every domain agrees. It is always the same bright morning — no room is ever at dusk, in shade-as-mood, or under artificial light. Every room in this house is a morning room.

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md § 7

lit / primary penumbra / support shade / quiet forbidden

The design consequence is total consistency of shadow: a designer never chooses a light direction, because the house has exactly one. This is a discipline that reads instantly as coherence and is impossible to fake room by room (OHDB § 6).

Light carries five meanings and no others — welcome · warmth · calm · clarity · optimism — and never performs. The house has lighting, not light shows. The one sanctioned light moment is the arrival's, and it reads as morning sun on a surface: once, softly (Experience Language Principles 6 and F; Blueprint § 7).

Rejected, and recorded — visual time NORTH2 § 3.5 refused a proposal that the light vary with the hour: "This is not an addition to the architecture — it is a reversal of its single most load-bearing law." The qualifier "without becoming themes or visual gimmicks" is the exact escape the canon has already refused: "atmosphere" is the trap. An atmosphere that varies by hour is a theme; there is no third thing between "the light changes with the clock" and "the light does not." The house has one hour; the household has all of them. Time shows through the household's data, never the house's weather.

A-110 Section — the three grounds, and the one that varies Scale — NTS · section through any room

The middle ground is the whole trick of many places. A domain expresses its character entirely within its ground plane and the light that falls on it, and touches nothing above or below. This is what makes rooms possible without forking the product.

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md § 8.1

the world — constant the room — varies what floats — constant

Solidity follows importance. The primary surface is solid and lit; supporting surfaces are lower and quieter; quiet content lies directly on the ground like a note on a counter. Support is anchored, never naked ink on the environment — dense data never sits on landscape (Blueprint § 8.2). The material palette carries hierarchy so that colour and motion do not have to.

Depth means distance, not drama. Elevation is reserved for what genuinely floats. There are no deep drop-shadows for effect, no layered parallax, no game-like depth. Texture is the honesty of a surface, never an applied grain — a painted wood-grain is the theme park (OHDB § 9).

One ground per workspace, never nested. The five rooms in this set differ in their middle ground and in nothing else — and two of them are drawn as deliberate opposites: the Planner must be one solid table, and the Cookbook must be discrete objects you pick up. Both are correct; both are the same house.

A-120 Aperture study — the orchard, held at four sizes Scale — NTS · plan of the window, not of the room

Orchard exposure is inversely proportional to functional density. The more a surface asks the eye to work, the further the orchard recedes — first into a glimpse, then into light alone.

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md § 6.2

orchard admitted as image ground plane — solid, lit light only, no image
The aperture — the find, and the narrowing that made it safe NORTH1 § 4.2 found why the render's orchard reads as a view and not a backdrop: it is admitted through defined openings separated by solid ground. "The wall is not the absence of orchard; the wall is what makes the orchard legible as orchard." NORTH2 § 3.1 adopted it narrowed: it binds only where the orchard appears as image — E2 and E3 — and the word is bounded, not separated, because E2 has a single region and cannot satisfy a rule presuming two. At E1 and E0 the rule is silent: the orchard is light alone, and its recession is the design.

Three rules ride on the scale and are drawn into every room board in this set:

1 — Exposure is a per-domain constant. Set once by design, never a per-surface choice, never adjusted for taste mid-feature.
2 — Empty states may open the window one level, never two. An honestly empty working room may breathe at E2; it returns the instant content exists. A full-strength landscape behind an empty room reads as nobody home, not as calm.
3 — Emotional surfaces may spend more window; functional surfaces may not. A form never earns a view upgrade because it looked plain.

Live, in every room at once — the orchard is behind the working text client/src/components/layout/orchard-backdrop.tsx:18 hardcodes a full-bleed orchard, and client/src/components/ui/card.tsx:12 is bg-card/82 backdrop-blur-md — so 18% of the orchard bleeds up through the grid cells and behind the meal text, in the Planner (E1, which admits no image at all), the Cookbook, the Pantry (the smallest window in the house) and Shopping alike. --orchard-opacity is defined at index.css:73 and never consumed. This is wallpaper — "the orchard applied uniformly behind everything at one strength on one plane" — and it is not a room defect. It is one line of shared component code, and it is everywhere. Recorded by NORTH1 and HOUSE4 § 11 before this board; drawn here because a board about apertures cannot honestly omit it.

A-130 Material specimens — the only warmth on this board Scale — 1:1 · specimens are fixed, not re-lit

It is warm before it is anything else. The ground of every surface is a warm, hand-finished-feeling plane in low morning light — never a white or cool canvas, never a clinical surface, never a synthetic gloss. Warmth is the temperature of the whole house, carried by light and material, and it is the first thing every surface must get right.

⚑ The specimen that contradicts the canon — one chip, zero saturation

--card: 0 0% 100% is pure white: no hue, no saturation. It is the material of the primary working surface of every room in the house — the counter, the table, the shelf, the note. The canon's requirement for exactly this plane is unambiguous: "warm before it is anything else… never a white or cool canvas" (TRANSLATION1, OAK § 3). Clinical white is named in Experience Language § 3A.1 as one of the eight temperatures THA must never feel.

The two defects are one defect, and they are load-bearing on each other. The reason the white does not currently read as clinical is that card.tsx:12 lets 18% of the orchard bleed through it (A-120). The wallpaper is warming the white. Remove the wallpaper without warming the value and the house gets a cold plane in every room at once — and HOUSE4 § 13.2 calls coldness the one failure the canon calls unrecoverable.

The house must get clearer without getting colder — in all four rooms at once, or in none. Not this board's decision to make, and not this board's rule: recorded by NORTH1 and NORTH2 § 6, which concluded "none of these requires an amendment; all of them require work… the architecture is sound and does not need refining — it needs obeying."

Not drawn — dark mode Blueprint § 18.4 records dark mode as unexplored design work and an open item. Values exist in index.css:116–164; a design for them does not. This board does not invent one — "any colour mode is a complete rendering of the one house's warmth, never a half-built alternate" (OHDB § 7), and inventing it here would be authoring the visual law this set exists to express. It stays open, and it stays the Blueprint's to close.

The material sensibility, cited whole

Few materials, used everywhere. A restricted palette is a design decision, not a limitation. A room that reaches for a new material to feel like itself has misunderstood the house.

The middle ground is where a room becomes itself. A counter, a table, a shelf, a note, a bench, a record: same material system, different proportion and posture.

Warmth is the constant temperature of every material. There is no cold surface, no clinical white, no synthetic gloss anywhere in the palette — point at any pixel of chrome and ask whether it is warm. One cold plane breaks the room (NORTH1 § 4.3).

Texture is the honesty of a surface, not an applied grain. No painted wood-grain, no faux timber, no paper fibre, no fabric weave. Oak is not a texture in this house — it is what warm light on an honest, uncluttered plane produces.

A-140 Hierarchy ladder — light and weight, colour spent last Scale — NTS · a ladder, not a layout

Hierarchy is made of light and weight; colour is spent almost nowhere. The primary leads by standing in the light, never by wearing a colour the room lacks.

NORTH1_THE_VISUAL_NORTH_STAR.md § 4.5 — extracted to survive the deletion of the image

Calm is mostly space. The emphasis budget is spent on a few elements and withheld from everything else. Calm is the layout, not a coat applied to it. A two-second glance yields orientation, state, and the one primary action — never decoration, promotion, or noise.

Air is a material. Generous breathing space is the resting state of every room. When there is more to consider, the room gives more room — it never crowds the work to fit. A larger screen becomes more air and more view, never more widgets.

A-900 General notes — what must never be drawn Binding on every future board and every screen
  1. The analogies are feelings to design toward, never pictures to draw. "The 'family table' is produced by material, light, and composition — the moment it becomes a picture of a table, the place has become a costume." Blueprint § 5.1, § 16
  2. The theme park. Illustrated kitchens, drawn furniture, wood-grain, page-turns, fridge magnets, jar clip-art, photo-corner frames, painted jars, drawn produce, fake steam, decorative crumbs, faux worn edges. The single most-forbidden thing in the entire document set. Blueprint § 16
  3. The costume. A room growing its own palette, component styling, decorative border, or motif to "feel like itself." A room that must break a shared constant to feel like itself has not been designed yet. Blueprint § 5.2, § 16
  4. Wallpaper. The orchard applied uniformly behind everything at one strength on one plane. Everywhere at once is nowhere in particular. Blueprint § 16 — and live in every room; see A-120
  5. All view, no room. A working surface that is mostly environment — a search box floating on a landscape. It reads as nobody home. Blueprint § 16
  6. The second sun. A room at dusk, in fog, in spa-light, or under drama — any lighting that breaks the one morning. Quieter never means darker. Blueprint § 16; NORTH2 § 3.5
  7. Metaphor taxing function. "If the concept ever fights the Planner, the concept loses." And this board is bound by it too: if this set ever starts arguing with a room, the board loses. Blueprint § 16; HOUSE5 § 6.5
  8. Calm must never become lifeless. Cold, clinical, empty, sterile, funeral-parlour still. The one failure the canon calls unrecoverable. Experience Language § 3A.4; HOUSE4 § 13.2
  9. Charm by paint, never by data. The only ornament in the house is evidence that this family lives here — data-borne, honest in absence, still. A painted prop is fabricated feeling, forbidden by construction. Blueprint § 12
  10. Fashion. "Am I adding this because it serves the household, or because it is current?" Built to look right in a decade, not fresh this quarter. OHDB § 14
The star this board is not

The Healthy Apples is a modern home in an ancient orchard, where technology quietly supports timeless family life… The design is done when a household ends every visit having thought about food a little less, trusted it a little more, and noticed the product not at all.

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md § 17 — the Design North Star

That sentence is the North Star, and this board is not. The star already exists, it is governing, and it is a sentence rather than a picture — which is precisely why it has survived (HOUSE5 § 0.3). No surface copies this board. Every surface is measured against the feeling it produces.

The one line to settle any translation Build the working surface first and honestly; carry its warmth in light and material; let in exactly the right amount of orchard; decorate it only with the household's real life — and then take the design, like the technology, quietly out of the way.
