A modern home in an ancient orchard, drawn as a set of architect's plans — because the one thing this house must never be given is a photograph of itself.

This set draws the Orchard House as an architect would present it at concept stage: a site plan, a floor plan, an adjacency diagram, a section, a room schedule, and general notes. It is a drawing about a house, in drafting language. It is not a picture of a kitchen, and that distinction is the whole design.

Sheet note 001 · Why this set is not a render

"The image is the most valuable artefact the visual programme has produced, and it must never be built … it earns its warmth by exactly the means the canon forbids — a drawn kitchen, painted props, an aphorism on the wall. Its power and its danger are the same property: it is so persuasive that a team will copy the picture instead of extracting the principle, and copying it produces the theme park — the single most-forbidden thing in the entire document set."

NORTH1_THE_VISUAL_NORTH_STAR.md:16 — about its own render

THA already has a photorealistic North Star. Its own author condemned it, and PLAN1 § 0 later recorded the predicted failure arriving in a brief one day later. A plan cannot fail that way. Nobody ships a floor plan; nobody copies a hatch pattern into a component. A drawing set is the one visual form that forces the reader to extract the principle, because the picture is unusable on purpose.

This set shows

The orchard surrounding the home · the rooms · how they relate · where the Companion belongs · each room's emotional character · light, materials and atmosphere · the household's journey.

This set is not

Implementation. Production UI. Governing architecture. A visual style guide. It sets no colour, token, size, or value, and every attribute drawn is cited to the document that owns it.

Drawn in red

Conditions no document owns. Architects redline unresolved scope; this house has some, and honest drawings show it. Red is never decoration here.

The site plan is the exposure scale drawn as what it actually is: a section through the house's relationship to its own garden. Exposure is not a style setting — it is how deep into the house a room sits, and therefore how much window it has earned.

"Orchard exposure is inversely proportional to functional density. The more a surface asks the eye to work, the further the orchard recedes — first into a glimpse, then into light alone."

HOUSE4 § 1 — headline

This sheet therefore draws only what is built and verified, from HOUSE4 § 5.5. It proposes no adjacency, names no circulation, and invents no route. Where the canon is silent, the drawing is silent — in red.

Every solid line on this sheet is a connection that exists in code. Every dashed red line is one that does not. None of them is governed by any document — and the strongest of them is the household's most-walked route.

The spine — built, excellent, unnamed

Cookbook → Planner → Shopping is the journey's spine "and it is excellent" — three rooms, two joins, zero governing rules about either join.

A-200 Section A–A — light and atmosphere Scale — NTS

"One sun, one direction, one hour. The morning sun sits upper-left, forever, in every room; every shadow on every surface in every domain agrees. It is always the same bright morning — no room is ever at dusk, in shade-as-mood, or under artificial light. Every room in this house is a morning room."

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md · § 7

A section is the honest way to draw this rule, because it shows what a render cannot: the sun does not move, the rooms do. Light carries five meanings and no others — welcome · warmth · calm · clarity · optimism — and it is "hierarchy's quietest instrument": the primary stands in the light, support waits in the penumbra, quiet content sits in the shade. Rooms have lighting, not light shows.

The atmosphere follows from this and from one refusal: "Calm must never become lifeless." The house must never feel cold, clinical, empty, sterile, or emotionally distant; it must feel calm, warm, energised, thoughtful, comforting, decisive, curious. Stated once, as the house itself: a warm, lived-in home where someone has already thought about dinner.

A-300 Materials — the three grounds Typical detail

Three grounds, and only the middle one moves. A domain expresses its character entirely within its ground plane and the light that falls on it, and touches nothing above or below — "this is what makes rooms possible without forking the product."

Four material laws bind every room: the ground plane is the room's identity, one warm plane per workspace, never nested. Solidity follows importance — support is anchored, never naked ink on the environment; dense data never sits on landscape. Air is a material — generous breathing space is the resting state of every room. The hand answers physically, identically everywhere — hover lifts into the light, press seats; rooms never invent their own physics.

A-400 The Companion — a fixture, not a room Fixture schedule

On an architect's plan, the Companion is not a space. It is a fixture symbol — the same mark, in the same position, in every room on sheet A-100. That is not a drafting shortcut; it is the canon's definition, drawn.

"The Companion is not a room. It is the person in the house — the knowledgeable friend at the kitchen counter: always in the same place, comfortable in every room, never following the household around the house talking. One presence, one fixed chair … It is in the rooms, never a room; its panel is foreground and carries the house's warmth, not a view of its own. In every room, the Companion joins after the person has arrived — manners rendered as motion. No pulsing, no typing theatrics, no simulated mood, no face."

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md § 13:323–335

Its place on the plan

Foreground layer (A-300). Never given a room, never given a window, never given an exposure level. It has no address because it is already everywhere.

The house-level observation

The Companion is the one inhabitant of the space sheet A-101 says has no name — the only entity whose place is described by its relationship to all the rooms at once, and described entirely without the vocabulary the house lacks.

HOUSE4 § 8.1

Silence is a fixture too

"Silence is a first-class outcome." Nothing to say → say nothing. The chair is empty far more often than it is occupied, and that is the design working.

Sheet note 007 · One fixed chair, four different pieces of furniture in it

Drawn as a single symbol on A-100 because that is the law. The as-built condition differs per room, and no room can see it: Shopping is in the right chair ("Worth a look before you shop" — the only title in the house with a deadline built into its grammar). That is a sequencing decision across four rooms, and no room can make it.

Sheet note 008 · Two signs of life have no data behind them

The schedule is the canon's, complete. But two of its ten "sign of life" entries are sourced from facts the platform does not hold: the Cookbook's well-thumbed page comes from "cook/plan counts" — and there is no cook count in ninety tables. The Pantry's freshness, honestly told comes from "item freshness data" — and no expiry, purchase or opened date exists anywhere in the pantry. Both were found by separate investigations asking the same question of different rooms, neither looking for it. A schedule that specifies a finish nobody can supply is the drawing's problem, not the builder's.

What is owned — and it is a lot

"Journeys, not screens, are the unit of experience design." Every journey starts from Home and ends somewhere restful. Home is always reachable — "nobody is ever more than one step from the centre." The mental model is fixed: Home → realm → thing, not a browser trail.

Those three rules mandate Home→realm and realm→Home. Realm→realm is never addressed by any rule in any governing document — while the platform's strongest seams are exactly realm→realm.

"Shopping is not the market. It is the threshold before it." The canon mentions money four times and every one is a prohibition.

SHOP2 § 0.3, § 5.1
Sheet note 010 · The one asymmetry — and it is drawn on A-100

Two of the three doors are boundaries the canon chose. The third is one the code walked through. The house's metaphor says "Shopping is preparing to leave the house" — the shop is off-stage, and there is no chapter in the Living Book in which the household is at the market. Meanwhile the platform runs nine retailers, real prices, and a live checkout inside that room.

"A Capability Card governs what the Companion may say about retailers. No document governs what it should feel like to be a household spending money inside a house whose entire emotional constitution was written about calm, care, and having nothing sold to you."

SHOP2 § 5.2 · restated by HOUSE4 § 12.2

It is not a defect in any document. It is a question no one has been asked — and it sits exactly where the house's metaphor runs out. This board draws the door and does not open it.

A-900 General notes — what must never be drawn Binding on every sheet
  1. The analogies are feelings to design toward, never pictures to draw. The "family table" is produced by material, light, and composition — the moment it becomes a picture of a table, the place has become a costume. Every sheet in this set is a diagram for this reason.
  2. The theme park. Illustrated kitchens, drawn furniture, wood-grain, page-turns, fridge magnets, jar clip-art, photo-corner frames. The single most-forbidden thing in the entire document set — and the failure mode of the render this board replaces.
  3. The costume. A realm growing its own palette, component styling, decorative border, or motif to "feel like itself." A room is a use of the canonical owners, never a fork of them.
  4. Wallpaper. No room contains the orchard; every room is oriented toward it. A backdrop applied uniformly behind everything is everywhere at once is nowhere in particular.
  5. All view, no room. A working surface that is mostly environment — a search box floating on a landscape. It reads as nobody home.
  6. The second sun. Any lighting that breaks the one morning. Quieter never means darker; every room in this house is a morning room.
  7. Metaphor taxing function. Any place-character that costs a click, a legibility point, or a frame of scroll performance. If the concept ever fights the Planner, the concept loses. — This note binds this board too.
  8. Calm must never become lifeless. The quiet counterfeit: stillness, hush and desaturation posing as serenity. This set is drafted, not drained.
The star this set is steered by

"The Healthy Apples is a modern home in an ancient orchard, where technology quietly supports timeless family life."

THA_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md § 17 — THE DESIGN NORTH STAR (governing)

That sentence is the North Star, and this board is not. The mission asked for "the definitive visual North Star"; the star already exists, it is governing, and it is a sentence rather than a picture — which is precisely why it has survived. A drawing that claimed to supersede it would be an investigation overruling a governing document, which is not a thing an investigation may do. This set is a drawing of the house that sentence describes.

The test any visit must pass

"A household should end every visit having thought about food a little less, trusted it a little more, and noticed the product not at all. … When two designs both pass every gate, choose the one the household would feel and could not name. When restraint and expression tie, choose restraint. When the product and the household tie, there is no tie."

The one thing to remember

The Orchard House is a modern home in an ancient orchard, on one bright morning that never moves. Ten rooms, one presence, one sun. Every room's window is sized by how hard it makes the eye work, every room's character is already written down, and the orchard outside every window is life — never decoration, never walked into, never asked to be tended.

And at the centre of the plan there is a room-shaped hole that no document owns, that four different words could name and none of them may, that the exposure scale borrows its floor from, that the Admin study takes its only light from, and that the household walks through every single time they move between the three rooms they use most.

The house is completely specified. The space between its rooms is not drawn here either — because it is not this board's to invent. An honest drawing shows the void and stamps it.
